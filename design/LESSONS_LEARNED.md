2026-05-05 | Mining 3×3×3 carve collapsed to 1×1×1 on some voxels | `_terrain.to_local()` does a float matrix transform; with `transform.scale = 0.166667` (1/6 m), world `-0.166667` round-trips to `-0.999999...` rather than `-1.0`. `Vector3i` truncation then loses one voxel per edge, collapsing the 3×3×3 to 1×1×1. 2026-05-05 | VoxelMesherCubes wrote vertex colours but the StandardMaterial3D rendered terrain uniform brown | Default `vertex_color_use_as_albedo = false` on the `StandardMaterial3D` makes it ignore the per-vertex colours the mesher emits. 2026-05-05 | Setting `vertex_color_use_as_albedo = true` made terrain render nearly black | NOT byte order. `VoxelBuffer.CHANNEL_COLOR` defaults to **8-bit depth** (one byte per voxel), so the 32-bit packed RGBA values the generator writes get truncated to just the R byte — the alpha-byte mat_id alone, ≈0.012-0.016 on screen. Mesher byte order matches Godot's `Color.to_rgba32()` (R high, A low) — no encoding rewrite needed. Fix: instantiate a `VoxelFormat`, call `set_channel_depth(CHANNEL_COLOR, DEPTH_32_BIT)`, assign to `terrain.format` BEFORE handing the terrain to systems that read it. The `format` property is null by default and lives on `VoxelLodTerrain` itself. | #135
2026-05-05 | Calling `out_buffer.set_channel_depth()` inside `_generate_block` made terrain disappear entirely | Confirmed twice in this codebase: the per-block call invalidates the buffer's channel storage even when made on a fresh buffer before any writes. 2026-05-05 | Spent hours rewriting voxel encode + decode across 7 files on a wrong byte-order hypothesis | The actual bug was upstream of byte order (8-bit storage truncating 32-bit values, see #135 above). 2026-05-05 | `WaterChunkMesher.set_player_chunk` was iterating `(2*radius+1)^3` chunks per chunk transition | At `MESH_RENDER_RADIUS_M = 64m` and `CHUNK_SIZE_M ≈ 2.67`, that's `49^3 = 117,649` iterations every ~2.7 m of walking. Each iteration does dictionary lookups + AABB tests against every source region. Cost: ~50-150 ms per chunk transition, exactly matching the long-standing "walking stutters but camera rotation is fine" hitch. Fix: water surfaces are 2D planes at fixed Y values. Iterate only the 2D `(dx, dz)` ring at each unique source-region surface Y. With 2 source regions = 49 × 49 × 2 ≈ 4,800 iterations. ~24× faster, fits under 1 ms. General principle: when iterating chunks for an effect that lives on a known plane, never sweep the perpendicular axis. | #135
