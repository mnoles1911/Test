# Mira-Thal Lore Bible — Complete Reference

> Single-file combined edition. For individual topic files see `lore/README.md`.

## Overview

Mira-Thal is a world of two continents in the third age of its existence. The western continent **Mira** is the heartland of civilization: four human kingdoms clustered at its center, the ancient Aelorin forests to the north, three dwarven mountain-kingdoms threading the Spine of the World, and the wild eastern frontier where the ash-lands begin. The eastern continent **Thal** is wilderness — unmapped beyond its fringes, dominated by the Ash Throne's influence, and anchored at its heart by the volcano **Drûn-Khazad**.

The age is paradox: men are more numerous than ever, cities grow, trade flows — but the world's deeper fabric frays. The Aelorin dwindle. Dwarven kings grow old or greedy. Something bound two thousand years ago beneath the volcano is learning, slowly, to breathe again.

## Geography

### Mira — The Heartland Continent

**The Central Plains**
Flat, fertile, heavily farmed. The four human kingdoms occupy this region, borders contested by rivers and old battle-lines. The most densely populated area in the world.

**The Spine of the World**
A vast mountain range running north-to-south through Mira's eastern third. Home to the three dwarven kingdoms. Impassable at its heights except through the **Underway** — the dwarven tunnel network threading the mountain's heart.

**The Greatwood**
Northern Mira. A vast ancient forest where the Aelorin have lived since the First Age. The wood grows darker and older the further north one travels. The Eight Sacred Glades are scattered through it. The silverwood trees in the heart of Lirien-Thal are not trees — they are ancestors.

**The Western Coast**
Dramatic sea-cliffs, rainy moors, fishing villages. Caer Brannoch territory. Site of **Caer Drowned** — the sunken cliff-city formerly known as Caer Sulwyn.

**The Ashfields**
Eastern Mira, beyond the Spine's eastern slopes. Grey dead ground, thin soil over ancient lava beds, permanent ash-haze drifting from Drûn-Khazad. Frontier territory: Vosskaran garrison towns and goblin raids.

**The Sorrowmarsh**
A dead wetland in eastern Mira where the Grand Alliance broke Mordvar's first host two thousand years ago. Nothing grows. Ghost-lights at night. The mud smells of old blood in warm weather.

**The Weeping Wood**
A section of forest north of the goblin lands, separate from the Aelorin Greatwood. Leafless year-round, permanent dark cloud cover overhead. Voices speak in the trees — not echoes but articulate words, the voices of past victims. Those who venture in come out as other people, if they come out at all.

### Thal — The Wild Continent

**The Ash-Steppe**
Western Thal — grassland transitioning to grey hardpan. Where the Gulmrak Horde ranges. Sparse water, brutal winters, occasional ruins of pre-First Age settlements that no scholar has been able to identify.

**The Vrothmor Peaks**
Central Thal — jagged volcanic mountains riddled with goblin warrens. The Vrothmor Peaks are older than any recorded history and contain tunnel systems that predate even the dwarven Underway.

**Drûn-Khazad — The Burning Wound**
The great volcano at Thal's heart. Called the "World's Mouth" by orcs, who consider it sacred ground. Active — permanent lava glow at night, ash drifting west on bad years. The caldera contains the **Hollow Hearth**, where the Aescstól (Ash Throne) was hidden after Mordvar's binding. The Hollow Hearth is below the caldera; the **Vault of Aen-Vael** is below the Hollow Hearth.

**Mor-Vethrin**
The obsidian city of the Naergrim dark elves, built into the eastern cliff-faces of the Vrothmor Peaks. Black stone, no windows facing west, entrance through a single gate guarded by a carved arch of bone.

**The Shroud Sea**
The eastern ocean. Locked in permanent storm since the night Caer Sulwyn slid into the sea. No ship that sails east returns. The Sailors' Brotherhood has charted the storm patterns for two hundred years and believes a navigable window opens every forty years. The next window opens nine years from the start of Game One.

### Key Islands and Archipelagos

**The Copper Isles**
Off Mira's southern coast. Nominally Solgrade territory. Major trading hub, politically unstable, home to the Sailors' Brotherhood's main archive and the primary records of the Shroud Sea charts.

**The Pilgrim's Chain**
Three small islands off Mira's western coast, each containing one of the Eight Sacred Glades. The most protected pilgrimage route in the Aelorin Circuit. No hostile force has successfully landed on the Pilgrim's Chain in recorded history.

## The Three Ages

### The First Age — The Age of Dragons

Titan-beasts ruled sky and stone. Most records lost. The dwarves remember it through the **Dragon-Watchers**, who maintain a continuous oral and written record from this period. The Aelorin have Loresingers who can recite the First Age's end. Humans have only fragments and myths.

Key event: **The Dragonfall** — the death of the last titan-dragon, cause unknown across all three peoples' records, that ended the age and allowed the Elder Folk to emerge.

### The Second Age — The Age of the Elder Folk

Aelorin and dwarves built great civilizations whose ruins scar every horizon. The human kingdom of the **Aescryd** (the First Kingdom) rose late in this age under a man then called **Bealoric the High King** — later known as Mordvar.

His fall: Bealoric, consumed by fear of death as his kingdom weakened, performed a ritual that emptied him of his mortality — and of everything else. The **Grand Alliance** of Aelorin, dwarves, and uncorrupted humans forged the **Sundered Crown** of seven metals and used it to bind him. The crown was shattered and its seven pieces hidden across the world. Mordvar was sealed in the Hollow Hearth within Drûn-Khazad.

The Aescryd sank into the sea within a generation of Mordvar's binding. Its ruins lie somewhere beneath the Shroud Sea.

### The Third Age — The Age of Men / The Long Twilight

Current era. Men are the fastest-breeding and most numerous people in the world. New cities rise faster than old ones fall. Trade routes span both continents. But the Aelorin dwindle through the Aelthiren, the dwarves grow inward and suspicious, and the Long Twilight is both the age of humanity's greatest expansion and its most existential threat.

The word *twilight* is used by Aelorin loremasters — not human scholars, who would name this a golden age. The Aelorin have seen ages end before.

## Religion

### Human Religion — The Ninefold Pantheon

Nine gods of the old world. Each kingdom emphasizes different aspects:

| God | Domain | Primary Kingdom |
|---|---|---|
| **Aldrath** | War and endurance | Eldermark |
| **Aeadis** | Harvest and sustenance | Eldermark |
| **Sorael** | Sea, journeys, honest reckoning | Caer Brannoch |
| **Lorthis** | Sky and sight | Solgrade |
| **Dunrath** | Beneath and secrets | Vosskara |
| **Aevelis** | Healing | All |
| **Lernath** | Learning | Solgrade |
| **Morith** | Death and transition | Vosskara |
| **The Hearth-Nameless** | Household, unnamed, always present | All |

The Ninefold are worshipped through shrines in homes, civic temples in cities, and the **Orders of the Shrine** — wandering priests who maintain the rural shrine circuit. No unified church exists. Religious authority is local.

### Aelorin Religion — The Eight Stars

Eight star-spirits sang the world into being and then withdrew. The Eighth Star fell when Mordvar first hollowed himself — drawn down by the disappearance of the note of completion it sang. The Aelorin believe the Eighth Star became the **Aeluvain**: a gem pale blue-white, cold to the touch, the size of a fist, that hums at a frequency only Aelorin can hear.

The **Eight Glades** are sacred sites — three on the Pilgrim's Chain islands, five in the Greatwood — where each star-spirit is said to have last walked. Pilgrimage between them is the central religious practice of the Aelorin. Aelorin priests are **loremasters**: they remember rather than petition.

The eighth glade exists and is tended in anticipation. It has no star. The Aelorin believe this will change.

### Dwarven Religion — The Forge and the Lost

The **Forge-Father** made the world's bones. The **Mountain-Mother** fills the bones with ore and life. The **Lost Brother** — a fourth god, equal to the other three — was slain by dragons in the First Age, which is the religious foundation of the dwarven vendetta against all dragonkind.

The **Night of the Listening** is a yearly ceremony: all dwarves in every hold observe one hour of total silence to remember what has been heard in the deep and to acknowledge that some things should not be heard.

The **Dragon-Watchers** order is simultaneously a military intelligence organization and a religious institution — tracking dragons is both tactical and devotional.

## Magic

### Nature

Magic exists and everyone knows it. It is rare — perhaps one person in a thousand has any innate ability, and of those, fewer than one in ten has enough to be practically useful. Those born with it often have unstable early lives: things happen around them before they understand why.

Magic is old. In the First and Second Ages, more beings could wield it. Most of those beings and their knowledge have faded. What survives is fragmentary, jealously guarded, and expensive to use.

### Cost

Every significant magical act has a personal cost borne by the user:
- Migraines lasting days
- Temporary sensory loss (blindness, deafness, numbness)
- Weeks of physical exhaustion
- Permanent alteration at high levels — hair whitens, eyes change color, the aging process accelerates

The Ashen Hand accepts costs the Conclave of the Unseen Hand would refuse. This is part of why they are more immediately powerful and ultimately more fragile.

### Types

**Innate magic** — Born with it. The Conclave trains this. Cannot be taught from scratch; can only be developed in those who already have it.

**Item magic** — Objects enchanted in the Second Age that retain properties. Increasingly rare. The Smiths' Confederation maintains the most complete catalog of known items. The Forgemasters' Society can sometimes create new ones.

**Ritual magic** — Slow, requires preparation, draws on place-memory or collective will. The Ashen Hand's plague-blight spreading is ritual magic. The Sundered Crown's binding was ritual magic at a scale not seen since.

**The Aeluvain** — Not magic in the conventional sense. It is a piece of the world's original song. It cannot be used; it can only be returned to where it belongs.

### Institutions

**The Conclave of the Unseen Hand (Eldermark)** — The only formal human magic school. Sixty-three members. Trains innate users. Does not teach magic to those who lack it. Two current members are Ashfallen (compromised but functional). See GUILDS_AND_FACTIONS.md.

**Aelorin Loremaster Tradition** — Millennia of accumulated magical knowledge, conservatively applied. Not a school — an inheritance.

**Dwarven Runework** — No innate magical tradition, but deep knowledge of Second Age enchanted items and stonework. The Forgemasters' Society preserves most of this.

## The Kingdoms of Men

Humans are the youngest major people but the most numerous. Four distinct kingdoms cluster in Mira's central plains. They share the Ninefold Pantheon, common ancestry, and recurring wars with each other.

---

### Eldermark — The Heartland Kingdom

**Capital:** Aldenholt — the largest walled city in the world. Stone walls three men thick, population of roughly eighty thousand, a market district that never fully closes.

**Ruler:** King Othric IV, the Iron-Tongued. Age 63. Aging warrior-king, politically astute, physically diminished. Three rival sons: the capable-but-cold eldest, the decent-but-unremarkable middle son, and Prince Aedric (youngest), whose Ashen Hand connections are the trilogy's first major political crisis.

**Culture:** Anglo-Saxon flavor. Thanes, longhalls, the witenagemot (council of lords). Heavy infantry tradition. Worships Aldrath (war/endurance) and Aeadis (harvest) most prominently. Eldermark's people are proud, stubborn, and deeply suspicious of Solgrade's commercial influence.

**Wealth:** Greatest of the four kingdoms. Controls the Central Plains' best farmland and the major east-west trade road.

**Internal crisis:** Othric's succession is openly contested. The eldest son has the military loyalty; the middle son has the popular support; Aedric has Ashen Hand backing and a genuine (if catastrophically misguided) plan. This crisis is woven through all three games.

---

### Caer Brannoch — The Western Coastal Kingdom

**Capital:** Caer Brannoch — a cliff-fortress city carved into the western headland. The lower city sits at sea level; the upper city is accessible only by a single switchback road or the sea-lifts.

**Ruler:** Queen Eilwen the Grey. Widowed. The only female ruler among men. A former naval officer who came to the throne through a succession crisis of her own and has spent fifteen years proving she belongs there. Her court knows not to test this.

**Culture:** Welsh/Celtic flavor. Bards, druidic remnants at the rural level, bronze torcs as noble insignia, rain-soaked moors inland. Master sailors and shipwrights — Caer Brannoch builds the best ships in the world and controls the western trade lanes.

**The Shroud:** Eilwen's court-scholars have been studying the Shroud Sea's permanent storms for two generations. The Sailors' Brotherhood's Shroud charts originate here. Eilwen believes a navigable window exists and opens every forty years. The next opens nine years from Game One's start.

**Tension:** Border disputes with Eldermark over fishing rights date back four generations. An old coastal massacre that neither kingdom officially acknowledges has never been resolved.

---

### The Vosskaran Despotate — The Frontier Kingdom

**Capital:** Vosskar-on-the-Iron — a fortress-city built into a river bend on the eastern frontier. Iron-banded walls, garrison culture, no wasted space.

**Ruler:** Despot Yaromir the Cold. Survived three assassination attempts. Governs through paranoia and competence in roughly equal measure. Slavic/eastern European flavor: fur cloaks, icon-worship of the Ninefold, brutal winters, a warrior culture that has been at war its entire history.

**Culture:** The shield of Mira. Vosskara's borders touch goblin lands. Its people are the hardest human soldiers alive because they have been fighting since they were old enough to hold weapons. They demand tribute from Eldermark for "holding the line," which is militarily justified and politically toxic.

**The Tribute War:** The ongoing dispute over what Vosskara is owed for frontier defense is the source of the most active human-on-human violence in Game One's world. Yaromir is not unreasonable — he is right that without Vosskara, Eldermark's eastern border would have collapsed decades ago. He is also inflexible about how he communicates this.

---

### The Free City of Solgrade — The Merchant Republic

**Capital:** Solgrade itself — a southern port city of terracotta roofs, banking houses, open markets, and hired swords. No king. Ruled by the **Council of Twelve Houses**, wealthy merchant families who have collectively decided that democracy is the most profitable form of government.

**Culture:** Italian/Mediterranean flavor. Banking, trade, contract law, mercenary armies. Controls the southern straits and the spice routes connecting Mira's south to the Copper Isles and beyond. Officially neutral in all conflicts. Secretly funds multiple sides of every conflict, because stability is expensive and instability is expensive differently.

**House Korvath:** The wealthiest of the Twelve Houses. They know the gold piece of the Sundered Crown is a piece of the Sundered Crown. They have been waiting for someone to come asking, at which point they intend to negotiate from the strongest possible position.

## The Aelorin — The Wood Elves

Single united people. Nearly immortal. Perhaps forty thousand total, declining. They inhabit the Greatwood and northern coastal forests. Ruled by **Lord Aelthurion the Sorrowful** and his consort **Lady Nimriel of the Singing Vale**.

### Three Cities

**Lirien-Thal** — "City of Silver Leaves." Capital. Built into the canopy of millennia-old silverwood trees. The trees are ancestral Aelorin who completed the Aelthiren — their hair became leaves, their bodies bark, over decades of transformation. Walking through Lirien-Thal is walking through a forest of ancestors.

**Aelorien** — "Vale of First Song." Religious center. Where the Aelorin first awoke under the stars. The Eight Glades nearest here are the most frequently visited. The Hall of the Vigil — where Aelorin who have begun the Aelthiren are tended before transformation completes — is here.

**Sirathiel-by-the-Sea** — "Where the Sea Sings Back." Coastal city of pale stone and mother-of-pearl inlay. The Aelorin here have the closest relationship with human traders. Sirathiel is the only Aelorin city where humans are regularly admitted without invitation.

### The Aelthiren

Aelorin who succumb to *aelnaeth* — the slow forgetting of why one ever cared, which comes to immortal beings as age comes to mortals — may choose the Aelthiren. They walk to one of the Eight Glades, lie at the roots of a silverwood tree, and slowly merge with the forest. Over decades: hair becomes leaves, body becomes bark, consciousness diffuses into the wood's vast slow dreaming.

The silverwoods are ancestors. The Aelorin tend them, speak to them, sing to them. Elves who approach a glade but cannot complete the Aelthiren surrender their name at the boundary (by custom) and live as **the Nameless** — reborn into new identities, operating in the world's grey spaces.

### Aelorin Culture

The Aelorin have a centuries-long outlook. They find human urgency alternately endearing and exhausting. Their fear is not death but *aelnaeth* — the slow loss of meaning across an immortal span. The word for this is one of the most dreaded in their language.

Pilgrimage between the Eight Glades is the central religious practice. Aelorin priests are loremasters who carry accumulated knowledge across centuries. Their political decisions can seem incomprehensibly slow to humans — an Aelorin lord considering a treaty may take years to decide, because years are a fraction of a percent of their expected lifespan.

Lord Aelthurion has been watching Mordvar's gathering strength for forty years through the Pilgrimage Circuit's intelligence network. He was not surprised by anything that happened in the trilogy. He knew it was coming. His grief is not for the world's danger but for the fact that he watched it approach for so long without being able to make humans and dwarves see it.

## The Three Dwarven Kingdoms

All three kings share descent from **King Brokkin Stonefather**, the dwarven founder-hero. Their realms are linked by the **Underway** tunnel network. The three kingdoms are close allies who come to each other's aid readily — more reliably than human kingdoms.

Dwarves live longer than humans (lifespans of three to four hundred years) but are shorter-lived than Aelorin. They are physically stocky, have excellent underground navigation, and build structures that outlast civilizations. Above ground they are adaptable but always faintly aware of the absence of stone overhead.

### Karaz-Dûn — The High Kingdom

**Location:** Northern Spine of the World. **Ruler:** King Thrarin Ironhand. The greatest hold — forges that have not gone cold in four thousand years. Master smiths whose work is recognized across both continents.

**Current crisis:** Thrarin has not left his treasury in two years. His gold-hunger has been artificially accelerated by **Drossvik**, the Ashen Hand mole in Bromrin's court who extended his operation to Karaz-Dûn through a compromised steward. A **Gold-Speaker auditor** documented the acceleration and had his report buried. He is now in protective custody in Kazaad-Brak, waiting for someone to ask him what he saw.

### Khorumzad — The Delving Kingdom

**Location:** Middle Spine. **Ruler:** King Bromrin Deepdelver. His realm tunnels deepest of all — nine layers from the Underdark. Pious, beloved, known for his personal humility despite commanding the most extensive underground infrastructure in the world.

**Current crisis:** Bromrin has been slowly poisoned into gold-hunger by Drossvik, his senior advisor and the Ashen Hand's primary inside agent in the dwarven kingdoms. His diggers, following the deepening gold-hunger's obsessive drive, broke through to the Hollow Hearth chamber below Khorumzad and then to the **Vault of Aen-Vael** below that. Since the vault seal was disturbed, the gold-hunger has spread from Bromrin outward through the ambient magic contaminating the hold's atmosphere.

### Kazaad-Brak — The Warden Kingdom

**Location:** Southern Spine. **Ruler:** King Hagrim Bloodbeard. The southernmost hold, closest to goblin lands. Besieged eleven times in Hagrim's lifetime. Aging, no confirmed heir, throne contested by two cousin-clans who have been more or less openly hostile to each other for a decade.

**Hagrim's strength:** The most militarily experienced dwarven king. His soldiers are the best in the Underway because they have been in continuous conflict their entire careers. When the call comes for the final battle, Hagrim does not wait for a council vote.

## The Shadow — Mordvar and the Ash Throne

### Mordvar — The Hollow King

**Origin:** Born Bealoric, High King of the Aescryd (the First Human Kingdom) in the Second Age. A capable, beloved ruler who watched his kingdom slow and crumble and became consumed by a single fear: death. He sought immortality through a ritual that required emptying himself — surrendering the core of his selfhood, the thing that makes beings mortal, in exchange for a form that contains nothing and therefore cannot die.

He got what he wanted. He became hollow. His kingdom became a death-engine. The Grand Alliance — Aelorin, dwarves, and the remaining uncorrupted humans — forged the **Sundered Crown** and used it to bind him. He was sealed in the **Hollow Hearth** within Drûn-Khazad.

Two thousand years later, the binding weakens. Mordvar cannot yet act directly. He acts through the Aescstól (Ash Throne), through the Ashlord, through the Ashen Hand and the Hosts.

**Physical presence:** Mordvar emerges from Drûn-Khazad's caldera on the night of the final battle's third day. He is tall and grey-pale, dressed in something that was once clothing and is now indistinguishable from his skin. He does not roar or speak or raise his arms. He simply begins walking down the slope. His presence is a cold of *will*, not temperature. Men who were fighting moments before find their arms heavy.

**His tragedy:** He was a man. He was afraid. What he did came from ordinary human fear of death — the same fear that wakes people at three in the morning. Understanding this does not make him less dangerous. It does make the question of how to end him more complicated.

### The Aescstól — The Ash Throne

An obsidian throne streaked with silver, forged from cooled lava and the bone-meal of Mordvar's first sacrifices, in the Hollow Hearth within Drûn-Khazad. Mordvar's will flows through it into whoever sits upon it.

The current occupant is the **Ashlord** — faceless even to his own forces, Mordvar's will made partially present. His identity is unknown to all but the closest Ashen Hand inner circle. He may be a corrupted human king; he may be a dark elf; he may be something that was a person once and is no longer.

**Symbol:** A black sun with seven rays. One for each Host. The seventh ray is vacant — reserved for Mordvar.

### The Hosts of the Ash Throne

**The Gulmrak Horde** — Sixty thousand orcs. Currently led by **Warlord Uglakh the Iron-Crowned**, who killed his predecessor (Drûn-Aghul) after the chaos following Game One's climax. Religious devotion to the volcano — they consider it their god's mouth and fight its defense with the fury of the righteous. They dislike light, prefer raw meat, and hate the smell of cooked food above all things. Their war-drums sound like the earth thinking.

**The Goblin Warrens of Vrothmor** — Eighty thousand goblins. Current king: **Kogul the Eight-Fingered**, eleven years in power — unprecedented stability for goblin politics, which normally sees kings killed within a year. Bound to the Ash Throne by terror. Individually weak, collectively a flood. They swarm, infiltrate, vanish underground and reappear unexpectedly. The Alliance commanders are more afraid of the goblins than the orcs.

**The Black Court / Naergrim** — Dark elves who chose blood-magic immortality when their Aelorin kin chose the stars. Five hundred elite fighters under **Serethi the Twice-Dead**, who survived his own partial Aelthiren attempt and returned wrong. Built into Mor-Vethrin, the obsidian city in the Vrothmor Peaks. Bound to the Ash Throne by will — they chose this freely, unlike the orc and goblin forces. A minority faction within the Naergrim questions this choice but has not acted on the question.

**The Ashen Hand** — Turned mages. Hierarch: **Vaeroth the Pale**, former Solgrade court mage who sold his allegiance to the Ash Throne a decade before the trilogy begins. Bound by gift — the Ash Throne amplifies their power beyond what they could wield otherwise. They spread plague-blights, summon forbidden things, and serve as Mordvar's most intelligent agents. Two hundred mages at peak strength.

**The Ashfallen** — Humans, dwarves, and elves who were captured and returned wrong — hollowed just enough to serve. Three thousand distributed through the Ash Throne's forces. The hardest to fight because they wear familiar faces. A soldier freezes for a second recognizing someone; a second is enough.

## The Great Wounds

Sites where the world was broken and has not healed. These are not myths — they are current geography.

### The Sorrowmarsh

Eastern Mira, where the Grand Alliance broke Mordvar's first host two thousand years ago. The battle's dark magic was concentrated here when Mordvar's forces were finally routed. Nothing has grown since. The wetland exists but produces only grey sedge and fen-moss that smells wrong. Ghost-lights appear at night — small, cold, blue-white, moving with apparent purpose. The lights do not respond to approach. No one has successfully followed one.

### Caer Drowned — Formerly Caer Sulwyn

The jewel of Caer Brannoch's coast for three centuries. One night — the same night Mordvar is believed to have made his first significant move toward breaking his binding — the coastal cliffs sheared into the sea. The upper city slid in sections. Parts of the towers still stand at wrong angles under grey water, visible at low tide. The harbor's shallows are navigable; the deep sections are too dangerous to dive.

The seas changed the same night. The Shroud Sea's permanent storms began within the week. Weather patterns along the entire western coast shifted and have not shifted back.

### The Weeping Wood

A section of ancient forest north of the goblin lands that was the site of a major Second Age battle involving dark magic of a kind the records do not clearly describe. The wood died. Trees stand but do not leaf, do not rot, do not fall — suspended in a moment of dying that never completes.

Voices speak in the trees. Not wind through branches — articulate voices, sometimes recognizable as specific people who entered and did not return. The voices repeat last words, fragments of conversations, names. Extended exposure causes dissociation and eventually permanent personality change. The **Nameless Council** maintains one operative who can enter and return; they have never explained how.

### Drûn-Khazad — The Burning Wound

The great volcano, Thal's heart. Active and worsening as Mordvar's binding weakens. Lava flows that were stable for centuries have shifted. The ash zone extends further west each decade. Orc theology holds that the volcano is their god's mouth and its increasing activity is their god's growing appetite — good news, to them.

### The Shroud Sea

Not a wound in the physical sense but a consequence of wounds. The eastern ocean locked in permanent storm since Caer Sulwyn's fall. Waves that come from nowhere, storm systems that move against the prevailing wind, weather that kills ships built for open ocean. The Sailors' Brotherhood believes the storms are a seal — that something was put on the other side and the storms are the lock. The Brotherhood's most senior members are divided between those who want to know what it is and those who believe not knowing is the correct response.

One major chivalric order per kingdom. Each has its own founding myth, internal politics, and connection to the trilogy's plot.

---

## The Iron Chalice — Eldermark

**Founded** three centuries ago after the **Red Siege of Aldenholt**, when a last company of soldiers held the city gates for nine days on nothing but well water and the belief that someone was coming. The Order was named for the cup of well water the last surviving defender was still holding when relief arrived.

**Composition:** Twelve hundred knights at full strength. Heavy cavalry trained to fight as dismounted infantry once their horses are gone — their founding doctrine assumes the cavalry has already failed.

**Religion:** Devoted to **Aldrath**, but specifically the aspect of *endurance* rather than *victory*. The Order does not celebrate wins. It commemorates the times it held.

**Current Grandmaster:** **Dame Calla Vane** (no relation to Aldric Vane). Knows the Order is financially insolvent — a debt to Solgrade's banking houses comes due in two years. She has told no one. She is quietly looking for a way out that does not involve selling the Order's independence.

**Plot connection:** The Order's chapel displays an iron pommel on its founding-relic altar. Dame Calla, like every grandmaster before her, does not know it is the **iron piece of the Sundered Crown**. **Roland Ashford** discovered this three years before Game One and was expelled for refusing to stay silent. Game One opens with him hunted for that knowledge.

**Endgame:** If Roland resolves the Order's debt during the Solgrade arc, the Iron Chalice arrives at the Battle of Drûn-Khazad as committed allies rather than reluctant conscripts. Of twelve hundred knights, one hundred and twelve survive the final battle — they spend themselves escorting Aldric to the summit.

---

## The Tidewarden Order — Caer Brannoch

**Founded** before Caer Brannoch was a kingdom — when the western coast was still a collection of cliff-villages whose sailors had grown sick of taking pirate bribes. The founding charter contains the line *"We will not be bought. We have tried it and it makes us sick."* This is not metaphorical.

**Composition:** Roughly nine hundred knights. Every Tidewarden must navigate, swim a mile in modified armor, and sail a single-mast vessel alone before being accepted. They fight on water as readily as land.

**Style:** Their land combat is adapted from shipboard fighting — close, economical, built for confined spaces and sudden footing changes.

**Religion:** Devoted to **Sorael**, who in Caer Brannoch theology is god of sea, journeys, and honest reckoning.

**Internal faction — the Deepwater Voices:** A minority within the Order who believe the **Shroud Sea's** permanent storms are not natural but a deliberate seal placed over something that needed to stay contained. The Order's leadership officially discourages this question because they find it *too interesting*.

**Plot connection:** Provides naval support for the Khorumzad relief fleet (Game Two). Plays a critical role in Game Three: Eilwen's fleet at the final muster on the Copper Isles is built around Tidewarden command.

---

## The Frost Brotherhood — Vosskara

**Founded** as a ranger compact, not a chivalric order. Began as men and women who tracked goblin raiding parties into the winter steppe and dealt with them before they reached settlements. Has retained the practical, unornamented culture of its origin.

**Composition:** Roughly four hundred members. **Initiation:** survive three days alone in the northern steppe in midwinter with one knife and no fire. **Failure rate:** approximately forty percent. The Brotherhood considers this acceptable — the ones who come back are reliably the ones who should be in the Brotherhood.

**Style:** No ceremonial armor. No tournaments. Every member is a tracker, interrogator, and wilderness surgeon as well as a fighter.

**Function:** Vosskara's intelligence apparatus as much as its military arm. They hold the eastern frontier with fewer numbers than any comparable force because they know the ground, know the enemy, and do not waste effort on anything except the work.

**Classified history:** The Brotherhood ran a covert information exchange with a Gulmrak orc warlord (predecessor to Drûn-Aghul) for eleven years. The exchange ended with that warlord's death in Game One. Three current captains know what was exchanged. They have agreed never to tell Despot Yaromir.

**Plot connection:** Provides intelligence on Ashen Hand frontier movements throughout Games One and Two. In Game Three, the Brotherhood is critical to **Aldric Vane's** protection in Coldstoke — they slow Vaeroth's pursuit and cover the escape route.

## The Golden Lance — Solgrade

**Officially:** Solgrade's chivalric order, founded to protect merchant caravans and the Council of Twelve Houses' interests.

**In practice:** Everyone in the other three kingdoms knows the Golden Lance is a political instrument. Members are drawn from second sons of wealthy families, trained to fight, and deployed primarily as diplomatic escorts, trade negotiators, and quiet problem-solvers.

**Combat style:** Precise and economical — drawn-out violence is bad for commerce. Sword and a small fast-draw crossbow that can be drawn and fired in under four seconds. Considered dishonorably practical by every other Order until the fourth time a Golden Lance member killed someone who had already disarmed them.

**Track record:** Has never fought a major pitched battle. Has been present at twelve significant assassinations, six contested inheritance settlements, and three changes of government in small city-states along the trade routes. Officially denies all of this.

**Current Grand Commander:** **Ser Aldric Vossant** (no relation to Aldric Vane — *Aldric* is a common Eldermark name). Has developed inconvenient genuine convictions about justice that conflict with the Council. Currently refusing contracts he believes cross a line. The Council is deciding whether to remove him or simply route problematic work elsewhere.

**Plot connection:** In Game One, Vossant offers Roland his help in exchange for evidence of a House Korvath weapons-smuggling operation he has been trying to prove for two years. Following this thread is an [OPTIONAL QUEST] that earns Golden Lance commitment to the final battle and forces a Council reckoning with House practices.

## Trade Guilds

### The Smiths' Confederation

**Oldest trade guild** in the human kingdoms. Only guild with formal affiliate chapters among **both** Aelorin and dwarves.

**Founder:** **Durak Ironwhisper**, a dwarven master-smith from Karaz-Dûn who retired four hundred years ago, lived among humans for sixty years teaching them to work iron properly because watching them do it badly was making him physically unhappy.

**Structure:** Six tiers of membership, from apprentice to **Grand Artificer**. Three living Grand Artificers — one human, one Aelorin, one dwarf. They meet every decade to argue about standards and refuse to agree on anything except that the previous generation's standards were insufficient.

**Politics:** Controls access to the highest-quality ore in the world. Every human king has at some point tried to bring the Confederation under crown control. None succeeded — the Confederation simply stops servicing that kingdom until the kingdom reconsiders. This has worked eleven times in three hundred years.

**The Ashsteel Secret:**
- **Ashsteel** is a metal that holds a better edge than anything currently produced and cannot be detected by Ash Throne magics
- The formula is in the Confederation's sealed archive
- Locked away sixty years ago after a member sold a batch to an Ashen Hand procurement agent disguised as a goblin buyer
- Reopening requires unanimous Grand Artificer vote
- Currently deadlocked: one Artificer (the nephew of the man who sold the original batch) votes no out of shame

**[OPTIONAL QUEST across all three games]:** Roland uncovers what happened, exposes the original betrayal publicly, and earns the trust of all three Grand Artificers separately. If completed by Game Three, Ashsteel weapons are distributed to key fighters before the final battle. They do not win the battle — but the people carrying them are alive at the end.

---

### The Sailors' Brotherhood

Based in Caer Brannoch. Chapters in every port city on both coasts.

**Origin:** A mutual aid society — sailors pooling earnings into a fund that paid out to families of those who drowned. Evolved into the closest thing the world has to an international institution.

**Member benefits:**
- Reduced dock fees in member ports
- Brotherhood safe-houses in foreign cities
- Access to the network of information that flows through every sailor who has ever crossed between coasts

**Absolute principle:** Information flows freely between members. Brotherhood members do not report to crowns, do not carry messages that must be hidden from other sailors, and do not take contracts requiring them to sink another member's ship.

**Track record:** Thirty-one members expelled in Brotherhood history. Twenty-nine for breaking the no-crown-service rule. The other two for reasons the Brotherhood will not discuss.

**The Shroud Sea Charts:**
- The Brotherhood has charted Shroud Sea storm patterns for two hundred years
- Believes a navigable window opens every forty years
- The next window opens **nine years** from Game One's start (revised from earlier estimates of eleven)
- Brotherhood leadership is divided on what to do with this knowledge

**[SIDE QUEST] The Brotherhood's Copper Isles Archive:** Access requires becoming a Brotherhood-endorsed sailor, which requires completing one voyage with a Brotherhood crew. Payoff: the Shroud window information and a Brotherhood contact who can move people and intelligence across faction lines throughout the trilogy.

### The Apothecaries and Surgeons' School (Solgrade)

**Not technically a guild** — closest thing the world has to a university.

**Founded** by a physician who survived the last great plague outbreak and spent thirty years writing down everything she learned, then started teaching others.

**Officially neutral.** Admits students from all races and factions. Has:
- A dwarven research wing (secretly funded by Thrarin) studying gold-hunger physiology
- An Aelorin loremaster occasional visiting program (the Aelorin find human medicine *crude but occasionally illuminating*)
- A six-year-running ethics committee on whether their battlefield wound research incentivizes the continuation of conflict (240 pages of inconclusive findings to date)

**Plot connection:** Game Two — Corvus Tane's environmental magic combined with the School's gold-hunger research provides the treatment that can [SIDE QUEST] save Bromrin's mind during the Khorumzad descent. Game Three — School-trained surgeons run the Alliance field hospitals during the final battle.

## Scholarly Orders

### The Conclave of the Unseen Hand (Eldermark)

**The only formal magic school** in the human kingdoms — though "school" implies more structure than exists.

**Description:** A loose collection of mages who have grudgingly agreed to share knowledge with each other. The agreement is maintained partly by the fact that mages who do not join tend to be found dead. The Conclave officially has nothing to do with this. Everyone understands the implication.

**Membership:** Sixty-three current members. Admission requires demonstrable **innate** magical ability. The Conclave does not teach magic — it teaches people who can already do magic to do it without destroying things they care about.

**Mortality rate:** Four members in their first year may not survive it — not from malice but from the statistical reality that learning advanced magic in concentrated doses from seven different teachers simultaneously is demanding on the human body.

**Internal threats:**
- Two current Conclave members are **Ashfallen** — compromised but not broken, working from the inside, or rationalizing. Unclear which. The Conclave does not yet know.
- Deeply afraid of the Ashen Hand and equally afraid of being confused with the Ashen Hand. This has caused three diplomatic incidents.

**Plot connection:** **Corvus Tane** (Roland's companion, joins between Game One and Two) is a third-year Conclave member. His arrival at the Conclave to report what he is sensing from the volcano triggers the events that drive him into the party. In Game Three, the Conclave commits its remaining loyal mages to the Battle of Drûn-Khazad as a counter-force to Vaeroth's Ashen Hand.

---

### The Loremaster's Archive (Eldermark)

**The largest written-knowledge repository** in the human kingdoms. Housed in a building in Aldenholt that has been continuously added to for three hundred years and now occupies an entire city block.

**Staff:**
- Forty full-time scholars
- Twelve archivists
- One woman named **Henrietta**, who has been there longer than anyone, knows where everything is, and refuses to create a written index because she *is* the index and she intends to remain indispensable

**Politics:** Maintains neutrality by charging equally high access fees to every kingdom and owing favors to none. Solgrade pays the most. Vosskara pays the least but consistently. The Aelorin Pilgrimage Circuit has a standing arrangement that allows Aelorin loremasters access without payment in exchange for occasional original-source contributions.

**Critical hidden asset:** The Archive maintains a complete genealogical record of Eldermark's noble houses going back to the Second Age. **Neither Henrietta nor any scholar has connected this record to Mordvar's bloodline.** The connection is there. Game One's research arc with Roland and Henrietta begins to make it.

**Plot connection (Game One opening):** Roland visits Henrietta to research the iron pommel he saw in the Iron Chalice chapel. She gives him notes she has been quietly compiling — a genealogical thread connecting an object matching the pommel to Second Age binding records. Within twenty-four hours she is dead, her quarters searched, and the original notes gone. Roland has only the duplicate she pressed into his coat at the door.

Her death is the first murder of the trilogy. It is what proves to Roland that the pommel matters and that hesitation will kill him.

## Shadow Organizations

### The Crimson Ledger

Operates out of Solgrade. The most professional criminal organization in the human kingdoms — less a gang than a brokerage for illegal contracts.

**Structure:** A Ledger-Master (currently anonymous) and twelve Account-Holders who manage regional operations. No one below Account-Holder level knows more than their own cell.

**Services:** Assassination, information brokerage, artifact acquisition, identity creation. They do not deal in kidnapping — it attracts too much attention — and will not accept contracts against children.

**Price:** Always paid in advance. Half refunded if the contract is cancelled within forty-eight hours. No refunds after execution begins.

**Plot connection:** House Korvath uses the Ledger for acquisition work. In Game One, Roland discovers a Ledger contract against Henrietta was placed three weeks before her death — evidence she was targeted deliberately, not opportunistically.

---

### The Hollow Court

The Ashen Hand's intelligence arm — not mages, but human agents recruited for loyalty and unremarkability. They look like merchants, scribes, sailors, servants.

**Method:** Long-term placement. A Hollow Court agent may spend years in a position before receiving a meaningful tasking. Most tasked actions are observation and reporting, not violence.

**Recruitment:** Always from people who have lost something to the existing order — merchants ruined by noble tariffs, families destroyed by kingdom wars, people who have genuine grievances and have been found by someone offering purpose.

**Internal reality:** Most members believe they are working toward a better world. They do not know they serve Mordvar. They know only their immediate handler.

**Plot connection:** Prince Aedric's Ashen Hand connections run through the Hollow Court. His handler is a Hollow Court agent who has been a minor Eldermark court functionary for eleven years.

---

### The Pale Supper

A death cult devoted to Mordvar's philosophy — not to Mordvar directly, who they have never met and whose existence as a bound entity they interpret metaphorically. They believe mortality is the world's fundamental error and that the Hollow King represents the truth of what existence should be.

**Membership:** Mostly educated people — failed scholars, disillusioned priests, minor nobles who have watched too many people they loved die. They meet in private homes. They share meals. They discuss theology.

**Danger:** They are passive until they are not. Three members have committed murders they described as "releasing" victims from mortality. The cult does not endorse this. Several members have quietly informed on these individuals to authorities. The internal debate about whether murder is theologically consistent is ongoing.

**Plot connection:** In Game Two, Roland encounters a Pale Supper cell in Solgrade that has acquired genuine information about the Vault of Aen-Vael, which they believe contains the proof of their theology. Their information is accurate. Their interpretation is wrong.

---

### The Forgetting (The Nameless Council)

Not a criminal organization — a network of the Nameless (Aelorin who approached the Aelthiren but could not complete it and gave up their names at the glade boundary).

**Nature:** Reborn into new identities, operating in the world's grey spaces. They remember being Aelorin. They remember their old names. They do not use them.

**Function:** Information network, sanctuary provision, and occasional quiet intervention. They have been watching the world's politics for centuries with the patience of people who have already surrendered everything they were.

**The Council:** Seven senior Nameless who coordinate. They have no formal leader. They make decisions by consensus that sometimes takes years.

**One operative** can enter the Weeping Wood and return. They have never explained how. The Council does not ask.

**Plot connection:** One Nameless Council operative becomes a critical resource for Roland in Game Two — they can move him into Khorumzad through routes that bypass the Ashen Hand blockade. Their price is not money but a service: deliver a message to someone in Lirien-Thal that the operative cannot go to themselves.

## Heroic Bands

### The Company of the Salt Road

A mercenary company based on the eastern trade road between Eldermark and Vosskara. Twenty-three members, mixed human and one dwarf (Orgin Saltshield, their siege specialist).

**Reputation:** Reliable, non-political, slightly expensive. They have fought for every kingdom at some point. They have never broken a contract.

**Captain:** **Mira Halsten** — former Frost Brotherhood ranger who left after a disagreement about a contract she found morally indefensible. She will not discuss what the contract was. Her people know not to ask.

**Plot connection:** Roland can hire the Salt Road as backup for the Khorumzad insertion. If the Ashsteel optional quest is completed, Mira accepts Ashsteel-equipped contracts at a reduced rate — she wants her people to survive, and she knows what the metal means.

### The Brightwatch

A volunteer defensive organization in the border towns between Eldermark and the Ashfields. Not soldiers — farmers, merchants, and craftspeople who have decided that if the Frost Brotherhood won't come this far south and the Iron Chalice won't come this far east, someone has to stand watch.

**Organization:** Town-cell structure. Each cell has a Speaker who coordinates with adjacent cells. No central authority. No pay — they supply themselves.

**Equipment:** Whatever they have. Some Brightwatch cells are well-equipped from local wealth. Others fight with farm tools. The disparity is a constant source of internal tension.

**Political status:** Legally gray. Eldermark's crown has not officially recognized them and has not officially dissolved them. The crown's actual position is that the Brightwatch existing means one fewer thing the crown has to pay for.

**Plot connection:** In Game One, a Brightwatch cell provides Roland shelter and information about Ashen Hand movements near the Ashfields. In Game Three, Brightwatch fighters join the march to Drûn-Khazad — the only force that travels the entire route from the Ashfields to the volcano.

### The Dawnbringers

A loose fellowship of Aelorin who have chosen active intervention in world events rather than the Pilgrimage Circuit's observational approach. Sanctioned but not officially endorsed by Lord Aelthurion — plausible deniability for both parties.

**Members:** Seven. Each operates independently. They share information through the Pilgrimage Circuit's infrastructure but do not act together except in emergencies.

**Role in the trilogy:** One Dawnbringer — **Seren of the Third Glade** — is Roland's Aelorin companion in Games Two and Three. She joined the fellowship because she watched aelnaeth take her mother and decided that passive contemplation was its own kind of surrender.

**Aelthurion's view:** He is quietly proud of them. He does not say this.

## Overview

**Full name:** Roland Ashford
**Age:** 34 at the start of Game One
**Origin:** Eldermark, raised in a border town in the Ashfields
**Former role:** Knight of the Iron Chalice, expelled

---

## Appearance

Lean and weathered beyond his years — the Ashfields age people. Brown hair cut short, always slightly too long by the time he gets around to cutting it again. Grey eyes. A scar on his jaw from a training accident at fourteen that he has long since stopped noticing. Carries himself like someone who was trained to stand straight and has slowly stopped bothering.

---

## History

Born to a minor landholder family in the eastern Ashfields, the youngest of three. His two older sisters inherited the land; Roland joined the Iron Chalice at sixteen because it was the most honorable exit available to a third child with a good sword arm.

He was a good knight. Not remarkable — no exceptional battles or heroic moments — but reliable, thoughtful, and well-liked by the enlisted soldiers he worked alongside. He rose to senior knight by competence rather than connections, which in the Iron Chalice means something.

**The expulsion:** Three years before Game One, Roland was assigned to inventory and document the Order's chapel relics — a routine administrative task. He recognized the iron pommel on the founding-relic altar from a description in a Second Age text he had read while recovering from a broken leg. He spent four months quietly researching before bringing his findings to the Grandmaster: the pommel is a piece of the Sundered Crown.

Dame Calla Vane's response was to ask him to write nothing down and speak of it to no one. Her reasons were not corrupt — the Order was financially insolvent, and the pommel was the founding relic's centerpiece; losing it would end the Order. Roland refused silence. He was expelled without charges, because charging him would require acknowledging what he had found.

He has been hunted since. The Iron Chalice did not send killers — but word spread, and those who want the Crown's location found him.

---

## Character

**Defining quality:** Roland does the thing that needs doing even when he cannot see how it ends well. This is not courage exactly — he is afraid often — but an inability to choose comfortable inaction once he knows what the right action is.

**Flaw:** He assumes people will eventually come to the same conclusion he has if he presents the evidence clearly enough. He is wrong about this more often than he expects, and it costs him relationships.

**Relationship to institutions:** The expulsion broke something in his faith in formal structures. He still believes in what the Iron Chalice *was* — the endurance doctrine, the culture of holding rather than winning — but not in its current execution. This creates a useful ambivalence: he can work within institutions without deferring to them.

**Sense of humor:** Dry, rare, usually at his own expense.

---

## Arc Across the Trilogy

**Game One:** Pursues the Sundered Crown's pieces while building the alliances that will eventually become the Grand Alliance. The central question is whether he can hold his own integrity under pressure to compromise.

**Game Two:** The Khorumzad descent is the hardest thing Roland has done. He watches Bromrin's mind deteriorate. He finds the Aeluvain in a place where the world's original song can be faintly heard by those close to the dying Eighth Star — a sound that should be beautiful and is instead profoundly sad. The question of the trilogy's second game: what do you do when doing the right thing requires watching people suffer?

**Game Three:** Roland's role shifts from seeker to protector. He has found what needed to be found. Now he has to keep Aldric Vane alive long enough to end this. The question of Game Three: can he let someone else be the instrument of the ending, or will the compulsion to do the right thing himself get in the way?

**Ending:** Roland does not wield the Aeluvain. Aldric does. Roland's role at the summit of Drûn-Khazad is to stand between Aldric and everything that would stop him. In both endings, Roland survives.

---

## Relationships with Companions

**Corvus Tane:** Corvus finds Roland exhausting and respects him deeply. Roland finds Corvus difficult and trusts him completely. They argue constantly. They would not survive the trilogy without each other.

**Seren of the Third Glade:** The most equitable relationship in the group — two people who have each seen institutions fail them and drawn similar conclusions. Seren challenges Roland's human-centric assumptions without contempt for them.

**Orion Farr:** The youngest companion. Roland's instinct is to protect him; Orion's instinct is to resist being protected. The resolution of this tension is one of Game Two's emotional throughlines.

**Bromrin:** Roland and Bromrin meet properly in Game Two, when Bromrin's mind is already beginning to slip. Roland is the only person in the descent party who consistently treats Bromrin as the king he still is, not as a patient. This matters more than any tactical decision Roland makes.

## Corvus Tane

**Age:** 29 at Game Two start (joins between Game One and Two)
**Origin:** Solgrade, lower-merchant family
**Role:** Conclave of the Unseen Hand mage, third-year member

**Appearance:** Slight, dark-skinned, always slightly pale. His hair began going white in patches at twenty-seven — the first permanent cost of advanced magic. He has stopped mentioning it.

**Magic:** Primarily environmental — he senses and manipulates ambient conditions. Temperature, pressure, the electricity in storm air. He can feel the gold-hunger's magical contamination spreading through Khorumzad before anyone else can measure it. This makes him invaluable in Game Two and slowly damages him.

**Character:** Corvus came to the Conclave because he understood, at sixteen, that the things that happened around him when he was afraid or angry would eventually kill someone he cared about. He has spent thirteen years building precise control. He is competent, exacting, and genuinely difficult to like until you understand that the precision is not coldness — it is the architecture he built over something much more volatile.

**Arc:** Game Two is Corvus's game. What he senses in Khorumzad is not just contamination — it is a resonance with the part of himself he has spent years containing. The question he cannot avoid: is his control mastery or suppression? By Game Three, he has an answer, and it costs him.

**Relationship to Roland:** They argue. They are right in different ways about different things. Corvus respects Roland's integrity and finds his emotional reasoning maddening. Roland respects Corvus's precision and finds his affect exhausting. Neither would choose a different partner.

---

## Seren of the Third Glade

**Age:** Appears 30; actually 180
**Origin:** The Greatwood, Third Glade of the Pilgrimage Circuit
**Role:** Dawnbringer (active Aelorin interventionist); joins Game Two

**Appearance:** Tall for an Aelorin, silver-grey hair kept practical, amber eyes. Wears clothes suited for moving through varied terrain rather than Aelorin ceremonial dress. Carries a bow she made herself from Third Glade silverwood — she will not discuss the significance of this.

**Background:** Watched her mother take the Aelthiren — the slow transformation from person to tree that is the Aelorin's death of despair — over the course of thirty years. Was present for the final decade. Decided afterward that contemplation without action was its own form of surrender.

**Character:** Clear-eyed without cynicism. She has seen more history than Roland and Corvus combined, but does not use this as leverage. She is neither warmer nor colder than the situation requires.

**Unique quality:** She can hear the Aeluvain. Not clearly — only as a sensation of something that should be present being absent, a frequency the world is missing. As they descend toward the Vault, this sensation becomes increasingly distressing in ways she does not explain because she does not have words for it in a language humans use.

**Arc:** Seren's role in the trilogy is partly practical (her long memory provides context no human character has) and partly personal. She chose the Dawnbringers to avoid her mother's path. Game Three forces her to consider whether active engagement with mortality — watching humans she cares about die quickly rather than slowly — is actually a better way to live an immortal life.

---

## Orion Farr

**Age:** 22 at Game Two start; joins midway through Game One
**Origin:** Caer Brannoch, sailor's family
**Role:** Scout, Brotherhood-endorsed sailor, Tidewarden auxiliary

**Appearance:** Short, compact, sun-dark, constantly moving. A habit of assessing exits in any room he enters, which Corvus finds annoying and Roland finds useful.

**Background:** Signed onto a Brotherhood crew at sixteen. Made his qualifying solo sail at nineteen. Was on the voyage that first tested the Shroud Sea approach patterns — the voyage that confirmed the forty-year window theory. He is one of three sailors alive who has seen the edge of the storm-wall and returned.

**Character:** Orion is the party's youngest member and the most practically competent in a specific domain. He has been sailing in dangerous waters since he was old enough to hold a tiller; he is comfortable with risk in a way that Roland's caution sometimes conflicts with. He is not reckless — he calculates — but he calculates faster and with less visible anguish than Roland does.

**Growth:** Orion begins the trilogy with a sailor's philosophy — do the voyage, come home, do the next one. By Game Three, he has understood that some voyages end differently. The question his arc poses is whether someone who has built their identity around competence can handle a situation where competence is not enough.

**Relationship to Roland:** Orion resists Roland's protective instinct, which Roland extends to him as the youngest. The resolution of this dynamic — Roland accepting Orion as an equal rather than a charge — is one of the trilogy's quieter emotional arcs.

## Aldric Vane (Companion — Game Three Only)

**Age:** 41
**Origin:** Coldstoke, a village in the Vosskaran frontier
**Role:** Last of Mordvar's bloodline; the only person who can wield the Aeluvain against him

**Appearance:** Broad-shouldered, scarred hands from the forge, grey starting at his temples. Looks like what he is: a man who has spent twenty years hammering metal and would rather be doing that.

**Background:** Born to a family that had hidden its lineage for three generations. He does not know he is Mordvar's descendant when Game Three begins. He knows only that he has headaches near old sites, that metal responds to his touch in ways it shouldn't, and that two people have tried to kill him in the last year for reasons no one has explained.

**Character:** Aldric has built a complete and satisfying life in Coldstoke. He has a reputation, a craft, relationships, a role in the community. He does not want to be part of a trilogy. He becomes part of one anyway, because the alternative is watching everything he has built be destroyed.

**The weight:** Wielding the Aeluvain against Mordvar requires that Aldric consciously carry Mordvar's hollow — the emptiness Mordvar made of himself — for as long as it takes to reach the Aescstól. He will know what it feels like to be Mordvar. Not the evil: the void where selfhood used to be. The fear that created it.

**Arc:** Aldric's arc is the trilogy's final argument for its governing theme — that bonds between people are more durable than institutions, and more powerful than any single act of heroism. He survives the summit not because he is special but because he is loved.

## Villains

### Mordvar — The Hollow King

Formerly **Bealoric**, High King of the Aescryd in the Second Age. A man who was afraid of death and chose a ritual that eliminated the problem by eliminating the self. He became hollow — not evil in the motivating sense, but absent. A void that was once a person, acting with the cold persistence of water through stone because there is nothing left inside to want anything different.

He has been bound for two thousand years. The binding weakens. He does not think; he accumulates. He does not plan; he exerts pressure. His hosts plan for him. The Ashlord interprets his will.

**Physical presence at the end:** He emerges from the caldera on the third day of the Battle of Drûn-Khazad. Tall, grey-pale, dressed in something that was once clothing. He does not speak. He does not threaten. He walks down the slope. His presence is a cold of *will* — not temperature. Men find their arms heavy.

**His tragedy:** He was a man. The fear that created him is a fear anyone can recognize at three in the morning. This does not make him less dangerous. It does make the question of how to end him more complicated.

---

### Vaeroth the Pale

**Former role:** Solgrade court mage
**Current role:** Hierarch of the Ashen Hand

Sold his allegiance to the Ash Throne a decade before the trilogy for the amplified power the Throne offers its mages. Believes this was a rational transaction. Has never allowed himself to examine whether what he received is worth what he surrendered.

**Manner:** Precise and cold. Not cruel for pleasure — cruelty is wasteful. He kills efficiently and feels nothing after because he has arranged not to.

**Weakness:** The amplification that makes Ashen Hand mages powerful is also deterioration. Vaeroth knows this. He does not discuss it. He has perhaps two years before his body cannot sustain the cost. He intends to finish the war before the bill comes due.

**Plot function:** Vaeroth is the chase in Game Three. He is hunting Aldric specifically — he knows what the bloodline means and what the Aeluvain requires. The Frost Brotherhood's rear-guard action to slow him is what buys Aldric the time to reach the summit.

---

### Prince Aedric Vane

**Age:** 26
**Role:** Youngest prince of Eldermark; Ashen Hand's primary political asset in the human kingdoms

Not a monster — a young man who believes genuinely that the current order is failing and that Mordvar's world would be more just than Othric's. His handler has spent six years carefully constructing this belief.

**What makes him dangerous:** He is not wrong that the current order fails people. The Hollow Court recruits from genuine grievance. Aedric's grievances are real. His solution is wrong in ways he cannot see because the person who should have shown him alternative solutions has been carefully kept away.

**Arc:** If Roland exposes the Hollow Court operation in Game One, Aedric is confronted with evidence that his beliefs were constructed rather than discovered. What he does with this is one of Game One's significant optional outcomes. He can become an ally. He cannot become a friend.

---

## Key NPCs

### Dame Calla Vane — Grandmaster, Iron Chalice

Age 58. The Iron Chalice's finances are her private crisis — she expended Roland because she believed his silence was necessary and has lived with that decision since. She is not corrupt; she is trapped between an institution she loves and a secret she cannot afford to expose.

**Roland's arc with her:** Game One requires Roland to resolve the Order's debt to earn their alliance. The confrontation with Dame Calla when he returns is the scene where she acknowledges the expulsion was wrong without being able to say it plainly. She says it in action instead: she rides to Drûn-Khazad.

---

### Henrietta — Loremaster's Archive

Has worked in the Archive longer than any living person. Refuses to create a written index because she is the index and intends to remain indispensable. She knows where everything is, including things she should not know about.

**Her death:** The first murder of the trilogy. She gave Roland a duplicate of her genealogical research before she was killed — she knew someone was watching. The fact that she made the duplicate and pressed it into his coat at the door proves she knew and did not run. Roland has thought about this often.

---

### King Bromrin Deepdelver — Khorumzad

Pious, beloved, personally humble. He was the most grounded of the three dwarven kings before Drossvik began poisoning his gold-hunger. By the time Roland's party reaches him, Bromrin is aware his own mind is failing and is trying to hide it — not from pride but because he does not want his people to see their king's dignity slip.

**The scene that defines him:** Deep in the Khorumzad descent, Bromrin has a clear moment and asks Roland to promise him that if the treatment fails, Roland will not let the others watch him become something unrecognizable. Roland promises. Corvus, who overheard, says nothing.

---

### Queen Eilwen the Grey — Caer Brannoch

Age 52. Widowed. Fifteen years on a throne she took through a succession crisis. Her court knows not to test whether she belongs there.

**Political position:** Eilwen is the most strategically sophisticated of the human rulers. She has been watching the Shroud Sea for twenty years and has privately concluded that something on the other side of the storm-wall is connected to Mordvar's resurgence. She is right. She has not yet told anyone because she is still deciding what to do with the knowledge.

**Role in the trilogy:** Provides naval support (Game Two) and builds the final naval muster around Tidewarden command (Game Three). Her relationship with Roland is professional respect without warmth — she likes capable people and is always slightly suspicious of why they are capable.

---

### Despot Yaromir the Cold — Vosskara

Age 61. Three assassination attempts survived. Governs through paranoia and competence in roughly equal measure.

**What he wants:** Acknowledgment. Not tribute — he knows the tribute framing is politically toxic and has largely stopped demanding it in formal contexts. What he actually wants is for Eldermark to say plainly, once, that Vosskara has been holding the line and the world would have fallen without them. No one will say it. This has made him inflexible about everything else.

**Roland's approach:** Game One's Vosskara arc requires Roland to not just negotiate with Yaromir but to understand him. The conversation that earns Vosskaran commitment to the alliance is not political — it is personal. Roland asks Yaromir what he wants to be remembered for, and Yaromir, who has never been asked this question, answers honestly.

### Drossvik — Khorumzad Senior Advisor / Ashen Hand Mole

The Ashen Hand's primary agent in the dwarven kingdoms. Planted in Bromrin's court eleven years ago as a trusted administrator. Has been slowly amplifying Bromrin's gold-hunger through a compound mixed into ceremonial wine.

**Character:** Drossvik is not ideologically committed to Mordvar's cause. He is the Ashen Hand's man because they found him when he was desperate and he has been in too deep to exit for years. He is miserable. He is also going to keep doing it unless something forces the choice.

**His end:** In Game Two, Corvus detects the compound's magical signature before Drossvik realizes he has been found. Drossvik's confrontation with Roland is the scene where he has the option to provide information in exchange for something he can't quite name. He does. He is not forgiven. He is used. He considers this fair.

## Premise

The Sundered Crown — a seven-piece binding artifact forged in the Second Age to seal Mordvar in the Hollow Hearth — is slowly losing its power as Mordvar's influence seeps back into the world. The pieces must be reassembled and the binding renewed before the seal fails completely.

Roland Ashford knows the iron pommel on the Iron Chalice's founding altar is one of the seven pieces. He has been hunted since he said so.

---

## Act I: The Hunted Scholar

### Opening

Game One opens with Roland being chased through Aldenholt at night. He is not running from the Iron Chalice directly — they expelled him without official charges. He is running from someone hired by someone who wants to know what he found. He does not yet know who.

He reaches the Loremaster's Archive and the scholar Henrietta, who has been quietly compiling genealogical research she did not fully understand. She gives him a duplicate she has prepared — a copy of her notes connecting an iron pommel to Second Age binding records. She presses it into his coat at the door with an urgency she does not explain.

Within twenty-four hours, Henrietta is dead, her quarters searched, the original notes gone. Roland has only the duplicate.

**This is the first murder. It is what proves to Roland that the pommel matters and that hesitation will kill him.**

---

### [MAIN QUEST] The Pommel — Piece One

The iron pommel is in the Iron Chalice's chapel in Aldenholt. Roland cannot walk in the front door — he is expelled and being watched.

The Order's financial crisis gives him a route. Dame Calla Vane has been secretly meeting with a Solgrade banking agent to negotiate the debt extension. Roland intercepts the agent, learns the terms, and realizes he can offer something the agent cannot: evidence of House Korvath's weapons-smuggling operation, which the banking house has been trying to document because Korvath is undercutting their caravan contracts.

This information earns him a private meeting with Dame Calla. The meeting does not resolve the expulsion — she cannot undo it without reopening the question of the pommel — but she gives him access to the chapel for forty minutes, alone, with the light off.

Roland takes the pommel. He also takes nothing else, replaces the altar fixture with an iron rod of identical weight, and tells Calla what he has done before he leaves. She does not stop him. She does not acknowledge him either. He understands.

**Piece One acquired.**

---

### [MAIN QUEST] Henrietta's Thread

The duplicate Henrietta gave Roland contains references to four more pieces — the gold coin (Solgrade), the silver clasp (Aelorin Greatwood), the copper wire (Karaz-Dûn), and two she could not locate. Her notes are partial because she was killed before she finished.

Her research points to a Second Age genealogical record in the Archive's restricted section. The record is now inaccessible — her death is officially unsolved but the Archive has been locked to outsiders pending investigation.

**[SIDE QUEST] The Archive's Locked Room:** Roland must find the Archive's assistant archivist, Tomlin, who has been placed on temporary suspension while the investigation proceeds. Tomlin has a copy of the restricted section's access key that he has not reported. He is terrified. If Roland can convince him that knowing what is in the restricted section is the only way to find out who killed Henrietta, Tomlin provides access.

The restricted section contains: the complete noble genealogy of Eldermark back to the Second Age. Roland finds the connection Henrietta was building — Mordvar's bloodline runs through a minor lateral branch, to a family that emigrated to the Vosskaran frontier three generations ago and changed their name. The name they changed it to: Vane.

**Not the Iron Chalice's Grandmaster. A different Vane. A blacksmith in a frontier village called Coldstoke. Aldric Vane.**

Roland does not yet know what this means. He logs it and moves on.

## Act II: The Four Kingdoms

### Overview

The second act spans all four human kingdoms as Roland tracks the remaining pieces. Each kingdom offers a major questline and one side/optional quest. The order is player-determined but the Solgrade arc is recommended last — it connects the information from the other three.

---

### [MAIN QUEST] Solgrade — The Gold Coin

House Korvath has the gold coin and knows what it is. They have been waiting for someone to come asking.

Ser Aldric Vossant of the Golden Lance approaches Roland first — he has been watching House Korvath for two years trying to document a weapons-smuggling operation and needs evidence Roland can potentially gather from inside the House. He offers Golden Lance cooperation in exchange.

Roland infiltrates Korvath's counting house using an identity the Crimson Ledger sold him (this costs him a favor he will repay in Game Two). He finds the smuggling evidence and the gold coin.

Korvath's negotiation, when it comes, is sophisticated. They know the coin is a piece of the Sundered Crown. They also know Roland needs it. Their price: Roland publicly endorses House Korvath as the broker for military contracts during the coming war. Roland does not accept this. He offers instead: the smuggling evidence he found — which implicates two other Houses on the Council — in exchange for the coin and Korvath's agreement not to interfere.

Korvath accepts. They are pragmatists.

**[OPTIONAL QUEST] The Vossant Verdict:** Roland provides the smuggling evidence to Vossant. Vossant takes it to the Council. Three Houses are implicated; two are censured; one (House Pelarin) is expelled from the Council entirely. Vossant is promoted to Council liaison. His conviction deepens. Golden Lance commits to the final battle.

**Piece Two acquired.**

---

### [MAIN QUEST] Vosskara — The Bronze Ring

The bronze ring is in the possession of Despot Yaromir, who does not know what it is. It was given to his grandmother by a wandering smith who stayed the winter and paid for his lodging with the ring. Yaromir wears it as a thumb ring because it was his grandmother's and she told him it was old.

Negotiating with Yaromir is not about the ring — Yaromir would give Roland the ring if asked plainly, because rings are not armies and he has bigger concerns. The negotiation is about whether Vosskara will commit to the alliance when the time comes.

The conversation that earns Vosskaran commitment is the one where Roland asks Yaromir what he wants to be remembered for. Yaromir, who has spent fifteen years demanding tribute no one will acknowledge is owed, answers: *holding the line when no one was watching.* Roland says: *Then let us make sure someone watches it.* He cannot promise anything — he has no authority — but he acknowledges the thing that has never been acknowledged.

Yaromir gives him the ring.

**[SIDE QUEST] The Tribute Papers:** Frost Brotherhood Captain Mira Halsten has intelligence on Ashen Hand movements along the Vosskaran frontier. She will share it if Roland helps her deliver evidence to Yaromir proving that the Ashen Hand has been agitating the Tribute War dispute from inside both Eldermark and Vosskara — deliberately maintaining the conflict. Yaromir reads the evidence. He is very still for a very long time. Then he says: *I see.* The tribute dispute de-escalates. Not resolved — but quiet.

**Piece Three acquired.**

## Act III: The Elder Peoples

### [MAIN QUEST] Caer Brannoch — The Copper Wire

The copper wire is in the Sailors' Brotherhood's Copper Isles Archive. The Brotherhood will not give it to Roland unless he earns Brotherhood endorsement — which requires completing one voyage with a Brotherhood crew.

Orion Farr (Game One's companion addition from Caer Brannoch) is a Brotherhood-endorsed sailor who signs Roland onto a voyage to the Copper Isles. The voyage itself is the quest: a storm that forces them off course, a detour to a Brotherhood safe-house on a small island where they discover a Hollow Court agent who has infiltrated Brotherhood communications, and the final approach to the Isles.

At the Archive, the copper wire is catalogued as a Second Age artifact of unknown function. The Brotherhood's archivist releases it without difficulty once Roland demonstrates endorsement. She does mention, offhandedly, that the storm-window in the Shroud Sea has been revised from eleven years to nine.

**[SIDE QUEST] The Brotherhood's Shroud Charts:** Access requires the endorsement Roland now has. The charts confirm the nine-year window and provide storm-pattern data that Eilwen's court has been seeking for years. Delivering the data to Eilwen earns Caer Brannoch's formal naval commitment.

**Piece Four acquired.**

---

### [MAIN QUEST] The Aelorin Greatwood — The Silver Clasp

The silver clasp is held in the Second Glade of the Aelorin Pilgrimage Circuit — not as a relic but as an unknown old object that was brought to the glade by an Aelorin who completed the Aelthiren three hundred years ago and left their possessions at the glade boundary. No one has examined it closely because the objects of the Aelthiren are not touched.

Roland must be granted access to Lirien-Thal and then to the Second Glade. Lord Aelthurion will not refuse this — he has been watching for someone to come asking about the Crown pieces for forty years. He has known it was coming. His grief is that it took this long.

**The conversation with Aelthurion:** One of the trilogy's significant scenes. Aelthurion tells Roland plainly what he has observed for four decades — the pattern of Mordvar's slow accumulation, the contamination spreading from Khorumzad, the Ashen Hand's movements. He provides a complete intelligence briefing. He also tells Roland what the Aeluvain is and what it requires: a vessel of Mordvar's bloodline to wield it.

Roland receives the silver clasp and the most important piece of information in the trilogy: Aldric Vane's name and what he is.

**Piece Five acquired.**

---

### [MAIN QUEST] Karaz-Dûn — The Copper Disc

**Note:** Two of the seven pieces include copper — the wire (Brotherhood Archive) and the disc (Karaz-Dûn). The disc is harder.

King Thrarin has not left his treasury in two years. His gold-hunger has been accelerated by a compromised steward (Drossvik's operation extended to Karaz-Dûn from Khorumzad). The Gold-Speaker auditor who documented the acceleration has been placed in protective custody in Kazaad-Brak — not imprisoned, but isolated.

Roland cannot reach Thrarin. He can reach Kazaad-Brak and the auditor, Barak Stonecroft, through the Underway.

Barak's information is the confirmation of Drossvik's operation in the dwarven kingdoms — which sets up Game Two. He also knows the disc's location: it is in Thrarin's treasury as part of a Second Age artifact collection. Thrarin collected it a century ago as a curiosity.

The approach: Roland works through Bromrin, whose mind is intact at this point in Game One. Bromrin contacts Thrarin's eldest daughter and regent-in-practice, Darva, who has been managing kingdom affairs while Thrarin stays in the treasury. Darva retrieves the disc in exchange for the auditor's report — which gives her leverage to deal with her father's situation on her own terms.

**Piece Six acquired.**

## Act IV: The Seventh Piece and the Climax

### [MAIN QUEST] The Seventh Piece — The Obsidian Shard

Henrietta's notes referenced six pieces but not the seventh. Aelthurion knows — the obsidian shard is in Mor-Vethrin, the Naergrim dark elf city, where it was taken after the original Grand Alliance binding. The Naergrim have held it for two thousand years.

This is not a quest that can be resolved by negotiation or infiltration. The shard is in the Naergrim's central vault.

**Serethi the Twice-Dead's offer:** The Naergrim leader makes Roland an offer rather than a demand: he will provide the shard in exchange for Roland's acknowledgment that when Mordvar is defeated, the Naergrim will not be included in any punitive settlement. They chose the Ash Throne freely; they now wish to unchose it. Serethi does not offer loyalty to the Alliance — only withdrawal.

The moral question this poses is Game One's last real decision: is freeing the world from Mordvar worth making an arrangement that lets the Naergrim walk away from the atrocities of the last decade?

Roland must decide. There is no option to delay. Serethi has already calculated that Roland will accept — he is watching the calculation play out on Roland's face.

**[OPTIONAL QUEST] The Dissenting Voice:** A minority faction within the Naergrim wants genuine alliance rather than mere withdrawal. Their leader, a young Naergrim who goes by the name the Pale Defection uses as a joke and has adopted as a title, approaches Roland quietly. If Roland acknowledges this faction and creates a quiet channel for them, three Naergrim fighters join the Battle of Drûn-Khazad under their own authority. They survive. Serethi is furious. He honors the deal anyway.

**Piece Seven acquired.**

---

### [MAIN QUEST] The Binding Site

The Crown must be reassembled at the Binding Site — the original location of the Second Age ritual, which is not Drûn-Khazad but the valley below it: the Ashfields approach to the eastern slope.

The assembly itself is not a puzzle — the pieces fit together as the Crown they were. What Roland does not know is that the ritual to renew the binding requires blood of the Grand Alliance: a human, an Aelorin, and a dwarf.

Aelthurion is present. He arranged this without explaining it. Bromrin sent a representative — his personal guard commander, Vrenda Copperhand, who volunteered.

The binding is renewed. The Ashlord, through whom Mordvar's will was flowing most directly, is severed from the connection. The Ashen Hand's amplification drops sharply. Vaeroth loses a significant fraction of his power.

**The Crown's limitation:** This is not a victory. The renewed binding stabilizes what was deteriorating — it does not seal Mordvar again. The seal is broken in Khorumzad, where the Vault of Aen-Vael was disturbed. The only thing that can truly end Mordvar is the Aeluvain, wielded by someone of his bloodline. The renewed Crown buys time.

---

### Climax — The Ashen Hand Counterstroke

Vaeroth, diminished but not broken, launches a coordinated strike to prevent Roland from leaving the Ashfields. The climax is a fighting retreat.

The Brightwatch fighters who sheltered Roland earlier hold the rear. Some do not survive.

Orion, whose sailing background means he has been planning the exit route since they arrived, navigates them through a route the Ashen Hand doesn't know about — a Brotherhood safe-house tunnel that exits west of the Ashfields.

**Game One ends** with the Crown renewed, the binding stabilized, Roland carrying Aelthurion's intelligence about Aldric Vane, and the knowledge that the next step is Khorumzad.

## Premise

The Vault of Aen-Vael lies below the Hollow Hearth within Khorumzad, the deepest dwarven kingdom. King Bromrin's gold-hunger — deliberately accelerated by the Ashen Hand mole Drossvik — has driven his diggers to breach both chambers. The gold-hunger is now spreading through Khorumzad's atmosphere, contaminating the hold. The Ashen Hand knows the vault seal was disturbed and is moving to exploit it.

The Aeluvain — the fallen Eighth Star, the only thing that can truly end Mordvar — is in the Vault. Roland must reach it before the contamination makes Khorumzad impassable and before Vaeroth's forces seal the approaches.

**Corvus Tane** joins at the beginning of Game Two. The Conclave sent him to report what he has been sensing from the volcano; Roland's arrival interrupts that errand.

---

## Act I: The Siege Approaches

### Between Games

In the months since Game One:
- Roland has sent a message to Coldstoke asking Aldric Vane to go nowhere and speak to no one about his family history (it did not arrive in time — Aldric has already had one attempt on his life)
- Corvus's Conclave assignment to Khorumzad was triggered by his sensing the Vault's disturbance from Eldermark
- The Ashen Hand has moved a significant force to the eastern approach to Khorumzad's entrances
- Bromrin is now largely confined to his throne room; his regent-in-practice is his daughter, Darna Deepdelver

### Opening

Roland arrives at Khorumzad's outer gate to find an Ashen Hand encirclement already beginning — not a siege yet, but a cordon. They are not trying to stop people from entering. They are trying to ensure no one leaves with the Aeluvain.

Inside, Darna Deepdelver provides the situation: Bromrin has moments of clarity decreasing in frequency. Drossvik is still in the court — she does not know he is the source. Three of the nine dig levels below the great hall have been contaminated; workers brought back up are showing early gold-hunger symptoms that resolve slowly in clean air. The tenth level — the Hollow Hearth approach — has been sealed.

**Corvus's first action:** He walks the contaminated levels without a mask, senses the spread, and maps it. He comes back pale. He tells Roland the contamination is deliberate — it has a direction, a source, a propagation pattern that matches designed release rather than accident. Someone in the court has been managing it.

---

### [MAIN QUEST] Exposing Drossvik

The evidence is in the ceremonial wine stores — Corvus can trace the magical signature of the compound to its point of introduction. Drossvik has been adulterating the ceremonial wine that Bromrin alone consumes at the weekly court ritual.

**The confrontation:** Roland presents the evidence to Darna, not Drossvik directly. Darna's response is methodical — she secures the wine stores, removes Drossvik from court access, and then invites Roland to the meeting where Drossvik is confronted.

Drossvik, cornered, provides information. He tells them: the compound is reversible in theory — the Surgeons' School in Solgrade has been studying gold-hunger physiology with Thrarin's funding, and the research Corvus's environmental magic combined with the School's findings can produce a treatment protocol. He tells them: Vaeroth knows the Vault was breached and has had a secondary agent in the hold besides Drossvik — someone lower, still active. He does not know who.

He is not forgiven. He is used. He considers this fair.

---

### [MAIN QUEST] Bromrin's Treatment

The treatment protocol Drossvik described requires:
1. Corvus's environmental magic to isolate the contamination spread
2. Compounds from the School's research (which Roland sent for after Game One — they arrive by courier midway through this act)
3. Bromrin's cooperation during a period of clarity

The treatment scene is the trilogy's most personal sequence. Bromrin, in a clear moment, asks Roland directly: *Will this work?* Roland says he does not know. Bromrin says: *Good. I prefer honest men to kind ones.*

The treatment does not fully restore Bromrin. It arrests the artificial acceleration and allows natural recovery to begin. He will have more clarity more often. He will not be who he was before Drossvik. He knows this. He proceeds anyway.

**[SIDE QUEST] The Second Agent:** Drossvik indicated there is another agent in the hold. Orion, whose Brotherhood training in infiltration and observation is suited for this, spends two days quietly watching court staff behavior. He identifies a kitchen worker who has been absent from his post at specific intervals that match the contamination's spread patterns. The worker is confronted; he is not an ideologue, only someone who was paid and threatened into it; he cooperates; the contamination stops spreading.

## Act II: The Descent

### [MAIN QUEST] The Nine Levels

The descent to the Vault of Aen-Vael requires passing through nine dig levels below the great hall and the sealed Hollow Hearth approach. Each level presents escalating difficulty:

**Levels 1-3:** Heavy contamination but passable with Corvus's protection. The gold-hunger in the ambient air manifests as a pressure on perception — everything valuable looks more valuable; every decision feels like it should be made quickly; the urgency is artificial and both Corvus and Roland know it, which helps.

**Levels 4-6:** The walls have traces of Second Age construction — this was excavated deliberately long before the dwarves arrived. Someone built these chambers. The architecture is not dwarven. It is not any recognizable style. Seren (who joined the party after Game One ended) is quiet for a long time looking at the walls, then says: *This was built before the Third Age. Before the Second. This was here when the world was young.*

**Level 7:** The party encounters Ashfallen — three former Khorumzad soldiers who were captured during an Ashen Hand incursion and returned. They wear familiar faces. Two of Bromrin's personal guard who accompanied the descent freeze on recognition. One does not survive the hesitation.

**Level 8:** The Hollow Hearth approach. The air here is wrong — not contaminated, but old. As if the air has not moved in centuries. Corvus says the magical contamination from above does not reach this level. Something else is present. He cannot name it.

**Level 9 / The Vault Approach:** The seal Bromrin's diggers breached is visible — a circular door of Second Age metal with seven slots in a pattern matching the Sundered Crown. The slots are empty. The door is already open.

**The implication:** Someone else has been here. The Vault was breached not just by Bromrin's gold-hunger obsession but deliberately — someone reached the seal and opened it from the right side. The door opens inward. Whatever was inside came out.

Or: the Aeluvain let itself be found.

## Act III: The Vault and the Star

### [MAIN QUEST] Inside the Vault of Aen-Vael

The vault is a chamber that should not be possible — larger inside than the approaches suggest, with walls that curve in directions that do not resolve. The stone is pale blue-white, faintly luminous.

The Aeluvain is in the center: a gem the size of a fist, pale blue-white, cold to the touch. It does not glow — it absorbs light and returns it changed, slightly warmer in color.

**Seren's response:** She hears it. Not words — a frequency the world is missing, which her senses have been noting as absence since the beginning of Game Two. She walks to it before Roland does. She does not pick it up. She stands close to it for a long time, then says: *The Eighth Star did not fall. It chose to stay.*

**What the Aeluvain does:** The gem hums at a frequency only Aelorin can hear. It is not magic in the conventional sense. It is a piece of the world's original song. It cannot be used; it can only be returned to where it belongs — which is wielded by someone of Mordvar's bloodline against Mordvar himself, completing the note that was silenced when the Eighth Star fell.

**The cost of carrying it:** The Aeluvain is not dangerous to carry, but it is uncomfortable. Corvus, who is the most magically sensitive human in the party, says it feels like standing next to something grieving. Seren says: *Yes. That is accurate.*

Roland takes it. He is the least sensitive to its presence — another small mercy for a party already carrying enough.

---

### The Hard Choice — Bromrin

Deep in the descent, before they found the Vault, Bromrin had a clear moment and asked Roland to promise: if the treatment fails and Bromrin becomes something unrecognizable, Roland will not let the others watch his dignity slip.

On the ascent, Bromrin has a difficult episode — the worst yet. He does not recognize Darna. He demands gold he has already been given. He becomes frightened of the descent party and draws his belt knife.

Roland keeps his promise. He gets between Bromrin and the others, speaks to him quietly, and waits. Bromrin's clarity returns after six minutes. Bromrin sheathes the knife without acknowledging what just happened.

Corvus, who overheard the original promise and watched this play out, says nothing. Later, alone, he tells Roland: *I did not think you would actually do it.* Roland says: *I made a promise.* Corvus says: *Yes. That is not what I meant.*

## Act IV: The Siege and the Exit

### [MAIN QUEST] The Ashen Hand Siege

While the party was descending, Vaeroth's forces completed the cordon and began the siege. Khorumzad's outer gates are holding — the hold is built for this — but the eastern approach is cut off.

Darna's military assessment: the hold can sustain a siege for eighteen months. They cannot sustain the gold-hunger contamination long-term, even with the second agent neutralized. They need to clear the siege.

**The relief fleet option:** Caer Brannoch's naval commitment from Game One is relevant here — Eilwen's ships can land forces on the eastern coast near the Spine's eastern foot and march inland. This is the plan Roland sends word for via the Brotherhood's communication network.

**The timing problem:** The relief fleet takes six weeks to mobilize and sail. The Ashen Hand knows the fleet is coming. Vaeroth will attempt to extract what he wants from Khorumzad before the fleet arrives.

---

### [MAIN QUEST] Vaeroth's Gambit

Vaeroth does not attack the gates. He sends a message: give him the Aeluvain and the siege lifts. He is precise in his terms. He offers to let Bromrin's people leave.

He knows they will refuse. He is buying time for a different operation — a tunnel approach through Level 7 that his people have been digging for three weeks.

The tunnel approach is discovered by Orion (whose Brotherhood training in finding hidden passages and exits proved relevant again) and Seren (who can sense the displacement of old stone). The defense of the tunnel breach is the climax of Act IV — a close, confined fight in the lower levels with poor light and limited space, exactly the environment Tidewarden-style combat was designed for.

Vaeroth withdraws when the tunnel fails. He does not pursue — the relief fleet's approach has been confirmed and he is not prepared to fight both the hold and Eilwen's forces.

---

### [SIDE QUEST] The Nameless Council's Price

The Nameless Council operative who helped Roland reach Khorumzad through the blockade delivers their price: Roland must carry a message to someone in Lirien-Thal. The message is sealed. The operative says only: *She will know what it means.*

In Game Three, this message is one of the threads that reveals more of the Aelorin's longer history with the Sundered Crown — and explains why Aelthurion was watching for someone to come asking for forty years.

---

### Game Two Climax — Departure

The relief fleet's arrival breaks the siege's supply lines. Vaeroth retreats east toward Thal. Khorumzad is held.

**Game Two ends** with the Aeluvain secured, Bromrin stable and beginning slow recovery, and the party en route west to find Aldric Vane.

**What has been learned:**
- The Vault was opened from inside — the Aeluvain was waiting to be found
- Vaeroth has perhaps eighteen months before his own deterioration ends him
- The Gulmrak Horde is mobilizing under Uglakh; the orc warlord is done waiting
- Aldric Vane's location in Coldstoke is now known to the Ashen Hand

## Premise

Aldric Vane is the last of Mordvar's bloodline and the only person who can wield the Aeluvain against him. He is a blacksmith in Coldstoke, a frontier village in Vosskara. He does not know any of this. He has already survived one assassination attempt and does not know why.

The Gulmrak Horde under Uglakh is marching. Vaeroth is hunting Aldric specifically. The Ashen Hand knows what the bloodline means. The race to reach Aldric before Vaeroth does is Game Three's opening act.

In parallel: the Grand Alliance is assembling — a military operation of a scale not seen since the Second Age, with the three-to-one disadvantage that the Alliance's bond of shared purpose must overcome.

## Act I: The Race to Coldstoke

### Parallel Opening

Game Three opens in two simultaneous threads, cutting between them:

**Thread A — Aldric Vane (Coldstoke):** Aldric at his forge. A second assassination attempt — more professional than the first. He survives using a hammer and a furnace in ways that show he is not a trained fighter but is competent and calm under pressure. He does not know what to do next. He goes to the village elder. The village elder says: *There was a letter.* The letter is Roland's original message, arrived late. Aldric reads it. He says nothing for a long time. Then: *I need to leave.*

**Thread B — Roland (en route):** Roland's party is riding hard from the Khorumzad region west and north toward Vosskara. Frost Brotherhood intelligence has confirmed Vaeroth's direction. The race is explicit — whoever reaches Coldstoke first determines the trajectory of the rest of the game.

The player controls Roland. The thread-cutting creates dread.

---

### [MAIN QUEST] Reaching Coldstoke

Roland arrives in Coldstoke six hours after the second assassination attempt. Aldric is still there — he waited, which Roland respects and finds alarming.

**The conversation:** Roland explains, as plainly as he can, everything. Mordvar. The bloodline. The Aeluvain. What wielding it requires — that Aldric will need to consciously carry Mordvar's hollow for the duration, and know what it feels like to be the king who emptied himself out of fear.

Aldric asks: *What happens after?*

Roland says: *The Exchange ends when the Aeluvain is returned to the vault. The Tether is permanent. Those are the two paths Aelthurion described. You choose at the summit.*

Aldric says: *I haven't agreed to go to any summit.*

Roland says: *No. You haven't.*

**This is the scene that establishes the party's dynamic for Game Three.** Aldric is not a chosen hero. He is a man with a craft and a life, who is being asked to sacrifice both for people he does not know. He agrees because the alternative is watching Coldstoke burn — not because of destiny, not because he has always been special, but because he is the only person who can stop it and he is aware of this.

---

### [MAIN QUEST] The Frost Brotherhood Rearguard

Vaeroth arrives in the Coldstoke area three days after Roland. The Frost Brotherhood, acting on their standing intelligence operation, has already positioned three captains and their units along the approach routes.

The rearguard action that buys Aldric time to leave Coldstoke is brutal and one-sided. The Brotherhood is outmatched by Vaeroth's mages — but they know the terrain in a way the Ashen Hand does not, and they buy the time needed.

**Captain Mira Halsten** commands the western approach. The Salt Road mercenaries are with her. If the Ashsteel optional quest was completed, her people are carrying Ashsteel weapons. The Ashen Hand cannot detect them magically — which is the advantage that saves Mira's life specifically.

---

## Act II: The Alliance

### [MAIN QUEST] The Muster at the Copper Isles

The agreed rally point for the Grand Alliance is the Copper Isles — neutral ground, Brotherhood-connected, accessible by sea to all parties.

The forces that arrive depend entirely on what Roland accomplished in Games One and Two:
- **Iron Chalice:** 1,200 knights (if debt resolved in Game One)
- **Tidewarden:** Naval command of the eastern fleet under Eilwen
- **Frost Brotherhood:** What remains after the Coldstoke rearguard
- **Karaz-Dûn soldiers:** King Hagrim's force (does not wait for a vote)
- **Khorumzad:** Darna Deepdelver's command (Bromrin stayed home; he wrote a letter)
- **Vosskaran Despotate:** Yaromir's full frontier force (if Tribute War de-escalated)
- **Aelorin Dawnbringers:** Seven operatives, each worth a platoon
- **Conclave mages:** Forty-one loyal mages (two Ashfallen identified and isolated)
- **Golden Lance:** If Vossant's contract honored in Game One
- **Brightwatch:** Volunteers from the eastern frontier

**Total Alliance forces at maximum commitment:** 55,000. The Ash Throne fields 160,000.

The ratio is known at the muster. Aelthurion, who is present, says: *We have been here before. The Second Age Grand Alliance was smaller. They held.*

---

### [MAIN QUEST] Aldric's Training

Aldric is not a warrior. He needs to survive the approach to the summit of Drûn-Khazad — which means learning enough to not be killed before he reaches it. He does not need to fight Mordvar. He needs to walk to him.

**Who trains him:**
- Roland: endurance and practical defense. What the Iron Chalice teaches — not how to win but how to keep moving when you're losing
- Seren: the nature of what he will carry. She sits with him in the evenings and explains the Aeluvain not as an object but as a presence. What he will hear. What it will ask of him
- Orion: how to move in confined terrain, how to use obstruction, how to fall correctly

**The most important session:** Aldric asks Roland: *Have you ever been afraid of what you were doing?*

Roland says: *At the beginning. And then at the end. The middle is the part where you're too busy to be afraid.*

Aldric says: *I don't think that will work for me.*

Roland says: *It doesn't have to. You only have to get to the summit. After that it's done.*

---

### [SIDE QUEST] The Nameless Council's Message

Roland carries the sealed message from Game Two — the Nameless Council operative's price for the Khorumzad approach. At the muster, he delivers it to the Aelorin representative from Lirien-Thal.

She reads it. She folds it. She says to Roland: *Lord Aelthurion has been carrying this question for eight hundred years. You have answered it.* She does not explain further.

What the message contains: evidence that the Eighth Star's fall was not random loss but a choice — the Star chose to come down and stay, to be present at the moment it would be needed rather than watching from a distance it could not close. The Aelorin theology around the Aeluvain shifts: from mourning the fallen star to understanding that it went where it could help.

## Act III: The March and the Battle of Drûn-Khazad

### [MAIN QUEST] The Eastern Crossing

The Alliance force sails east from the Copper Isles — across the Shroud Sea's navigable edge, using the Brotherhood's storm-window intelligence and Orion's firsthand knowledge of the approach. The window is not comfortable. It is survivable.

Orion navigates the lead ship. He has been here before, at nineteen, on the voyage that confirmed the window existed. He is thirty-one now and knows things he did not know then. He does not talk during the crossing. After they are through, he says: *That's the second time. There won't be a third.*

The fleet makes landfall on Thal's western shore and begins the march east toward Drûn-Khazad.

---

### [MAIN QUEST] The Approach to the Caldera

The Gulmrak Horde and the Goblin Warrens defend the approaches in depth. This is not a pitched battle on open ground — it is three days of grinding advance through terrain the orcs know and the Alliance does not.

**Day One:** Hagrim's Kazaad-Brak soldiers lead the van. They have been fighting in adverse terrain against numerically superior forces their entire lives. They take the worst casualties and ask for no relief.

**Day Two:** The Alliance reaches the ash-steppe below the caldera. The Conclave's mages engage the Ashen Hand. Vaeroth is present — diminished but not broken. Corvus, who has been building toward something since the Vault, confronts him directly.

**Corvus vs. Vaeroth:** The fight is not a duel — it is one person who has spent years building precise control against one person who traded precision for amplification. Corvus wins because Vaeroth's deterioration has advanced further than Vaeroth acknowledged. Vaeroth's body cannot sustain what he asks of it. He knew this was coming. He chose to fight anyway because there was nothing else left to choose.

Corvus survives the fight with permanent alteration — his eyes change color, his aging accelerates visibly, the cost the Conclave warned him about paid in a single moment. He considers it acceptable. The fight is won.

**Day Three:** The push to the caldera. Uglakh's personal guard makes the final stand at the caldera's eastern approach — the most disciplined fighting the Alliance encounters. The Iron Chalice holds the center. Of twelve hundred knights, sixty-three survive Day Three.

---

### Mordvar Emerges

On the third day's evening, as the fighting near the caldera is at its peak, Mordvar emerges from the caldera itself.

He is tall and grey-pale. He was once a man who was afraid of death. He has been hollow for two thousand years. He does not speak. He does not threaten. He walks down the slope.

His presence is a cold of *will*. Men find their arms heavy. Not cold in temperature — cold in the sense of absence. The thing that makes people fight is the sense that the fighting matters. Mordvar's presence is the suggestion that nothing does.

**Aelthurion, watching from the Alliance's rear:** says nothing. He has been watching this approach for forty years. His grief is not for the danger — it is for the man this used to be.

**Roland, at the front:** recognizes the paralysis beginning in the soldiers around him and does the only thing he can think of — he shouts, loud enough for the line to hear, exactly what Mordvar is doing and exactly why it is not true. It is not eloquent. It works well enough. The line holds.

---

## Act IV: The Summit

### [MAIN QUEST] The Ascent

While the battle holds at the caldera's base, Roland, Aldric, Seren, Corvus, and Orion ascend. Five people. The summit of a live volcano. Mordvar walking toward them from below.

This is not a combat sequence — it is an endurance sequence. The heat is real. The ash is constant. The path is not designed for human use.

**What each person carries:**
- **Roland:** The promise he made at the beginning of the trilogy without knowing where it led. He does not regret it.
- **Aldric:** The life he is putting at risk for people he did not know a year ago, and now does.
- **Seren:** The weight of understanding that she chose to be present for mortality, and that this specific mortality, at this specific moment, is something she cannot look away from.
- **Corvus:** Pain. Genuine physical pain from the cost he paid fighting Vaeroth. He keeps moving because he made a commitment and because Roland is still moving and he is not going to be the one who stops first.
- **Orion:** The knowledge that he navigated here correctly and this is the furthest point of any voyage he has made. He does not know what comes after. He has decided this is all right.

---

### [MAIN QUEST] The Aescstól

The Hollow Hearth. The Ash Throne — obsidian streaked with silver, forged from cooled lava and bone-meal, the instrument through which Mordvar's will has flowed for two thousand years.

The Ashlord is here. Faceless, Mordvar's will made present. He is the last obstacle.

**The Ashlord's identity** is revealed here. It does not matter who he was. What matters is what he is now: a person who surrendered their self so completely to Mordvar's service that the distinction between self and service has collapsed. He is the warning for what Aldric must not become during the wielding.

The party defeats the Ashlord together. It is a fight that costs something. It is not the climax — the climax is what comes next.

---

### [MAIN QUEST] The Choice

Mordvar reaches the summit as the Ashlord falls.

Aldric stands before the Aescstól with the Aeluvain.

Seren says: *It is time. The Star is waiting.*

**Aldric's choice:**

---

**THE EXCHANGE (Light Ending):**

Aldric opens himself to the Aeluvain and takes Mordvar's hollow into himself voluntarily — the emptiness, the void where selfhood used to be, the two-thousand-year absence that was once a man's fear of death.

For thirty seconds, Aldric is hollow. He knows what Mordvar felt. He knows the fear that created it. He feels the absence of everything that makes a person themselves.

Then he returns the Aeluvain to the Aescstól — places it in the slot that was always there in the throne's design, the place prepared two thousand years ago. The hollow passes from Aldric into the vault. The note the Eighth Star was singing completes. Mordvar dissolves — not destroyed, not punished, but completed. The hollow fills. The two-thousand-year fear is finally over.

Aldric sits down on the ash. He is pale and shaking. He is entirely himself.

The world does not transform. The volcano does not go quiet. The sky does not clear. But Mordvar is gone, and the binding is not necessary, and the Aeluvain is home.

**Roland:** asks Aldric if he is all right. Aldric says: *I understand him now.* Roland says: *Yes. We can discuss that later. Can you stand?*

---

**THE TETHER (Dark Ending):**

Aldric takes Mordvar's hollow as in the Exchange — but at the moment of return, he makes a different choice. He does not send it to the vault. He binds it to himself. Mordvar's hollow becomes something Aldric carries, consciously, permanently: a contained void that cannot escape, anchored by Aldric's selfhood.

The cost is permanent. Aldric will live with the awareness of Mordvar's absence inside himself for the rest of his life. It will not be comfortable. It will be survivable.

What Aldric gets in exchange: certainty. Not just that Mordvar is gone, but that Mordvar cannot be reconstituted, cannot escape the vault, cannot find another host. The Exchange seals the vault. The Tether is a lock that only works while Aldric is alive — and passes, through the bloodline, to whoever comes after.

Aldric's descendants will carry this, generation to generation, until the bloodline ends or the world changes enough to dissolve it.

**Seren, watching:** says, very quietly: *The Eighth Star chose to stay when it could have remained distant. You chose the same thing.*

**Aldric:** says nothing.

## The Descent

After the summit, the party descends the caldera. The battle below has shifted — Mordvar's dissolution severed the Ashen Hand's amplification completely. Without it, the mages who had been sustaining Vaeroth's forces have nothing. The Gulmrak Horde, whose religious devotion to the volcano was always Mordvar's influence wearing orc theology, fractures — Uglakh survives but is fighting his own subordinate commanders by the time the Alliance's surviving forces reach the caldera's base.

**The count, at dusk of Day Three:**

Alliance losses vary based on which factions committed. At maximum Alliance:
- Iron Chalice: 112 survivors of 1,200 — they spent themselves escorting Roland's party to the caldera
- Tidewarden: 340 of 900 naval infantry who made landfall
- Frost Brotherhood: 89 of approximately 400 (the Coldstoke rearguard cost them most)
- Kazaad-Brak: Hagrim is alive; roughly a third of his force
- Khorumzad: Darna's command fought well; she survives
- Vosskara: Yaromir's full force arrived; held the supply lines; survived mostly intact
- Conclave mages: 23 of 41 (the engagement with the Ashen Hand was the most costly fight mage-for-mage)

The governing theme of the trilogy: bonds between people are more durable than institutions, and more powerful than any single act of heroism. The counts reflect this — the best-equipped, most prestigious forces took the highest casualties, and the volunteer fighters and frontier soldiers who came because of personal commitment to Roland or to each other performed beyond what their numbers should allow.

## Epilogues (Both Endings)

### The Exchange — Epilogue

**Aldric Vane** returns to Coldstoke. He is received with no ceremony because he told no one he was coming. His forge is cold. He restarts it. He is at work by the next morning.

He is invited to courts, offered titles, asked to speak at celebrations he did not attend. He declines most of them. He accepts one: an invitation from Dame Calla Vane to attend the Iron Chalice's annual Holding Day commemoration — the ceremony where the Order commemorates the times it held, not the times it won. He does not speak at the ceremony. He stands in the back with the enlisted soldiers.

**Roland Ashford** is offered reinstatement to the Iron Chalice with full honors. He declines. He has been expelled from an institution he believed in and rebuilt what he valued about it piece by piece outside its walls. The institution is not the thing. The thing is the thing.

He returns to Aldenholt and begins work with Tomlin (the assistant archivist) to rebuild the Archive's research into the Second Age binding records — not as a project with an endpoint, but as a permanent record so no Henrietta needs to compile it again alone.

**Corvus Tane** returns to the Conclave. His permanent alteration — changed eyes, accelerated aging — is noted but not remarked upon. He identifies the two Ashfallen members that the Conclave did not know about. He does not explain how he became certain. He does not rejoin the senior instruction track. He asks to teach the first-year students, who are the most likely to destroy something by accident and the most likely to benefit from someone who has already made the costly choices.

**Seren of the Third Glade** completes the pilgrimage to all Eight Glades — the first full circuit since the Eighth Star fell. At the Eighth Glade, which has been tended in anticipation for two thousand years with no star, she places the message that was delivered to her from the Nameless Council operative, having carried it since the Copper Isles muster. She is not there when the glade brightens for the first time in recorded history. She is already on the path to the next glade.

**Orion Farr** is offered his own ship by Queen Eilwen. He accepts. His first voyage is not a mission — it is the Shroud Sea window, now nine years open. He sails east, past the storm-wall, into whatever was on the other side. He sends one message back through the Brotherhood network before the window closes: *Ruins. Extensive. Unmistakably Second Age. This will take years.*

The Brotherhood archive is updated. The question of the Aescryd — Mordvar's sunken kingdom — becomes the next generation's project.

---

### The Tether — Epilogue

Most epilogue elements are the same. The differences:

**Aldric Vane** does not return to Coldstoke permanently. He stays close to the Aescstól — not inside Drûn-Khazad, but in the Thal frontier, where he can feel what he carries and knows he is its anchor. He is not imprisoned. He chose this. He builds a forge in the settlement that grows up in the Thal western shore — the Alliance's logistics base, which becomes a permanent outpost rather than a temporary camp.

He has a son, eventually. The son is told what he carries, when he is old enough to understand it. The conversation is in the Tether epilogue's final scene. The son asks: *Is it heavy?*

Aldric says: *Yes. But you were born knowing how to carry it. That is what the bloodline means.*

**Seren** visits Aldric regularly for the rest of his life, which is longer than most human lives — the Tether's anchoring has a side effect no one anticipated. She says, one visit: *You chose to stay. Like the Star.* He says: *You said that at the summit.* She says: *Yes. I wanted to see if it still felt true.* He says: *Does it?* She says: *Yes.*

---

## Final Reflection

The world does not transform. The age does not end. The Third Age continues — the Long Twilight that the Aelorin loremasters named it, which is both the age of humanity's greatest expansion and its most existential threat. Men are still numerous, cities still grow, trade still flows.

The Aelorin still dwindle. The dwarves grow somewhat less inward, with Bromrin's slow recovery serving as an argument that the Ashen Hand's manipulation is beatable. Thrarin's daughter Darva manages the transition to Karaz-Dûn's new reality with more grace than her father managed before it. Hagrim Bloodbeard lives four more years and names one of the contesting cousin-clans as his heir, to the other cousin-clan's outrage and everyone else's relief.

The world is not saved in the sense of being made invulnerable. It is saved in the sense of having been given more time, by people who chose to spend themselves buying it.

The Aelorin word for the Long Twilight is also, in an older usage, the word for the hour before dawn.
