# Draft: The Sorting Room

**Scene:** Act I, Aldenholt — Loremaster's Archive, side sorting room
**Characters:** Roland, Tomlin
**Story function:** Roland persuades the suspended assistant archivist to give him access to the restricted section. Sets `tomlin_helped = true`. Requires `henrietta_dead = true` for the key persuasion branch.
**TTS script:** `scripts/act1_scene_sorting_room.txt`

---

## Scene-Specific Voice Notes

**Tomlin:** Archivist's voice — precise pronunciation, verbal footnotes ("which is to say," "that is," "I should clarify"). Slightly too much breath under the words. When frightened, the footnotes increase. When approaching a hard decision, they stop and he goes plain. He is not a coward; he is a man who is frightened, knows he is frightened, and is embarrassed about both. Waiting, without knowing it, for someone to tell him he doesn't have to be unafraid to do the right thing.

**Roland:** Baseline. Direct, unhurried. The one tell is a slight pause before he says something he has decided he must say and wishes he didn't have to. That pause should land twice in this scene: before "She's dead, Tomlin" and before "You don't have a position here."

---

## Setting

Late afternoon. Grey light through a high narrow window. The room is cramped with shelved returns and mid-sorted materials — the work of someone who was pulled from the middle of something and hasn't come back. Tomlin sits at a small table, not working. He has been not working for some time. There is a stack of cataloguing slips in front of him that he has been moving from one side of the table to the other.

Roland enters. Tomlin startles, then recognizes him and is immediately less reassured.

---

## Script (Prose)

**TOMLIN**
*(standing quickly, knocking the cataloguing slips — gathering them with both hands)*
You shouldn't be — the Archive is under restricted access. I thought you knew that. Everyone knows that.

**ROLAND**
I know it.

**TOMLIN**
Then —

**ROLAND**
The clerk at the front desk has a copy of Fenwick's *Territorial Surveys*, third edition, on his reading stand. The real third edition, not the corrected reprint. He's been reading it since I came in. He didn't look up.

*(beat)*

He won't look up for another hour, probably.

**TOMLIN**
*(quietly)*
You shouldn't be here.

**ROLAND**
*(gently, not a challenge)*
Where are you staying? Since the suspension.

**TOMLIN**
*(startled)*
I — Maret's boarding house, on the Weavers' side. Why would that —

**ROLAND**
Are you going back tonight?

**TOMLIN**
I don't see what that —

**ROLAND**
Tomlin.

*(Tomlin stops. The name landed differently than he expected.)*

**ROLAND** *(cont.)*
Are you going back tonight?

**TOMLIN**
*(quietly, after a moment)*
I haven't decided.

**ROLAND**
*(nodding, as though this answers something)*
The restricted section. You have a copy of the access key.

**TOMLIN**
*(fast)*
I don't know who told you that, but I don't have — that key was reported in with the inventory when —

**ROLAND**
You cut a copy three years ago because Henrietta asked you to. In case she was ever incapacitated and a research request couldn't wait. You kept it because getting rid of it felt like getting rid of the reason she gave it to you.

*(Tomlin doesn't answer. He is very still.)*

**ROLAND** *(cont.)*
I know you haven't reported it because if you had, the investigation would have flagged it by now and you'd be in a different kind of trouble than a suspension.

**TOMLIN**
*(controlled — forcing himself to sound reasonable)*
Whatever you think you know about the restricted section — whatever you found in Henrietta's work — I don't think you understand what you're asking me to risk. My position here is —

**ROLAND**
*(quietly)*
You don't have a position here. You have a suspension and a boarding house room you haven't decided whether to go back to.

*(Tomlin flinches. Not from cruelty — from precision.)*

**ROLAND** *(cont.)*
I'm not saying that to diminish you. I'm saying it because I think you already know it, and I'd rather not waste your time pretending I don't.

**TOMLIN**
*(carefully)*
What do you think is in there?

**ROLAND**
A noble genealogy. Second Age lineage records. Something Henrietta was building toward — a connection she couldn't finish.

**TOMLIN**
*(footnotes returning, nervous)*
The restricted section has any number of records. Noble lineages, monastic charters, deed transfers from the pre-Consolidation period — which is to say, the category you're describing would encompass —

**ROLAND**
She's dead, Tomlin.

*(Silence. The footnotes stop.)*

**ROLAND** *(cont.)*
She made me a copy of her notes two nights ago. She pressed them into my coat at the Archive door. She already knew someone was watching. She didn't run. She made me a copy and she stood at the door until I was gone and then she went back inside.

*(Tomlin looks down at the cataloguing slips.)*

**ROLAND** *(cont.)*
I've been thinking about that. I think she knew what she was doing. I think she knew what it was going to cost. And I think the reason she made the copy instead of running is that she believed someone needed to finish what she'd started.

I'm trying to finish it.

*(Long pause. Tomlin does not look up.)*

**TOMLIN**
*(very quietly, to the table)*
She taught me to catalogue.

When I came here I didn't — I had a clerk's certification, which is to say I knew systems, but the things she knew about physical documents, about how to read age and condition and provenance from the material itself — she didn't have to teach me that. That wasn't my job. She taught me anyway because she said anyone working here ought to understand what they were actually handling.

She had strong opinions about that.

**ROLAND**
*(quietly)*
Yes. I imagine she did.

*(Tomlin looks up — directly at Roland for the first time. The footnotes are gone.)*

**TOMLIN**
If I give you the key. If. I don't — I want to be clear that I'm not —

I need you to tell me that what you find in there matters. Not as a promise. I know you can't promise that. I need you to tell me you believe it.

**ROLAND**
*(without hesitation)*
She's dead. Someone made sure she would be before she finished. Whatever is in that room, they already knew it was there. That's enough for me to believe it matters.

*(Pause. Tomlin reaches into his coat pocket. Sets a small iron key on the table.)*

**TOMLIN**
The lock is stiff. Lift the handle while you turn it. She showed me that.

*(He returns to the cataloguing slips. He does not watch Roland leave.)*

**ROLAND**
*(at the doorway)*
Go back to Maret's tonight.

*(No answer. Roland leaves. Tomlin remains. After a moment, he begins actually sorting the slips — not moving them from side to side, but reading them. One by one.)*

---

## Designer Notes

- The line "She's dead, Tomlin" is the dramatic pivot. Everything before it is Roland establishing he sees Tomlin clearly. Everything after is Tomlin choosing.
- "She had strong opinions about that" is the line where Tomlin commits without saying so. The grief is doing the work, not the argument.
- The closing beat (Tomlin sorting the slips one by one) is environmental — not in the TTS script, but worth a brief in-engine animation if budget permits.
