# Game Three: The Last Star — Aftermath and Epilogues

---

## The Descent

After the summit, the party descends the caldera. The battle below has shifted — Mordvar's dissolution severed the Ashen Hand's amplification completely. Without it, the mages who had been sustaining Vaeroth's forces have nothing. The Gulmrak Horde, whose religious devotion to the volcano was always Mordvar's influence wearing orc theology, fractures — Uglakh survives but is fighting his own subordinate commanders by the time the Alliance's surviving forces reach the caldera's base.

**The count, at dusk of Day Three:**

Alliance losses vary based on which factions committed. At maximum Alliance:
- Iron Chalice: 112 survivors of 1,200 — they spent themselves escorting Roland's party to the caldera
- Tidewarden: 340 of 900 naval infantry who made landfall
- Frost Brotherhood: 89 of approximately 400 (the Coldstoke rearguard cost them most)
- Kazaad-Brak: Hagrim is alive; roughly a third of his force
- Khorumzad: Darna's command fought well; she survives
- Vosskara: Yaromir's full force arrived; held the supply lines; survived mostly intact
- Conclave mages: 23 of 41 (the engagement with the Ashen Hand was the most costly fight mage-for-mage)

The governing theme of the trilogy: bonds between people are more durable than institutions, and more powerful than any single act of heroism. The counts reflect this — the best-equipped, most prestigious forces took the highest casualties, and the volunteer fighters and frontier soldiers who came because of personal commitment to Roland or to each other performed beyond what their numbers should allow.

## Epilogues (Both Endings)

### The Exchange — Epilogue

**Aldric Vane** returns to Coldstoke. He is received with no ceremony because he told no one he was coming. His forge is cold. He restarts it. He is at work by the next morning.

He is invited to courts, offered titles, asked to speak at celebrations he did not attend. He declines most of them. He accepts one: an invitation from Dame Calla Vane to attend the Iron Chalice's annual Holding Day commemoration — the ceremony where the Order commemorates the times it held, not the times it won. He does not speak at the ceremony. He stands in the back with the enlisted soldiers.

**Roland Ashford** is offered reinstatement to the Iron Chalice with full honors. He declines. He has been expelled from an institution he believed in and rebuilt what he valued about it piece by piece outside its walls. The institution is not the thing. The thing is the thing.

He returns to Aldenholt and begins work with Tomlin (the assistant archivist) to rebuild the Archive's research into the Second Age binding records — not as a project with an endpoint, but as a permanent record so no Henrietta needs to compile it again alone.

**Corvus Tane** returns to the Conclave. His permanent alteration — changed eyes, accelerated aging — is noted but not remarked upon. He identifies the two Ashfallen members that the Conclave did not know about. He does not explain how he became certain. He does not rejoin the senior instruction track. He asks to teach the first-year students, who are the most likely to destroy something by accident and the most likely to benefit from someone who has already made the costly choices.

**Seren of the Third Glade** completes the pilgrimage to all Eight Glades — the first full circuit since the Eighth Star fell. At the Eighth Glade, which has been tended in anticipation for two thousand years with no star, she places the message that was delivered to her from the Nameless Council operative, having carried it since the Copper Isles muster. She is not there when the glade brightens for the first time in recorded history. She is already on the path to the next glade.

**Orion Farr** is offered his own ship by Queen Eilwen. He accepts. His first voyage is not a mission — it is the Shroud Sea window, now nine years open. He sails east, past the storm-wall, into whatever was on the other side. He sends one message back through the Brotherhood network before the window closes: *Ruins. Extensive. Unmistakably Second Age. This will take years.*

The Brotherhood archive is updated. The question of the Aescryd — Mordvar's sunken kingdom — becomes the next generation's project.

### The Tether — Epilogue

Most epilogue elements are the same. The differences:

**Aldric Vane** does not return to Coldstoke permanently. He stays close to the Aescstól — not inside Drûn-Khazad, but in the Thal frontier, where he can feel what he carries and knows he is its anchor. He is not imprisoned. He chose this. He builds a forge in the settlement that grows up in the Thal western shore — the Alliance's logistics base, which becomes a permanent outpost rather than a temporary camp.

He has a son, eventually. The son is told what he carries, when he is old enough to understand it. The conversation is in the Tether epilogue's final scene. The son asks: *Is it heavy?*

Aldric says: *Yes. But you were born knowing how to carry it. That is what the bloodline means.*

**Seren** visits Aldric regularly for the rest of his life, which is longer than most human lives — the Tether's anchoring has a side effect no one anticipated. She says, one visit: *You chose to stay. Like the Star.* He says: *You said that at the summit.* She says: *Yes. I wanted to see if it still felt true.* He says: *Does it?* She says: *Yes.*

## Final Reflection

The world does not transform. The age does not end. The Third Age continues — the Long Twilight that the Aelorin loremasters named it, which is both the age of humanity's greatest expansion and its most existential threat. Men are still numerous, cities still grow, trade still flows.

The Aelorin still dwindle. The dwarves grow somewhat less inward, with Bromrin's slow recovery serving as an argument that the Ashen Hand's manipulation is beatable. Thrarin's daughter Darva manages the transition to Karaz-Dûn's new reality with more grace than her father managed before it. Hagrim Bloodbeard lives four more years and names one of the contesting cousin-clans as his heir, to the other cousin-clan's outrage and everyone else's relief.

The world is not saved in the sense of being made invulnerable. It is saved in the sense of having been given more time, by people who chose to spend themselves buying it.

The Aelorin word for the Long Twilight is also, in an older usage, the word for the hour before dawn.
