# Backstory: The Seven Dawnbringers

## What the Dawnbringers Are

The Dawnbringers are not an army or a formal order. They are seven individuals — and their various contacts and assets — who share two things: knowledge that the Aeluvain binding is failing, and a commitment to doing something about it before the Conclave or any of the four kingdoms decides to act.

They were convened by Aelthurion roughly sixty years before Game 1, after the binding's deterioration crossed a threshold he had been monitoring for three centuries. He did not recruit warriors. He recruited people with specific useful knowledge or access, and asked them to begin positioning themselves.

They operate without central coordination in the day-to-day sense. Aelthurion receives reports; he does not issue orders. Each Dawnbringer works within their own domain, exercises their own judgment, and is trusted to recognize when something requires the others' attention. This structure is deliberate — a cell structure compromised can only lose what it knows, and no single Dawnbringer knows everything.

---

## Verath

Verath is the Dawnbringers' intelligence specialist — specifically, their specialist on the Hollow Court's operations. He is a human man in his late fifties, former royal intelligence service in one of the middle kingdoms, who has spent thirty years building a picture of the Hollow Court's structure from the outside.

His most significant contribution has been the caldera reconnaissance: three separate observation operations near Drun-Khazad's accessible edges that have produced the only reliable maps of the Hollow Court's Thal installations. He has never crossed the Shroud Sea himself. He has sent eight people who were willing to go. Four came back. He keeps their debrief records in an archive that he has prepared instructions to destroy if he dies before the party reaches it.

He is not comfortable with what he asks of people. He does it anyway, and he does not pretend the discomfort absolves him.

---

## Caela

Caela is the Dawnbringers' expert on Thal's interior — the only one of the seven who has spent significant time on the eastern continent. She is a human woman in her late forties who spent twelve years with a geographic survey company before the company's Thal expedition was ambushed by Hollow Court forces and she was the only member to escape.

She reached the coast on foot over six weeks, having memorized everything she'd observed before the ambush. Verath found her debriefing report through an intelligence contact and brought her in. She has been the Dawnbringers' primary source on Thal's interior geography for fifteen years.

Her ambush warnings — specific knowledge of how the Hollow Court positions interdiction forces near the caldera — are the reason the party's Game 3 approach has a viable route at all.

---

## Lyren

Lyren is a half-elf scholar who has spent forty years analyzing the Hollow Court's magical methodology from the Conclave's own suppressed records. She holds a senior position in the Conclave under a false research brief that has never been scrutinized because she publishes enough legitimate work to maintain cover.

Her role is twofold: she monitors Conclave internal communications for signs that the Hollow Court has placed assets inside the institution (she has identified three so far, two of which were Vaeroth's placements), and she maintains a line of communication between the Dawnbringers and the two Conclave senior figures she believes can be trusted in a crisis.

She has never met Corvus, but she knows his resonance signature from the monitoring records. When they eventually meet she will have a great deal to tell him about his own capabilities that he does not yet know.

---

## Sorin

Sorin is human, in his early sixties, and has spent twenty years embedded in the communities of the Naergrim's human-integrated settlements — the fringe populations near the eastern forests who have intermarried with Naergrim over generations and occupy an ambiguous middle ground that neither the kingdoms nor the Naergrim fully claim.

His work is relationship-based: he knows which Naergrim clan leaders are genuinely committed to their isolation and which might, under the right circumstances, consider communication with the outside world. He also monitors Naergrim dissident activity — specifically the small factions who believe the ancient bargain with the entity should be ended but cannot move against the clan consensus without outside support.

His intelligence on Naergrim internal politics is the primary reason the party has any possibility of reaching Serethi-Twice-Dead in Game 3.

---

## Aevril, the Twice-Named

Aevril has two names because she earned a second one. Born simply Aevril, she became Aevril-Twice-Named after the operation in the Weeping Wood where she broke the Hollow Court's center column during their attempted advance into the Borderlands thirteen years before Game 1.

She is a combat operative — the Dawnbringers' only one — and she does not do the subtle work that the others do. She goes to places where something needs to be physically stopped and she stops it. She has been wounded seriously twice and has not slowed down, which her colleagues find equal parts admirable and alarming.

She is in her late thirties. She is not reckless — she plans carefully and executes with discipline. But she has a very clear read on the math of the situation: if the binding fails completely, the number of people who die is not recoverable. Against that number, the acceptable cost of intervention is large. She makes peace with this arithmetic so the others don't have to.

---

## Maeva

Maeva is the Dawnbringers' naval and weather specialist, a woman in her early fifties who spent her first career as a maritime navigator before joining a research consortium studying the Shroud Sea's weather patterns.

She co-developed the window theory — the systematic account of the Shroud Sea's forty-year navigable periods — with a navigator named Oran Saltfish, who died of old age three years after they completed the paper. The theory was based on forty years of Brotherhood records, two of Maeva's own crossings of the sea's outer edges, and Oran's analysis of atmospheric data going back a century.

Her specific contribution to the party's mission is practical: she knows the current window's weather patterns, the viable approach corridors, and the specific hazards of the Shroud Sea crossing that the Brotherhood's general charts do not include because they were observed only by people who crossed under duress and did not write things down until much later.

She is the one who first told Eilwen, thirty years ago, that the Shroud Sea was crossable. She does not entirely forgive herself for what Eilwen has spent thirty years doing with that information.

---

## The Seventh

The seventh Dawnbringer's identity is not documented in any record Aelthurion keeps. This is intentional. The seventh works inside one of the four human kingdoms at a level of access that would be immediately compromised by documentation.

The party will not know the seventh's identity until late in Game 2. The reveal will recontextualize several things they believed they understood about how the kingdoms' politics have developed.
