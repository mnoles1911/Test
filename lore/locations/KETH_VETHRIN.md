# Keth-Vethrin

**Type:** City (outpost / fortified port)
**Kingdom/People:** Naergrim
**Position:** Western volcanic coast of Thal, above the rocky shoreline
**Story relevance:** Not visited in Game One. Game Two territory — the Shroud Sea crossing lands near here.

---

## Physical Description

Built into dark volcanic cliff-faces above the rocky grey-black shoreline of Thal's western coast. Smaller and more militarized than Mor-Vethrin — Keth-Vethrin is not a capital or a home; it is a watchtower. The Naergrim who garrison it rotate from Mor-Vethrin. No one is assigned here permanently if it can be avoided.

The name means "the sea-watching city" in old Naergrim tongue. It faces west across the Shroud Sea toward Mira — the only direction that matters to its purpose. It is the listening post through which Mor-Vethrin monitors events on the other continent, and the point of contact between the Naergrim of Mira and Mordvar's Ash Throne on Thal.

The architecture follows the same principles as Mor-Vethrin: vertical cliff-face construction, no wasted material, no decorative elements, narrow passages, no windows facing the interior of Thal. All openings face west toward the sea. A bone arch gate — smaller than Mor-Vethrin's — marks the single entry point from the rocky approach below the cliff. The volcanic rock here is genuine, unlike Mor-Vethrin's sedimentary escarpment; the construction material is local dark basalt.

Unlike Mor-Vethrin, Keth-Vethrin has no deep-rock cold problem: the volcanic coast provides ambient heat through the rock itself. This makes it more physically tolerable than Mor-Vethrin, which the garrison considers a small mercy.

There is a rough landing approach — not a harbor, as no natural bay exists here. Ships must anchor offshore and approach by small boat. The Aescryd ruins found forty years ago by Sailor's Guild explorers are on a flat terrace nearby, half-buried in volcanic aggregate above the tide line. The Naergrim are aware of the ruins and have left them alone; old things belong where they are placed.

Map note: Dark cliff-face fortress symbol, sea-facing, western Thal coast. No harbor symbol — anchorage only.

## Art Direction Notes

Similar palette to Mor-Vethrin but with volcanic elements: dark basalt rock with occasional volcanic heat shimmer, the grey-black rocky shoreline below, and the permanent storm-grey of the Shroud Sea to the west.

The key difference from Mor-Vethrin: Keth-Vethrin has light. Not warm light — the volcanic rock glows faintly in deep cracks, and the sea-facing openings bring grey storm light from the west. It is less mysterious than Mor-Vethrin and more obviously hostile. Mor-Vethrin feels like it is waiting. Keth-Vethrin feels like it is watching.

The Shroud Sea visible from the western openings: permanent storm-grey, waves visible in the distance, the sky and sea blending at the horizon. No other land visible — Mira is 900 miles west and the Shroud is opaque.

Art design for Keth-Vethrin can be deferred until Game Two pre-production.
