# The Sorrowmarsh

**Type:** Dead wetland (ancient battle site)
**Kingdom/People:** No control — unclaimed borderland, eastern Mira
**Position:** Eastern Mira, between the Ashfields and the Weeping Wood's southern edge
**Story relevance:** Background lore throughout the trilogy; the site where Mordvar was unmade two thousand years ago; the location Caerith/the Ashlord guarded for fifteen hundred years

---

## Physical Description

A dead wetland roughly twelve kilometers across at its widest. Nothing grows. The water in the channels does not move. The mud at the surface is a deep grey-black and smells of old blood in warm weather. Ghost-lights appear at night — pale blue-white, drifting at knee-height, vanishing if approached. They do not follow people. They do not threaten. They simply persist.

The Sorrowmarsh has looked this way for two thousand years. The battle that ended the Second Age's final war was fought here — the Grand Alliance's forces pushed Mordvar's army south and east through three weeks of grinding advance before the final confrontation at the marsh's center. Aelric Caelborn wielded the Aeluvain here. The Crown shattered here. Whatever was in Mordvar dispersed into the ground and the water and the air of this place, and the marsh has been wrong ever since.

The wrongness is not dangerous in the direct sense — there are no monsters, no active threat. It is the ambient wrongness of a place where something cosmologically significant happened and left a residue. The Dragon-Watchers' records describe it as "a location where the grain of the world runs backward for a short distance, then stops." They do not explain what this means in terms that non-dwarves find useful.

## 3D Scale Reference
> At 10 voxels per meter (project standard: 1 voxel = 0.1 m)

Key dimensions:

- Total playable footprint (if visited): suggest a representative section ~200 m × 150 m = 2000 × 1500 voxels; the full marsh is much larger but a scene uses a slice
- Channel width: dead waterways, ~3–6 m wide = 30–60 voxels; knee-to-waist depth
- Open ground between channels: low hummocks of dead grass and grey mud, ~10–20 m between waterways = 100–200 voxels
- Ghost-lights: particle effects at ~1–1.5 m elevation, pale blue-white, low energy — OmniLight3D at very low intensity, radius 2–3 m, color `#B8D4FF`
- Visibility: low ground fog in morning and evening; permanent overcast sky (lighter than the Weeping Wood's cloud cover — grey-white rather than dark grey)
- Camera arm length: arm 12–14, elevation 45° (flat terrain, wide sightlines, fog adds depth)

## Art Direction Notes

The Sorrowmarsh is a place of aftermath, not active horror. It should feel exhausted rather than threatening. Everything is the same color — grey-black mud, grey-brown dead grass, grey-white sky, grey water in the channels. The only color is the ghost-lights: pale blue-white, the same color as the Aeluvain's blade. This is not a coincidence.

The smell of old blood: do not represent this visually, but mention it in dialogue or journal entries when the party first arrives.

The ghost-lights should not animate aggressively. They drift slowly at knee-height and vanish when the player gets within a few meters. They are not threatening. They are the opposite — they feel like something trying to remember something. The effect should be melancholy, not frightening.

No music in the Sorrowmarsh, or music that fades to near-silence with only low ambient tones. The silence is part of the description.

## Story Role

The Sorrowmarsh is where Caerith maintained his vigil for fifteen hundred years before abandoning his post. A Vigil-Keeper's marker — a specific kind of Aelorin carved stone, used to mark a duty-post — still stands at the marsh's center. Seren recognizes it on sight. The date inscribed is roughly fifteen hundred years ago. Caerith left it standing when he walked away.

The party may or may not pass through the Sorrowmarsh depending on route choices. If they do, this is where the breadcrumb about the Vigil-Keeper tradition can be delivered — before Caerith's identity is known. Seren's recognition of the marker, and her refusal to explain it until she is certain, is a Game One or Two beat depending on routing.

The marker also tells the party something they may not have known: someone was watching this site. Was watching. Past tense.
