## Physical Description

The Copper Shelf is a wide, shallow underwater apron extending south from Mira's southern coast. The Copper Isles sit on this shelf — close enough to Mira to be reached easily by small boat, far enough to maintain political independence in practice. The shelf makes the southern coast poor for deep-draft shipping but excellent for small-boat trade and fishing.

The archipelago takes its name from the rust-green copper seams that streak the western sea-cliffs of the main island. The seams were never worth industrial-scale extraction — too thin, too scattered, too far from any smelting operation that matters — but they have been pried out by hand for two thousand years. Every roof tile, kettle, and door-fitting on the islands has at least a trace of local copper in it. The cliffs on the windward side glow a peculiar oxidized green-blue at sundown, which is what most travelers remember.

The water between the islands is the second thing they remember. Inside the inner archipelago the sea is a clear pale aquamarine over white sand, two to eight meters deep, warm enough in summer to swim in. The deep channel that runs between the main island and the Archive Isle is darker — twelve to eighteen meters — and the working shipping route. South of the outer islands the shelf edge falls away to thirty-plus meters and the water turns proper open-ocean blue.

From `CITY_DESCRIPTIONS.md`: "Surgeons' School, Solgrade: Accepts students from all four kingdoms and the Copper Isles. Aelorin healers have occasionally lectured here." — Copper Isles has a medical/academic relationship with Solgrade.

## The Islands

The archipelago has four named islands, two named rock-stacks, and roughly a dozen unnamed islets, shoals, and bird-rocks. The whole chain fits inside a 3 km × 3 km box and is easily walked or rowed end-to-end in an afternoon.

### Main Island ("Copper Isles Port", or just "the Port")

Footprint roughly **1,100 m east-west × 800 m north-south**. The largest landmass in the archipelago and the only one with permanent population beyond a handful of caretakers.

- **Western cliffs (windward):** 60–90 m sheer rock face running the island's full west side. This is where the copper seams are. The cliffs catch the prevailing southwesterly wind and the constant Atlantic-grade swell. Sea-birds nest in the upper third. Climbable in two or three known places, suicidal everywhere else.
- **Central spine:** A low ridge running roughly north-south at ~70 m elevation, cresting at **Watcher's Hill (85 m)** roughly two-thirds of the way down. The hilltop has a stone watch-cairn predating the current Port by several hundred years; nobody knows who built it. From the cairn you can see every other island in the archipelago and, on a clear day, the dark line of Mira's coast to the north.
- **Eastern terraces:** The lee side of the spine drops in three or four broad agricultural terraces — vineyards, olive trees, small grain plots — to a flat coastal apron at ~10 m above sea level. The terraces are the population's food supply and are old enough that nobody remembers cutting them.
- **The Port (Copper Isles Port):** A sheltered south-facing crescent harbor bitten into the southeast corner of the island. The harbor mouth is roughly 250 m wide; the water inside is 4–10 m deep. The town wraps the harbor in two crescent terraces — wharf level at sea, market level ~8 m above. Buildings are low-slung pale stone with copper-trimmed shutters and clay-tile roofs. Population maybe 1,500. The Sailor's Guild Hall sits on the north side of the harbor, a square three-story building with a slate roof.
- **The Salt Road ferry mole:** A long stone breakwater extending south-southeast from the Port, used by the small ferries that connect to the Salt Road's southern terminus on the Mira mainland. The mole is roughly 180 m long.
- **South-facing beaches:** Two narrow sand beaches east and west of the Port, used for fishing-boat haul-out.

### Archive Isle (officially "Maesha's Reach", but nobody calls it that)

Footprint roughly **600 m × 400 m**, sitting north-northwest of the Main Island, separated by the **Deep Channel** (12–18 m deep, ~500 m wide). The smaller of the two large islands.

- **Topography:** Lower and flatter than the Main Island. Maximum elevation ~45 m at a single rounded hill near the island's center. The west side has a 30 m sea-cliff; the east, north, and south coasts are gentler — pebble beaches and low rocky points.
- **The Sailor's Guild Archive:** A four-story windowless stone building set on the western cliff-top, deliberately positioned to be the first thing any approaching ship sees. The building is ~30 m × 40 m at its base, with a pyramid-stub roof and a single sea-side door at second-floor level reached by an exterior stone stair. The architecture is older than the Guild — the building was a kingdom-era lighthouse before the Guild bought the entire island roughly 170 years ago and converted it.
- **Archive grounds:** The rest of the island is wooded — wind-bent salt-pines, hawthorn, a scatter of stone outbuildings (archivist quarters, document-repair workshop, the records overflow annex). A single gravel path connects the cliff-top archive to a small stone jetty on the east shore where boats from the Main Island land. Total Guild population on the island is ~25 — archivists, copyists, two rotating Guild members on quiet-duty, the cook.
- **The wood:** Locals don't go there. The Guild doesn't forbid it, but it's understood. The island reads as private property even though no fence says so.

### Lantern Rock

A single sea-stack on the **southeast edge** of the archipelago, roughly **150 m × 100 m at the waterline**, rising to a sharp **55 m pinnacle**. The Sailor's Guild maintains a stone lighthouse on the summit — a squat round tower with a copper-shuttered lantern room. The light is lit from dusk to dawn year-round and is the visual anchor for any ship approaching the Port from the south or east. Two keepers, six-month rotation, hate each other by month four every time.

### Cradle Isle (the quiet-harbor safe-house — "Cradle" to Guild members, unmarked on civilian charts)

Footprint roughly **350 m × 250 m**, in the **southwest corner of the archipelago**. The lowest of the named islands — maximum elevation ~25 m — and the most overlooked, which is the point.

- **The hidden lagoon:** A roughly 80 m × 60 m natural cove bitten into the south face of the island. The cove's mouth is masked by two rock spurs that overlap from a south-approach view; you only see the cove exists once you're inside ten meters of the spur. Water inside is calm and 4–6 m deep — enough for a small craft to anchor.
- **The safe-house:** A single low stone building set thirty meters back from the lagoon shore, mostly buried in the hillside on its landward side. Roof is sod over slate; chimney exits sideways through a hill-face crack so smoke disperses before it ever rises high enough to read against the sky. Inside: bunks for fourteen, a hearth, a pump-well, a stocked larder kept rotated by the Caer Brannoch steward's office, and the **lower cellar** — accessed by a trapdoor under the larder, where the Hand agent's prepared chest is found in the Game One Act III voyage.
- **The wider island:** Salt-grass and stunted juniper. No permanent population. A shepherd from the Main Island lands twice a year to check the cistern and take stock of the sheep that have, over generations, become semi-wild on the upper meadow.

### The Hollow Sisters

Twin islets at the **northeast corner** of the archipelago, ~200 m × 150 m each, separated by a narrow ~40 m channel. Both are jagged and uninhabited — vertical sea-cliffs on three sides, a single shallow saddle on the fourth where a dinghy can land. Bird-rocks. The Sisters are the navigational landmark every approaching captain looks for; lining the Sisters up with the Lantern Rock astern means you're in the Deep Channel and not about to ground on the central shoal.

### Unnamed islets, shoals, and bird-rocks

Roughly twelve to fifteen smaller features scattered across the shelf:

- **The Boneyard:** A field of low rock spires in the central-west of the archipelago, breaking the water at low tide, fully submerged at high. Has eaten three ships in living memory.
- **The Cup:** A circular reef enclosing a tidepool roughly 80 m across, on the east shelf. Locals fish it.
- **Two or three sandbar islets** that change shape every winter storm — sometimes two of them, sometimes one long one, sometimes none if the year's storms have been brutal.
- A scatter of bare granite stacks 5–15 m above water, used by sea-birds and nothing else.

## Bathymetry (the water between)

- **Inner shelf (most of the playable area):** 2–8 m depth, white sand bottom, clear water. This is where small boats work.
- **The Deep Channel** (between Main Island and Archive Isle): 12–18 m, the only route deep enough for the Sailor's Guild's larger trading sloops.
- **The Approach Channel** (south of the Main Island, between Lantern Rock and Cradle Isle): 6–10 m, the standard ferry route in.
- **The Boneyard shoal:** 0–2 m at low tide, charted carefully, marked by two iron pole-buoys.
- **Outer shelf edge** (south map edge): 25–40 m, where the Copper Shelf falls off into proper deep water.
- **North map edge (toward Mira mainland, off-map):** Stays shallow, 4–10 m, the shelf continuing toward the Mira coast.

## Climate, Wind, Weather

- **Latitude:** Southern temperate. Warmer than Caer Brannoch year-round, occasionally hot in late summer, never genuinely cold in winter.
- **Prevailing wind:** Southwesterly, almost constant. The west coast of every island is the windward face — taller cliffs, salt-stunted vegetation, copper-stained rock. The east and southeast coasts are sheltered, which is why every harbor and beach in the archipelago is on a south-facing or east-facing shore.
- **Weather profile** (ties to the `WeatherManager` system in `design/WEATHER_AND_ENVIRONMENT.md`): Light overcast is the default. Clear sun roughly 30% of days. Light rain frequent but short — 10–30 minute cells that pass through. Heavy rain rare except in autumn squalls. Fog common at dawn over the inner shelf, burns off by mid-morning. Snow effectively never. The archipelago's `WeatherLocationProfile.tres` should sit between `mira_temperate.tres` and a future `southern_coastal.tres`.
- **Tides:** ~1.5 m range. Visually significant only at the Boneyard (which appears and disappears) and at the Cradle Isle lagoon mouth (which is two meters tighter at high tide).

## Cultural Identity

Briefly, because the demo level needs the texture even if the deeper content is a Game Two job:

- **Independent.** Not part of any human kingdom. Governed by a small elected council of harbor-masters, the Guild steward, and the senior surgeon at the Port's hospital wing. Decisions are local, slow, and stubborn.
- **Maritime trade hub.** Every kingdom's small-boat traffic lands here. The market square at the Port has, on any given week, Caer Brannoch fishmongers, Solgrade scholars in transit, two Aelorin sailors who've been "passing through" for thirty years, dwarves from Karaz-Dûn buying olive oil, and at least one Vosskaran exile trying to look like he's not one.
- **Scholarly.** The medical relationship with Solgrade's Surgeons' School means every Copper Isles–born physician trains there. The Sailor's Guild Archive is the largest library of maritime knowledge on the planet. The islands punch far above their weight in literacy.
- **Architecture:** Pale stone (local limestone, quarried from the eastern face of the Main Island), copper-trimmed shutters and door-fittings, clay-tile roofs in muted reds and ochres. Buildings hug the ground — nothing taller than three stories on the Main Island except the Archive itself.

## Art Direction Notes

**Palette:**
- **Sea (inner shelf):** Pale aquamarine to turquoise (`#7FD9C8` to `#3E9FA8`) over sand. Use the existing `assets/shaders/water.gdshader` with shifted base hue for the inner archipelago; the outer water uses the standard deep-blue.
- **Sea (outer shelf, south edge):** Standard deep blue (`#1E3A5F` to `#0E1F38`).
- **Beaches:** Pale silver-cream sand (`#E8DFC4`).
- **Cliffs (windward, copper-streaked):** Warm rust-grey base (`#8A7560`) with vertical streaks of oxidized copper green-blue (`#3E8C7A` to `#5FA89A`). The streaks are visually striking and are the defining art-direction beat for the archipelago.
- **Cliffs (leeward, plain stone):** Warm pale limestone (`#C8B89C`).
- **Inland vegetation:** Olive-green and sage (`#7A8B5A`, `#9CA88C`) for the terraces and salt-grass; dark salt-pine green (`#3E5240`) for the Archive Isle wood.
- **Roof tiles:** Muted reds and ochres (`#A85838`, `#C68A4E`).
- **Architecture stone:** Pale warm limestone (`#D8CDB0`).

**Lighting:** Strong directional southern sun. Long shadows from the western cliffs in early morning, from the spine in late afternoon. The Archive Isle wood is markedly cooler-toned than anywhere else — deliberate, the island reads as private and slightly forbidding.

**Visual focal beats** (what the player should remember):
1. The copper-streaked western cliffs of the Main Island, glowing green-blue at sunset
2. The Sailor's Guild Archive standing alone on Archive Isle's western cliff, silhouette-first as the ship approaches
3. The Lantern Rock pinnacle with its small lighthouse, steady through every weather
4. The Cradle Isle hidden lagoon — invisible until you're inside the spurs, then suddenly enclosed
5. Watcher's Hill cairn at sunrise, the whole archipelago laid out below
