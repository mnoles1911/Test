# The Sundered Isles

**Type:** Archipelago (drowned civilization ruins)
**Kingdom/People:** No formal control — pirates in the outer islands; Ashen Hand controls the deep interior, specifically the Ash Tower
**Position:** Middle-north of the Shroud Sea, between Mira and Thal
**Story relevance:** Game Three — the Ash Tower, where Hadran Volthen maintains his forge and the party confronts him; accessed via Sailor's Guild storm-window routing

---

## Physical Description

What the Sundered Isles are: the ruins of Aescryd, the first human kingdom, which drowned in the generation after the Sundered Crown shattered at the Sorrowmarsh two thousand years ago. The island did not sink cleanly. It broke apart as it went — the upper floors of towers and citadels are still above water, tilted at angles, rising from channels where the sea runs fast and cold. Below the waterline: streets, plazas, archives, workshops, the accumulated infrastructure of the world's first human civilization, preserved in cold dark water.

The archipelago stretches roughly forty kilometers across. The outer islands are accessible from the Shroud Sea's storm-edge — pirates use the anchorages there, running on the knowledge that no one with formal authority can easily follow them into the outer channels. The inner islands require navigating channels that run between half-submerged towers; at the wrong tide, the channels fill and the route closes.

The water in the inner channels runs deep-green, almost black in shade. Sea creatures the Sailor's Guild has no formal classification for inhabit the drowned sections. Sailors who have entered the inner islands describe the sound of the water against the old stone as wrong — not like normal water against normal stone. As if the stone is absorbing the sound rather than reflecting it.

## The Ash Tower

The largest surviving structure in the Sundered Isles — the remnant of the Aescryd capital's central citadel. The lower three floors are flooded. The upper four are dry and occupied.

**Access:** Small craft at slack tide through a sea-cave below the Tower's eastern face, which opens into a flooded inner courtyard. Iron rungs in the courtyard wall lead up to the fourth floor's stone doorway.

**Floor by floor:**

**4th floor — Hadran's Forge.** The forge proper: a workshop built around a central anvil that dominates the room, its face bearing seven shaped recesses — the sockets for the Sundered Crown's seven pieces. The forge burns with a black-orange flame fed by Drûn-Khazad charcoal. Tools, reference documents, and Hadran's notes cover every available surface. The sea-petrel in its wicker cage on the workbench. This is where Hadran has worked for ninety years.

**5th floor — barracks.** Quarters for roughly eighty Hand members: former Conclave mages, second sons of minor noble houses, two Aelorin who followed the Ashlord's ideology to its conclusion. They eat together, train on the beach below at low tide, and know exactly what they serve.

**6th floor — war room.** Maps of Mira and Thal on every wall: Hand cell locations, Crown piece positions, Sailor's Guild shipping lanes, Naergrim territory boundaries. The Ashlord's planning table. This is where operations were directed.

**7th floor — the Ashlord's quarters.** Spare. A single chair angled toward the west-facing window. The window faces Mira, across the Shroud Sea. Long hours were spent here. What was thought is not recorded.

**Roof — signal pyre and watch-post.** A pyre for signaling the black-sailed Naergrim fleet in the south cove. Black-sailed ships arrive at intervals.

## 3D Scale Reference
> At 8 voxels per meter (project standard: 1 voxel = 0.125 m)

Key dimensions:

- Ash Tower footprint: roughly 25 m × 25 m = 200 × 200 voxels per floor
- Floor height: ~4 m = 32 voxels (tall ceilings — this was a citadel, built for presence)
- Total tower height (floors 4–7 + roof): ~20 m above the waterline = 160 voxels exposed; 12 m below waterline (floors 1–3) = 96 voxels submerged
- The anvil on floor 4: dominant feature, ~1.2 m tall × 0.8 m wide = 10 × 6 voxels
- Sea-cave approach channel: ~6 m wide × 3 m tall at high clearance point = 48 × 24 voxels; tighter at the inner courtyard entry
- Inner courtyard (flooded): ~15 m × 15 m open water = 120 × 120 voxels; iron rungs on the north wall
- Camera arm: forge (floor 4) arm 10–12, elevation 50° (enclosed workshop); war room (floor 6) arm 10, elevation 45°; approach channel arm 8, elevation 35° (tight, claustrophobic)

The outer island channels: wide enough for a small craft, lined on both sides by half-submerged tower sections. Camera arm 12–14 during navigation approach, pulling back to show the towers flanking the channel.

## Art Direction Notes

**Palette:** Dark Aescryd granite — blue-grey stone, darker than the dwarven holds, with a quality that does not soften in saltwater. The forge's black-orange flame is the only warm color in the Tower. Outside, the channel water is deep-green-black. The sky over the inner islands tends toward overcast grey — the Shroud Sea weather system affects even the calmer inner channels.

**The forge specifically:** Black-orange flame (color `#E05010` to `#FF8030`) in a forge that is functional and old and very carefully maintained. The anvil with its seven sockets is the visual focal point. The sockets should be obviously shaped — not holes, but specific recesses, each a different geometry.

**The drowned lower floors:** Not visited in gameplay but visually present — through the courtyard water, the flooded second floor's arched ceiling is visible below the surface. Cold, dark, and deep.

**The Tower's position in the outer channels:** The approach through the channel should make the Tower visible from a distance first as a dark silhouette against the grey sky, growing as the craft approaches. It is not a ruin — it is maintained, inhabited, and purposeful. The wrongness is not decay but the specific wrongness of something very old still being used for something it was never meant for.
