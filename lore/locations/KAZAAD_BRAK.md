# Kazaad-Brak

**Type:** Dwarven Hold
**Kingdom/People:** Southern dwarves (most militarily experienced in the Spine)
**Position:** Below the Broken Fang, southern Spine of the World
**Story relevance:** Game One, Act III — mid-point of the Karaz-Dûn arc; Barak Stonecroft scene

---

## Physical Description

Besieged eleven times. More than any other hold. The record is a point of identity — Kazaad-Brak dwarves are the most militarily experienced in the Spine, and they do not let anyone forget it. The hold's architecture reflects this: every corridor has a defensible choke point, every level has independent water and food storage, every gate has three backup mechanisms.

The Broken Fang above — twin peaks split by an ancient fault — is a lightning magnet in storm season. The dwarves have built lightning-conductors into the Fang's approaches and sell the captured charge as a curiosity to human traders.

Map note: Mountain-gate icon below twin-peaked symbol.

## 3D Scale Reference
> At 10 voxels per meter (project standard: 1 voxel = 0.1 m)

Key dimensions:

- [x] Defensive choke points: narrow corridor pinch-points — ~1.5–2 m wide = 15–20 voxels at narrowest (deliberately tight for one-at-a-time combat)
- [x] Standard corridor width: ~3–4 m = 30–40 voxels (wider than choke points but narrower than trade-hold equivalents — efficiency, not hospitality)
- [x] Main gate: three backup mechanisms implies triple-gate structure — each gate ~3 m wide × 3.5 m tall = 30 × 35 voxels; spacing between gates ~4 m = 40 voxels
- [x] Independent storage per level: water cisterns and food stores built into each level — structural feature, not decoration
- [x] Lightning conductor infrastructure: exterior, above-ground; conductor towers visible from outside — ~10–15 m tall = 100–150 voxels (on the approach path to the Broken Fang)
- [ ] Barak Stonecroft's isolation area: not imprisoned, but separated — private quarters or guarded wing; GAP for dimensions
- [x] Camera arm length: interior corridors = arm 10, elevation 50°, no horizontal rotation; choke-point corridors = arm 8, elevation 52° (tight spaces)

Eleven sieges means the architecture has been rebuilt, reinforced, and modified repeatedly. The hold should feel layered — older sections visible in the stonework, newer reinforcements added over them.

## Art Direction Notes

Where Karaz-Dûn reads as warm and industrial (forge glow), Kazaad-Brak reads as cool and military. No forge warmth here — the southern hold's industry is weapons and siege materials, not instruments and trade goods. Torchlight is functional rather than ambient.

The Broken Fang above: twin peaks split by an ancient fault. Lightning conductors visible on the mountainside. In storm season (if visited then), the lightning strikes on the Fang would be visible from inside through any exterior-facing window or gate opening.

The eleven-siege identity: ambient NPCs here reference sieges. The walls themselves have battle damage that has been repaired and patched but not cosmetically smoothed. The history is in the stone.
