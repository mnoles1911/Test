# World Geography — Mira-Thal

Detailed terrain, scale, and physical geography reference. Companion to WORLD.md.

---

## Continental Layout and Scale

Mira and Thal are separated by the Shroud Sea. At its narrowest the crossing is approximately 900 miles. The two continents sit on a roughly east-west axis — Mira to the west, Thal to the east.

**Mira** is the larger continent: approximately 1,500 miles east-to-west at its widest and 1,200 miles north-to-south. It is broadest in its middle latitudes, tapering toward a rugged northern coast and a gentler southern one. The western face is dramatic sea-cliff coastline. The eastern face transitions into ash-waste frontier.

**Thal** is smaller and more volcanic: roughly 900 miles east-to-west and 700 miles north-to-south. It sits slightly south of Mira's midpoint on the north-south axis, meaning the shortest Shroud Sea crossing approaches Thal's northwestern coast.

On a map, north is toward the Greatwood and the tundra fringe. The Spine of the World runs vertically through Mira's right third. Drûn-Khazad sits near Thal's center-left, slightly south of the continent's midpoint.

## Major Rivers of Mira

**The Aldwater**
The great commercial river of Mira. Rises in the northern Spine near the glacial snowfields above Karaz-Dûn, flows west and southwest through the Central Plains, passes through Aldenholt, and empties into the Bay of Mourne on the western coast. Navigable by barge for its lower two-thirds. The primary trade artery connecting the dwarven holds to the human capitals and the sea. On a map it appears as the dominant river feature of the continent, cutting a broad west-running line across the Central Plains.

**The Silverthread**
A slower, narrower river rising in the southern Greatwood and flowing south through western Eldermark farmland before joining the Aldwater a day's travel east of Aldenholt. The water runs unusually clear and cold year-round — a quality locals attribute to its Greatwood origin. Eldermark farmers consider land along the Silverthread the best in the kingdom. On a map it appears as a north-south tributary feeding into the Aldwater from the northwest.

**The Vossrun**
Rises in the southern Spine near the Kazaad-Brak approaches, flows northeast across the open Vosskaran frontier. Forms the natural boundary between Vosskara and the eastern Eldermark before dispersing into the Sorrowmarsh's eastern drainage. Fast and cold in its upper reaches, slow and silty where it flattens out near the marsh. No one navigates its lower section. On a map it runs northeast from the Spine's southern end toward the Sorrowmarsh.

**The Greyflow**
Not a pleasant river. Originates in the Sorrowmarsh and flows southeast toward the Shroud coast. Slow, dark-grey water with a faint ash smell even far from its source. No fish. Local Vosskaran garrison towns do not draw from it. Empties into the Shroud Shore through a wide, silted delta that makes the coast here particularly inhospitable. On a map it is the only river on Mira's eastern side, marking the southern boundary of the Sorrowmarsh.

## Coastlines and Bays

**The Bay of Mourne** (western Mira)
A broad, deep bay where the Aldwater meets the western sea. The bay's northern headland is where Caer Brannoch is built — a fortress city on dramatic sea-cliffs with a natural harbor below. The bay is the most sheltered deep-water anchorage on Mira's western coast and consequently the center of western maritime trade. The Tidewarden fleet is based here.

**The Bay of Sulwyn** (western Mira, south of Mourne)
Also called the Drowned Bay. Where Caer Sulwyn slid into the sea two hundred years ago. The ruins of the old city are visible at extreme low tide — stone towers emerging from the water at odd angles, streets running into nothing. Ships avoid it. Fishermen report strange currents and will not net in the bay. On a map it sits just south of the Bay of Mourne, marked with a ruined-city symbol.

**The Copper Shelf** (southern Mira)
A wide shallow-water shelf extending south from Mira's southern coast. The Copper Isles sit on this shelf — close enough to Mira to be reached easily, far enough to maintain political independence in practice. The shelf makes the southern coast poor for deep-draft shipping but excellent for small-boat trade and fishing.

**The Shroud Shore** (eastern Mira)
Mira's eastern coast where the Ashfields meet the sea. Grey pebble beaches, no natural harbors, a permanent low ash-haze. The sea here is technically the Shroud Sea's western edge — already strange, already wrong. No fishing villages. The Brotherhood posts one observation station here, staffed by volunteers who serve six-month rotations and are never quite the same afterward.

**The Thal Coast** (western Thal)
Where the Sailor's Guild's previous crossing landed. Rocky, grey-black volcanic shoreline. No natural bay — the approach requires anchoring offshore. The Aescryd ruins found forty years ago are here, half-buried in volcanic aggregate on a flat terrace above the tide line.

## Terrain — Region by Region

### The Greatwood (northern Mira)

From above the canopy appears as an unbroken dark-green mass broken only by the pale-silver patches of silverwood groves near Lirien-Thal and the circular light-openings of the Eight Glades. The wood's northern edge transitions into a shorter, stranger tree-line — locals call it the Edgewood — before giving way to tundra scrub at the continent's northern coast.

At ground level: ancient trunks wider than houses, root systems that form walls and natural bridges, a permanent green twilight even at noon. The deeper you go the larger the trees and the quieter the animals. Sound travels differently in the old growth. Visitors notice it without being able to say what changed.

Map texture: dense stippled or cross-hatched forest canopy with individual large trees indicated near Lirien-Thal. The Glades shown as small open circles within the forest mass.

### The Spine of the World (eastern Mira)

From the west the Spine presents as a wall of blue-grey mountains with three distinctive peaks marking the dwarven holds:
- **Kara-Thûn** (above Karaz-Dûn): the tallest, permanently snow-crowned, visible from the Central Plains on clear days
- **Khorumzad's Crown**: a broad flat-topped massif with a distinctive silhouette, the hold carved into its central mass
- **The Broken Fang** (above Kazaad-Brak): twin peaks split by an ancient fault, lightning magnet in storm season

The western face has gentler foothills — pine forest transitioning to farmland. The eastern face drops sharply into the Ashfields as sheer cliff-faces of grey-black volcanic rock.

Glaciers are visible at the highest elevations in the northern Spine. Snow is permanent above a certain altitude. The mountain color shifts from grey-green in the foothills to grey-blue in the mid-ranges to white-black at the peaks.

Map texture: classic Tolkien-style row-of-peaks illustration, denser and more dramatic in the north, the three named peaks taller than their neighbors. Dotted lines indicating the Underway running beneath the range connecting all three holds.

### The Central Plains (central Mira)

From above: a patchwork of farmland, managed woodland, and river valleys. The borders between kingdoms are marked by fortified towns on river crossings and road junctions rather than dramatic terrain. Occasional drumlins — low rounded hills left by ancient glaciers — break the flat.

The plains are not featureless. The river valleys of the Aldwater and Silverthread are the main organizing features. Forests have been managed for timber for centuries — you can tell old-growth from managed woodland by the regularity of the second-growth trees.

Map texture: light stippling or open farmland hatching with small woodlot symbols scattered throughout. River valleys shown with gentle shading.

### The Ashfields (eastern Mira)

From above: a gradual color change from brown-gold farmland to flat grey-brown dead soil. The transition takes about fifty miles — you notice the soil lightening, vegetation thinning, the first ghost stumps appearing, then crumbled farmstead walls, then the ash-haze thickening as you move east.

The Ashfields were once productive rolling hills and light forest — farmland and wilderness that supported life. They died over centuries as ash drifted west across the Shroud Sea on prevailing winds from Drûn-Khazad, year by year, growing denser as Mordvar's power stirs. The dead zone creeps westward still. Crumbled stone walls of long-abandoned farmsteads and ghost stumps of ancient trees break the grey monotony — evidence of the world that was here before the ash.

Vosskaran garrison towns are the last built structures before the landscape empties. Beyond them: grey-brown ground, wind, the smell of ash, goblin territory, and the occasional outlaw camp of those who fled justice in Vosskara or Eldermark.

Map texture: light grey-brown stippling increasing in density toward the east. Ghost stump symbols scattered through the middle zone. Dotted lines for the garrison road. Occasional crumbled wall symbols in the dead farmland zone. No living trees.

### The Sorrowmarsh (eastern Mira)

Low-lying wetland in the shadow of the Spine's southeastern slopes. The Vossrun feeds it from the west; the Greyflow drains it (slowly) to the east. The ground is never dry. Black mud, standing water that never fully clears, occasional dead trees bleached white standing at wrong angles.

Nothing grows here in the conventional sense. Reeds died out centuries ago — the water chemistry is wrong. The ghost-lights (natural phosphorescent gas from decomposing Second Age battlefield dead, not supernatural in origin — though the origin is dark enough) are visible from the Ashfields road at night as faint blue-green smears low over the water.

Map texture: marsh stippling (horizontal dashes), dead tree symbols, the ghost-light notation as small circles. The marsh is roughly oval, positioned southeast of the Spine's southern end.

### The Weeping Wood (northeastern Mira)

A dead forest in northeastern Mira, north of the Ashfields, east of the Greatwood's southeastern fringe. Naergrim-controlled territory — the Naergrim dark elves chose the Ash Throne two thousand years ago and settled here, and their corruption magic killed the forest. The trees are grey-trunked with permanent bare branches — they have not produced leaves in recorded history. The cloud cover above the wood does not move with regional wind patterns; it sits fixed overhead in all weather, a permanent effect of Naergrim corruption magic.

The voices in the trees are Naergrim scouts watching the borders, not echoes or supernatural haunting. The Weeping Wood is extremely dangerous territory: almost no one enters and returns unchanged, because the Naergrim do not permit casual passage.

Mor-Vethrin sits at the wood's eastern edge, built into dark cliff escarpment rock where the dead forest meets hard stone.

From a map perspective it appears as a dark irregular patch of dead forest north of the Ashfields, east of the Greatwood's southeastern fringe, clearly distinct from both.

Map texture: bare-branch tree symbols (no leaf canopy), grey/dark shading beneath, fixed cloud symbol above. Dark fortress symbol at the eastern cliff edge for Mor-Vethrin.

### The Ash-Steppe (western Thal)

Vast flat grassland in Thal's western reaches transitioning to harder grey hardpan as you move east toward the volcanic interior. Seasonal rivers run in spring snowmelt and vanish by midsummer, leaving dry riverbeds as the only topographic variation in otherwise featureless terrain.

Scattered standing stones mark pre-First Age ruins — not dramatic ruins with walls and towers, just stones, some worked, most now tilted or fallen. No scholar has identified what civilization placed them or why they were placed here specifically.

Map texture: flat open space with very light stippling. Dry riverbed lines running east-west. Occasional small stone-circle symbols for the ruins.

### The Vrothmor Peaks (central Thal)

Not a range like the Spine but a cluster of irregular volcanic cones, dark grey-black rock with no glaciation (too geologically active for sustained ice). Steam vents are visible from a distance as white plumes. The rock formations are sharper and more chaotic than the Spine — not the rounded-by-erosion mountains of Mira but fresh volcanic extrusions with jagged profiles.

Map texture: jagged irregular peak symbols rather than the smooth Tolkien-row used for the Spine. Darker, more crowded, steam vent symbols at several locations.

### Drûn-Khazad (central Thal)

A massive shield volcano rather than a cone — broad, low-profile from a distance, with a wide flat summit and a caldera visible as a dark depression. The ash cloud above it is permanent, shown on a map as a grey smear extending west-northwest (the prevailing wind direction).

The lava flows on the lower slopes create a concentric banded terrain — alternating grey and black rock — that is distinctive from above. Nothing grows on the upper two-thirds of the volcano. The lower slopes have sparse ash-adapted scrub.

From Thal's western coast on clear days you can see the glow of the caldera at night above the horizon. The Shroud Sea is 900 miles wide — the caldera glow is not visible from Mira's eastern coast.

Map texture: large shield-volcano symbol (different from the cone-peaks of Vrothmor), concentric lava-flow banding indicated on lower slopes, permanent ash-cloud extending west, caldera marked with red/orange glow symbol.

## Major Roads and Routes

**The Salt Road** — Mira's primary north-south coastal road, running the length of the western coast. Caer Brannoch is its northern anchor, the Copper Isles ferry port its southern terminus.

**The King's Road** — East-west road from Aldenholt through the Central Plains to the Spine's western foothills and the Underway entrance near Khorumzad.

**The Frontier Road** — North-south road running through the Ashfields along the Spine's eastern face, connecting Vosskaran garrison towns. Not pleasant travel.

**The Pilgrim's Road** — Northwest from Aldenholt through western Eldermark farmland to the Greatwood's southern edge, then the Aelorin manage their own paths inward.

**The Underway** — Underground, shown on maps as a dotted line connecting Karaz-Dûn, Khorumzad, and Kazaad-Brak beneath the Spine. The only reliable transit through the mountains year-round.
