# Side Quests: Game 3

## Overview
Game 3's side quests are the payoffs. Most threads planted in Games 1 and 2 resolve here. These quests are emotionally heavier than the earlier ones — the party knows what is at stake and the choices reflect that weight. Several have no good answer, only answers the player can live with.

---

## 1. The Ashsteel Formula (Final Manufacturing)

**Context:** The formula has been assembled across Games 1 and 2. The volcanic aggregate is now accessible.

**Summary:** Barak Stonecroft, if he survived Game 2 (his survival is not guaranteed and depends on player choices), has traveled with the party's knowledge to the Thal coast. He can manufacture ashsteel given the aggregate and a forge capable of the temperatures required. The Ashen Hand is also aware the formula is nearly complete and has sent agents to prevent it.

**What the player does:** Protects Barak through the manufacturing process, which takes two in-game days and requires the party to hold a position against intermittent pressure. The process is mostly Barak working while the player manages the perimeter.

**Payoff:** Completed ashsteel has specific properties against magically-reinforced structures — relevant for the approach to Drun-Khazad's lower levels. Also Barak's emotional payoff: he is an old man doing the most significant work of his life for reasons he only partially understands, and he does it without complaint. If Dagna is present, there is a scene between them here.

**Tone:** Protective and grounded. The world-saving machinery requires someone standing guard while an old man works at a forge.

## 2. Eilwen's Theory

**Questgiver:** Eilwen, finally able to share her full twenty years of private research.

**Summary:** Eilwen has developed a theory that the Shroud Sea's weather anomalies are not natural — they are a side effect of Mordvar's first hollowing ritual, which occurred near a coastal site that happens to sit on the same deep-rock formation as the sea floor between the continents. She wants the party's help testing this theory at a specific coastal location on Thal.

**What the player does:** Accompanies Eilwen to the coastal site, which is in Ashen Hand-monitored territory. Protects her while she takes measurements. Discovers she is correct: there is a resonance signature at the site consistent with a major working, and it has a specific frequency that Corvus recognizes from his own deepest sensitivity to Mordvar's reforming presence.

**Payoff:** Corvus understands, for the first time, that his ability to sense Mordvar's reforming presence is not a random trait. His mother's family had a connection to those who worked the original unmaking that was never documented. Eilwen's theory is also practically significant: the coastal site is a secondary convergence point for Mordvar's scattered essence — a place in Mira where his presence pools — which means the final confrontation has a specific vulnerability the party can exploit.

**Tone:** A scholar's life work validated at the worst possible time and in the most useful possible way.

## 3. Aldric's Children

**Context:** By Game 3, Aldric's investigation into his sister's death has progressed significantly. He has identified the Ashen Hand figure responsible.

**Summary:** That figure is dead — killed in the events of Game 2's main plot. But they left behind two children who are now orphaned and in the care of distant relatives who are becoming aware of what the children's parent did. Brand, one of the party's Game 3 contacts, insists on following the party specifically because he knows where the children are and believes they need protection the relatives cannot provide.

**What the player does:** Decides whether to divert to verify Brand's concern. If they do: the children are fine, the relatives are decent people, Brand was overcautious. But the detour reveals a piece of intelligence about the Ashen Hand's succession planning that changes the party's understanding of what happens after Mordvar is defeated.

**Payoff:** The detour is the point. It's a quest about whether you stop for people when the world is ending. Aldric watches the player make this choice and it affects his final scene with Roland.

**Tone:** Quiet and human. The detour costs time the party cannot easily spare. The children are okay. That's worth something.

## 4. The Sailor's Guild Confession

**Questgiver:** Three Sailor's Guild captains who ask to speak with Roland privately.

**Summary:** Forty years ago, one of their ships crossed the Shroud Sea. Eight of twenty-two crew returned. They found Aescryd ruins and evidence of Mordvar's operations. The surviving captain filed a report that was buried by Guild leadership at the time. Two of those three original captains are dead. The third is one of the three who has come to Roland. She has been carrying this for forty years and the Sailor's Guild's current leadership — Yaromir — doesn't know.

**What the player does:** Advises the captains on whether and how to tell Yaromir. The captains want Roland's counsel specifically because he is outside the Sailor's Guild's institutional hierarchy and has demonstrated judgment they trust.

**Payoff:** If the captains tell Yaromir, the Sailor's Guild contributes two additional ships to the Thal crossing with full knowledge of the risk. If they don't, the Sailor's Guild contributes one ship and the surviving captain spends the crossing visibly frightened in a way that affects crew morale. The practical difference is small. The moral difference is the point.

**Tone:** Old guilt and whether honesty serves anything at this stage. Roland's answer reveals something about how he thinks about his own expulsion from the Iron Chalice.

## 5. Hagrim's Heir

**Context:** Hagrim was freed from his informal house arrest in Game 2 through the Bromrin's Letters quest.

**Summary:** Hagrim is dying. He has known for a year and told no one. His clan's succession is contested in ways that will, if unresolved, create a political crisis in Khorumzad at the worst possible moment. He needs a witness to an oath he has already prepared — an oath that designates an heir and forecloses the contested claims. He cannot use a clan witness because the contested claimants have influence over the witness pool.

**What the player does:** Serves as outside witness to the oath's execution. This is ceremonially simple and politically significant. The player must understand enough of what they're witnessing to confirm it correctly if challenged later.

**Payoff:** The succession is secured. In the epilogue, Khorumzad's political stability during the post-trilogy reconstruction is partly traceable to this oath. Hagrim dies three weeks after the party leaves for Thal. He knew he was the last task.

**Tone:** An old man finishing his work. Dignified and sad and complete.

## 6. Three Naergrim

**Context:** Follows Serethi's cooperation with the party in the main plot.

**Summary:** Three Naergrim from the dissident faction have followed the party to Thal without authorization from Serethi. They want to fight. They do not have the training or preparation for what the party is about to do, and if they die in Ashen Hand territory it will be used to justify reprisals against the dissident faction.

**What the player does:** Intercedes with them. Sends them back or finds a role for them that doesn't get them killed. Sorin, if present, handles most of the conversation — he knows these three. The choice has a genuine argument on both sides: they have a right to fight for their own freedom, and exercising that right here will cost them.

**Payoff:** If sent back, they reach Naergrim territory and their return gives Serethi political ammunition against the mainstream faction — a small thing, but useful for the post-trilogy world. If kept, one of them dies in the approach to Drun-Khazad and the other two survive with the specific knowledge that they were there at the end. Both are valid. The player chooses.

**Tone:** The cost of being right about a thing but in the wrong place at the wrong time.

## 7. Caerith's Tree

**Timing:** After Caerith's death. Available only if Seren is in the party.

**Summary:** Seren asks the party to stop. She has something she needs to do and she won't explain it fully, only that it will take half a day and she needs to do it alone. She goes into the forest at the edge of Drun-Khazad's caldera region and comes back looking different in a way that is hard to name.

**What the player does:** Waits. Optionally, the player can follow Seren — if they do, they find her standing in a specific clearing, speaking in High Aelorin to no one visible. There is a young tree in the clearing that was not there yesterday. If the player does not follow, they see only Seren returning.

**Payoff:** Seren plants a tree for Caerith in the Aelorin tradition of the Aelthiren — the ritual acknowledgment of a death. She cannot give Caerith the full rite (that would require the Aelthurion's recognition, which is complicated), but she can give her this. If the player followed and asks about it, Seren says: "He was Aelorin for a very long time before he was anything else. Someone should remember that part."

**Tone:** Quiet grief and the specific care of someone who understands what the dead were before they became what they died as.
