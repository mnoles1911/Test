# Side Quests: Game 2

## Overview
Game 2's side quests are heavier than Game 1's. The party is deeper in the world's problems and the stakes of individual choices are clearer. These quests develop companion arcs, continue threads from Game 1, and set up specific payoffs in Game 3.

---

## 1. The Ashsteel Formula (Resolution)

**Context:** Continued from Game 1. The player has Orvin's incomplete formula documentation.

**Summary:** Barak Stonecroft, the retired dwarven engineer the party encounters in the Khorumzad region, has the missing materials reference in his personal archive. He doesn't know it — it's buried in forty-year-old survey documentation that nobody has asked about since he retired.

**What the player does:** Gains Barak's trust enough to access his archive (this happens organically through Dagna's personal quest, which brings the party to Barak anyway). Identifies the correct document. Barak is intrigued and offers to help complete the formula if given time.

**Payoff:** Barak completes a partial formula and notes that full production requires a specific type of volcanic aggregate only available near Drun-Khazad. The ashsteel thread continues to Game 3. This quest also advances Dagna's personal arc — Barak's records contain the maintenance document that clears her father's name, which is its own payoff regardless of the formula.

**Tone:** A long thread finally finding its middle. The satisfaction is partial — you have more than you had, not everything you need.

## 2. Corvus's Unregistered Signature

**Questgiver:** Lyren, the Dawnbringers' Conclave insider, who finds Corvus through Aelthurion's introduction.

**Summary:** Lyren has been monitoring Conclave resonance records and has found Corvus's signature in files she shouldn't be able to access — which means either Corvus has been doing things he hasn't told anyone about, or someone has been filing actions under his credentials. She wants to know which before she decides whether to trust him.

**What the player does:** Corvus and the player investigate the flagged records together. It turns out to be both: some entries are Corvus's own unregistered work, which he has to account for honestly. Others are entries filed by a former Conclave contact of Vaeroth's — a placement made before his death — using a copied credential signature. The investigation identifies the contact and gives the party their first clear picture of how deep the Hand's legacy network had been embedded in the Conclave under Vaeroth's direction.

**Payoff:** Major Corvus character development. He has to explain his hidden resonance capabilities to the party — this is the scene where he tells Roland about sensing Mordvar's reforming presence. Lyren, once she understands what Corvus's diagnostic resonance actually does, substantially upgrades her assessment of what the party is capable of.

**Tone:** Trust-building through uncomfortable honesty. Corvus has been carrying a secret for a long time and the investigation removes his ability to keep carrying it. This is relief and exposure simultaneously.

## 3. The Pale Supper's Vault Intel

**Context:** The Pale Supper cell from Game 1 has been monitored by Wyn since the events of Game 1's side quests.

**Summary:** Wyn surfaces with intelligence: the Pale Supper has been tasked with identifying independent scholars who have accessed records related to the Vault of Aen-Vael. This means someone in the Ashen Hand has started looking for the Vault — and the party is not the only group moving toward Khorumzad.

**What the player does:** Uses Wyn's intelligence to identify the scholars who have been flagged, reach them before the Pale Supper does, and either warn them or extract the information the Pale Supper is trying to suppress. One of the scholars has a document connecting the Vault's location to a specific geological formation in the Spine of the World that significantly narrows the search area.

**Payoff:** Accelerates the Vault search and creates a race. Also reveals what Wyn witnessed in Game 1 that made her defect: the Pale Supper executed a scholar for asking the wrong questions. She describes it here for the first time. This is the emotional core of the quest.

**Tone:** The world getting more dangerous faster than expected. And Wyn finally saying the thing she's been carrying.

## 4. Bromrin's Letters

**Questgiver:** Bromrin, a dwarven courier in Khorumzad's middle district, who has three letters he was paid to deliver but whose recipients have become unreachable.

**Summary:** The three letters were commissioned by an anonymous sender before the party arrived in Khorumzad. Each recipient is someone who would, if warned, become a significant obstacle to the Ashen Hand's operations in the region: Queen Eilwen (of Caer Brannoch), Hagrim (a dwarven clan elder with records relevant to the Vault), and Darna (a Conclave-affiliated researcher who has been getting too close to sensitive records).

**What the player does:** Delivers the letters. Each recipient has been made unreachable in a different way — Eilwen is in the middle of a sea observation and won't break for messages, Hagrim is under informal house arrest by his own clan for reasons that aren't what they appear, Darna has gone to ground after a near-miss she hasn't told anyone about. Reaching each one is its own small puzzle.

**Payoff:** All three recipients become allied to the party's cause in specific ways. Hagrim's oath-witness role in Game 3 comes from this introduction. The anonymous sender is eventually revealed to be the Seventh Dawnbringer.

**Tone:** An organizational quest that's really about three separate people each doing their best in a bad situation.

## 5. The Second Agent in the Hold

**Questgiver:** No single questgiver — the player notices something is wrong and investigates.

**Summary:** Drossvik, the Ashen Hand agent placed in Khorumzad's administrative tier, has been in position eleven years. He is good at his job. But the party's arrival has forced him to begin active countermeasures, and active countermeasures leave traces. The player can find those traces if they're paying attention.

**What the player does:** Investigates the traces, identifies Drossvik, and must decide: expose him publicly, surveil him and use him as a vector for disinformation, or confront him privately. Drossvik, when confronted privately, is not what the party expects — he is a man who has been in a deep cover role for eleven years and is tired in a very specific way. He doesn't want to die. He isn't sure he still believes in what he was placed here for.

**Payoff:** The choice of what to do with Drossvik has real consequences. Exposed publicly, he becomes a martyr the Ashen Hand uses to harden their remaining assets. Surveilled, he feeds the party one accurate piece of intelligence and one false one before his handler notices and recalls him. Confronted privately and handled carefully, he becomes a reluctant source for the rest of Game 2. None of these is clean.

**Tone:** A moral grey area with a human face. Drossvik is not good, but he is comprehensible.

## 6. The Nameless Council's Message

**Questgiver:** A message arrives for the party through Aelthurion's communication network with no identified sender, asking them to deliver a physical object to a specific location in the Spine of the World's middle reaches.

**Summary:** The object is a sealed iron cylinder. The location is a cairn three days from Khorumzad. The instructions say: leave it, do not wait, do not open it.

**What the player does:** Delivers it. On the return, passes a traveller moving in the opposite direction who glances at them and says nothing. The cylinder is gone when the party passes the cairn again two days later on a different route.

**Payoff:** Nothing immediate. In Game 3, a specific piece of information reaches the party at exactly the right moment through a channel that shouldn't have known where they were. If the player made this delivery, they understand why. If they didn't, they never find out. This is the kind of quest that rewards players who did the obscure thing without knowing why.

**Tone:** Deliberately opaque. The world has parts that don't explain themselves.
