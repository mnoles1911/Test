# Copper Isles — Backstory

> Companion: `GEOGRAPHY.md` (physical geography of the archipelago). This file covers the political, historical, and economic context that drives Game One.

---

## In one paragraph

The Copper Isles are an old land in shallow new development. For fifty generations the islands have been home to a self-organized fishing people — humans, no kings, no central authority, a loose confederation of village elders who meet quarterly at a stone cairn on the westernmost summit. About eighty years ago a Solgrade survey expedition discovered that the islands' marble peaks were veined with copper. About sixty years ago Solgrade's Merchant's Council issued the first extraction charter to a private company. Five more charters have followed. The chartered companies operate their mines and smelters from fortified compounds on the larger islands; they pay tribute to Solgrade and a token harbor fee to the locals. The locals do not see the harbor fee as recognition of their political existence. The companies have not noticed they have not noticed.

This is the engine of Game One.

## The Locals

> Internal name: TBD. Externally referred to by Solgrade as "the islanders" or, in colonial company correspondence, "the natives" or simply "the under-people." None of these are flattering. The locals' own name for themselves is reserved for authoring.

A human population, descended from an early coastal migration roughly fifteen hundred years before the Third Age. They came in small boats from the south, settled the largest islands first, and never left. Their society has been remarkably stable for the bulk of that span: village-scale, fishing-and-forest-based, decentralized.

**Social organization.** No kings, no nobles, no inherited offices. Every village is governed by an informal council of elders chosen by consensus from the village's adult population. Decisions affecting more than one village are made — when they need to be made — at a quarterly gathering called the Cairn (see below). Disputes between villages, between families, between individuals are handled by mediation; the mediators are usually elder women, though this is custom rather than rule. Murder is rare. When it happens, the perpetrator is exiled to one of the smaller islands and not spoken of again.

**Economy.** Subsistence-plus-trade. Every village fishes; most farm a little (grain plots, vegetable gardens, small orchards on the leeward terraces); some specialize in charcoal-burning, fleece, salt-making, boat-building, or the carving of certain ceremonial objects. Goods move between villages through a network of small craft and a system of accounting kept on knotted cords by the harbormaster of each village.

**The Cairn.** A stone cairn at the summit of Marrow Holt — the westernmost large island — predating any record. The local council meets here on the four quarter-days of each year. Attendance is voluntary; villages send whichever elder is willing to make the climb. The meetings are not secret in principle but the chartered companies do not know they happen, because no local has ever told a Solgrade clerk anything about them. The Cairn has no formal authority; what it produces is consensus.

**Religion.** Animist, low-key, mostly observed at home. The islands themselves are not regarded as gods but as ancestors — older relatives who can be addressed and propitiated but not commanded. The marble peaks are regarded with particular respect. There is a quiet horror at the company practice of blasting the marble for ore.

**Numbers.** Pre-colonial population probably ~12,000 across the archipelago. Current population (after sixty years of pressure) ~9,000, weighted heavily toward Marrow Holt, Caer Vethryn, and the resettlement villages on the smaller islands.

## Solgrade

The mainland city-state of Solgrade is the political force behind everything that has gone wrong in the archipelago over the last sixty years. Solgrade itself does not appear in Game One — Roland never goes there — but its hand is on every charter, every fortress, and every piece of company-stamped silver in the islands.

**What Solgrade is.** A wealthy mainland mercantile city, governed by a Merchant's Council, oligarchic in practice, with a long tradition of issuing exclusive trade charters to private joint-stock companies in exchange for a fixed share of profits and political loyalty. (The broader description of Solgrade lives in `lore/locations/SOLGRADE.md` and `lore/WORLD_GEOGRAPHY.md`. Game One uses only the colonial-master facet.)

**Why the islands.** Copper. Solgrade's regional rivals control the established mainland mines; Solgrade has been hungry for an independent ore source for generations. The islands' marble seams — discovered by a Solgrade survey expedition eighty years ago — were the answer.

**The Charter system.** Solgrade's Merchant's Council issues exclusive copper-extraction charters for fixed terms (15 years, renewable). Each charter grants a single company:
- Exclusive mining rights over a defined territory (one or more named islands, or a portion of one)
- The right to fortify
- The right to import labor from the mainland
- The right to administer summary justice over the company's own employees
- The right to retain a private armed garrison (capped, per charter, at varying numbers)

In exchange the company pays Solgrade a fixed annual tribute (roughly a third of declared profits) and a "harbor fee" to the local population (negotiated separately, paid in coin or trade goods, generally token).

The charter does **not** grant the company sovereignty over the local population. This is a deliberate Solgrade fiction — Solgrade does not legally claim the islands, the locals are not Solgrade subjects, and the company's authority over them is limited to the company's own land and employees. In practice the company fortresses and mines occupy enough of the islands' usable surface that the legal distinction is meaningless. The locals know this. They have no formal mechanism to do anything about it.

**The Chancellor of the Copper Charters.** Solgrade's appointed colonial governor in the archipelago. Resides in the **Cinderhall** — the fortified counting-house at Caer Aelynd's harbor mouth. The Chancellor's role is theoretically supreme: mediating between the chartered companies, collecting Solgrade's tribute, representing Solgrade's interests in the rare disputes the locals push hard enough to register. In practice the Chancellor's power is mostly ceremonial. The chartered companies pay Solgrade through the Cinderhall but answer politically to the Merchant's Council back home, and the Chancellor's mediating function works only when the companies want it to.

The current Chancellor is named for authoring. Suggested character: an aging Solgrade administrator near the end of his career, philosophically uncomfortable with the colonial project but career-trapped. Useful as a non-aligned NPC for Roland.

## The Chartered Companies

Six charters are currently active (one of them moribund). Each is described in `GEOGRAPHY.md` from the geographic side; this section covers the political and economic side.

### The Iron Furrow Charter — Bryn Mor (proposal)

The second-oldest charter, granted ~50 years ago. Family-owned (the Caradan family, junior partners in Solgrade's oldest mercantile dynasty); the current senior factor is the founder's grandson, a hard man in his fifties who has spent his entire adult life on the island. The Iron Furrow's mines are the most aggressively worked in the archipelago — they have stripped Bryn Mor's east face to bedrock — and the most violent in their treatment of locals. Most of the disappearances of company foremen happen here.

### Coppergild & Sons — Caer Aelynd (proposal)

The oldest charter in the archipelago, ~60 years old. Conservative, family-run, modestly profitable. Holds the central wharves of Aelynd Port. The current senior partner is a woman named for authoring; she is regarded by the other companies as old-fashioned and excessively concerned with what she calls "the long view." She is the only senior factor who consistently pays the harbor fee in full and on time. This has not made her friends among her peers.

### The Saltvein Combine — Caer Aelynd (proposal)

The largest of the three Aelynd companies. Joint-stock, founded ~30 years ago by a syndicate of mid-tier Solgrade merchants. Operates the largest smelter in the archipelago. Aggressively expanding. The Combine's senior factor is openly contemptuous of both the locals and the older Aelynd companies and has lobbied Solgrade for years to consolidate the Aelynd charters under Combine management.

### The Vendrick House — Caer Aelynd (proposal)

The youngest of the Aelynd charters, ~12 years old. Founded by the Vendrick family — Solgrade old-money with political connections at the Merchant's Council. Aggressive expansion into territories the older companies considered settled; the controversial second smelter inside the Cinder ward (the local charcoal-burners' neighborhood) was a Vendrick decision and remains a flashpoint.

### The Bronze Hand Company — Tor Galdoryn (proposal)

The wealthiest single chartered company. ~40 years old. Consortium of several Solgrade mercantile houses, structured to keep ownership obscure. Holds the entire Tor Galdoryn island under a single charter — a unique arrangement, granted only because Solgrade was willing to make exceptions for the richest copper deposit. Standing private garrison of ~250 men, the only company army in the archipelago at proper military scale.

The Bronze Hand operates Tor Galdoryn as a closed labor camp and has effectively cleared the island of native population over four decades. Its senior factor is the only company head who lives full-time inside his fortress (the Calderet) and is rarely seen outside it. The company's relationship with both the locals and the other companies is openly hostile.

If Game One has a single antagonistic institution, this is it.

### The Vethryn Mining Concern — Caer Vethryn (proposal, dwindling)

Granted a charter ~35 years ago when surface ore on Caer Vethryn looked promising; the surface deposit ran out within fifteen years and the deep shafts proved uneconomic. The Concern is now down to twelve men in a half-empty walled compound on the south coast. The charter has been renewed twice on political inertia. The current senior factor is a tired middle-aged man who hates his job and would gladly close the operation if Solgrade would let him. The locals on Caer Vethryn essentially run the island around the Concern's compound.

## Tensions, Resistance, and the Texture of Daily Life

The colonial situation is not a static oppression. It is a sixty-year-old slow-motion conflict that has produced its own equilibrium of pressures, resistances, accommodations, and quiet betrayals. The flavor of Game One should come from this texture.

**On the harshest islands** (Bryn Mor, Tor Galdoryn): the locals are visibly subjugated. Forced or near-forced labor, displacement, dispossession, sporadic violence by company guards. Resistance is mostly passive — sabotage, foot-dragging, theft, occasional disappearance of foremen.

**On the middle islands** (Caer Aelynd): a more complex coexistence. Locals and colonials share the same space, drink in the same taverns, sleep with each other, sometimes marry, and hold each other in exquisitely calibrated mistrust. The Triumvirate's three companies have built informal accommodations with the local wards (the Cinder, the Wash) which the locals view as pragmatic rather than legitimating.

**On the lightest islands** (Marrow Holt, Caer Vethryn): the locals retain meaningful autonomy and the colonial presence is small enough to be locally managed.

**Cross-archipelago networks.** The locals' quarterly Cairn meetings have become a slow-motion coordination mechanism that the companies don't know exists. Information flows between islands in fishing boats and in the Sailor's Guild's safe-house network (which the Guild quietly extends to local fishermen they trust). What the Cairn produces is consensus rather than command, but the consensus has begun, in the last decade, to include things the Cairn would not have considered a generation ago.

**The Sailor's Guild.** The Guild is an international institution older than the colonial period and has deep roots in the archipelago. Its position is officially neutral, in practice quietly sympathetic to the locals. The Guild's Archive on its own small island — the largest collection of maritime knowledge in the world — has nothing to do with the colonial dispute, but the Guild's Copper Isles steward has been known to lose chartered company mail in the post in subtle ways. (See `lore/BACKSTORY_SAILORS_GUILD.md` for the Guild's broader role; for Game One, treat the Guild as a present-but-non-aligned faction.)

**Solgrade's blind spots.** The Merchant's Council does not understand the archipelago. Reports from the Chancellor reach the Council in quarterly bundles; the Council reads them through the lens of Solgrade's mainland politics, which is consumed with rivalries the islands play no part in. Most Solgrade councillors have never been to the Copper Isles and have no plans to go. This is the colonial project's strategic vulnerability: a directing center too distant to perceive what it is directing.

**The current decade.** A pivotal one. The Iron Furrow's grandson-factor has been escalating. The Vendrick House's smelter expansion in the Cinder ward has turned a marginal local grievance into a furious one. The Bronze Hand's clearance of Tor Galdoryn is now politically visible to the other companies and the Chancellor, and not all of them are comfortable with it. The Cairn met three months before the start of Game One and produced its first cross-island consensus to act in coordination on something concrete; what they decided is reserved for plot authoring.

## Roland's Entry Point

> Mechanics of arrival, opening quest, and Game One's spine are reserved for plot authoring. This section is a holding pen for the design constraints the geography and politics impose on the opening.

Roland arrives in Caer Aelynd Port on a Solgrade-flagged trader. He is an outsider — not a Solgrade administrator, not a chartered company employee, not a native islander. He carries his own reasons (to be authored) and is read by everyone he meets through the lens of the colonial situation: the companies see him as a possible new factor or hire, the Chancellor's office sees him as a possible Solgrade agent (or a possible spy on Solgrade), the locals see him as another one of the people who arrive and never leave.

His freedom of movement is a privilege Solgrade citizens take for granted and locals do not have. This is itself a Game One mechanic: Roland can pass through company gates and across charter boundaries without being stopped, and most of his early-game interactions with locals will be conditioned by what he chooses to do with that privilege.

The geography puts Roland on the most colonial of the islands at the start. The arc of Game One — to the extent it can be sketched in advance — is the gradual acquisition of context, contacts, and reasons that move him out toward the lighter islands and eventually to the Cairn.
