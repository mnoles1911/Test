# Copper Isles — Geography

> Companion: `BACKSTORY.md` (political and historical context). The geography described here is the playable area of Game One.

---

## Overview

The Copper Isles are a chain of five large islands strung in a loose southward-bowing arch across roughly 4 km of open ocean, with a scatter of perhaps fifteen smaller islands threaded between and around them. The five large islands have stood for ages — wave-eroded marble massifs whose summits rise up to 580 m as bare jagged outcroppings, the surrounding softer rock long since carried off by the sea. From far out, sailors recognize the archipelago by these distinctive white-marble peaks long before the lower forested slopes come over the horizon.

The seas around the islands are vicious in nearly every season. Storms drive the western swells against the windward cliffs constantly. Inside the chain — between and among the islands — the water is no kinder; the islands shelter each other less than a sailor would hope, and the gaps act as venturi for wind and current. Travel between islands is done in heavy-keeled fishing boats and in the chartered companies' iron-bound trade ships, never in small craft except in the brief calm windows of late summer.

All five large islands are forested below the treeline (~350 m). The forest is hard, weathered coastal woodland — dwarf-oak, salt-pine, sea-laurel, gorse — never tall but very thick. Above the treeline the marble peaks rise bare. All five large islands support populations of deer, boar, sea-birds, and a particular small black-furred fox the locals call a *brennock*.

All five large islands have native fishing villages older than memory and chartered colonial fortresses younger than living elders.

**Playable area:** 5 km × 5 km. The mainland city-state of Solgrade lies far to the north and is entirely off-map; Roland never sees it during Game One. He arrives in the archipelago in the opening sequence and remains within it for the duration.

## The Five Large Islands

> Names below are **proposals**, marked with [P]. Rename freely.

The islands are arrayed roughly west-to-east along a chain that bows slightly southward. Coordinates are given as (x_meters_from_west_edge, z_meters_from_north_edge) with +X east, +Z south, origin (0,0) at the northwest corner of the 5 km × 5 km playable area.

### 1. Marrow Holt [P] — westernmost

- **Position:** centered near (700, 2700)
- **Footprint:** ~1.0 km × 0.7 km
- **Peak:** 320 m (a single broad-shouldered marble dome, called locally **the Pate**)
- **Forest:** thick across the lower slopes, especially on the leeward east face
- **Character:** the locals' island. Smallest of the five large, oldest in continuous habitation. Three fishing villages, no chartered company fortress, no Solgrade garrison. There is one Solgrade trading post on the south harbor — a single timber-and-stone building staffed by two clerks who buy fish, sell salt and rope, and report quietly back to Caer Aelynd.
- **The Marrow:** the most extensive ancestral dugout network in the archipelago — generations of locally-carved tunnels honeycombing the marble core of the island, used variously as dwellings, food stores, storm-shelters, ritual halls, and (lately) hiding-places for things the companies should not see. Every old family on the Pate has at least one inherited side-tunnel they treat as private property without anyone outside knowing it exists.
- **Sacred geography:** the **Cairn** — a stone cairn at the summit, predating any record. Local village elders gather here quarterly. The companies do not know this happens. (See `BACKSTORY.md` → "The Cairn".)

### 2. Bryn Mor [P] — west-center

- **Position:** centered near (1700, 2400)
- **Footprint:** ~1.2 km × 0.9 km
- **Peak:** 480 m (a sharp twin-spired marble pinnacle, the **Brothers**, visible from anywhere in the archipelago and on a clear day from 50+ km out at sea)
- **Forest:** present on the west and north slopes; the entire east face has been stripped, blasted, and terraced for surface mining
- **Character:** the most aggressively extracted island. Eastern face scarred with company shafts, tailings piles, ore-roads, and the long iron-rimmed gravity-tracks that run finished ore down to the loading wharves.
- **Charter:** held by the **Iron Furrow Company** (proposal). Their fortress, **Furrow Keep**, sits at a mid-elevation cliff-shelf controlling the road from harbor to mine. Walled compound, garrison of ~80 mercenary guards, the company's senior factor in residence.
- **Locals:** pushed to a single overcrowded fishing village on the leeward (west) coast called **Lowbeck**. Tense. Disappearances of company foremen happen here at a rate the company finds politically irritating but cannot prove are deliberate.

### 3. Caer Aelynd [P] — center, the largest island

- **Position:** centered near (2700, 2500)
- **Footprint:** ~1.6 km × 1.1 km
- **Peak:** 420 m (a broad-shouldered massif rather than a spire, **Caer Aelynd's Crown**)
- **Forest:** runs up the windward (south and west) sides almost to the treeline; the leeward (north and east) sides are terraced for vineyards, grain plots, and orchards older than the colonial period
- **Character:** the de facto capital of the archipelago. Largest natural harbor — a south-facing crescent biting deep into the south coast, sheltered enough to hold even in autumn weather. Where Roland arrives at the start of Game One.
- **Settlements:**
    - **The Port** (also "Aelynd Port"): the colonial-era town wrapping the harbor. Pale stone buildings, copper-trimmed shutters, clay-tile roofs. Population ~3,000 — a mix of Solgrade administrators, company employees, contracted Solgrade workers, and a thin local minority pushed to the margins.
    - **The Cinder:** local ward uphill of the port, named for its charcoal-burners. Older than the Port. Locals here are tolerated because the companies need their charcoal for the smelters.
    - **The Wash:** local ward downhill of the port, the gutters and fish-cleaning yards. Where the under-people of Caer Aelynd live and work.
    - **Inland villages:** four traditional fishing-and-farming villages on the leeward coast that have so far avoided being absorbed by the colonial economy.
- **Charters:** three companies headquarter here, sharing the island in tense and constantly-litigated proximity. Locally referred to as **the Triumvirate**:
    - **Coppergild & Sons** (proposal) — the oldest charter, conservative, family-run, holds the central wharves
    - **the Saltvein Combine** (proposal) — newer, joint-stock, holds the eastern wharves and the largest smelter
    - **the Vendrick House** (proposal) — youngest, aggressive, expanding, holds the western wharves and a controversial second smelter inside the Cinder ward
- **Solgrade governance:** the **Chancellor of the Copper Charters** — Solgrade's appointed colonial governor — keeps a fortified counting-house called **the Cinderhall** on a rocky promontory at the harbor mouth. Office is theoretically supreme; in practice mostly ceremonial, mediating between the three companies.

### 4. Tor Galdoryn [P] — east-center, the highest peak

- **Position:** centered near (3700, 2400)
- **Footprint:** ~1.4 km × 1.0 km
- **Peak:** **580 m** — the highest in the archipelago, the marble white-veined and visible 60+ km out at sea (sailors set their final approach by it)
- **Forest:** present, but heavily logged on the east face for mine-timber and charcoal
- **Character:** the most fortified place in the archipelago. The richest copper deposit, the most efficient extraction operation, and the strictest control over labor.
- **Charter:** the entire island is held under a single charter by **the Bronze Hand Company** (proposal) — the wealthiest of the chartered companies and the only one with a private standing garrison in serious numbers (~250 men).
- **The Calderet:** a star-shaped fortress of white marble blocks (quarried locally) on the harbor mouth, walls 8 m thick at the base. Houses the company's senior factor, the senior factor's household, the garrison commander, and the company's strongroom.
- **Locals:** the Bronze Hand operates the island as effectively a closed labor-camp. Locals work in the mines, on company terms, or are removed from the island by company ship. Most have been removed. The remaining native population is small, sullen, and watched.
- **Plot weight:** the most authoritarian environment in Game One. Whatever Roland needs that's locked behind walls is here.

### 5. Caer Vethryn [P] — easternmost

- **Position:** centered near (4500, 2700)
- **Footprint:** ~1.1 km × 0.8 km
- **Peak:** 380 m (an older, more weathered marble outline — the **Hooded Mother**, a profile that locals have always read as a robed figure facing west)
- **Forest:** the deepest and densest in the archipelago — old-growth dwarf-oak and salt-pine cloaking the entire interior up to the treeline. Easy to get lost in; locals know the paths, outsiders generally do not.
- **Character:** the island where the colonial pressure has eased. A modest copper deposit ran out at the surface decades ago; the deep shafts are uneconomic to work; the company that holds the charter — **the Vethryn Mining Concern** (proposal) — is nearly defunct, down to a skeleton crew of twelve in a half-empty walled compound on the south coast.
- **Locals:** dominate the inland. Three traditional fishing villages on the coasts, two inland farming villages, and a network of forest paths connecting them that is essentially invisible to outsiders. Functional local-led society, local council, local custom.
- **Hidden caves:** numerous, in the forested interior. Several have been used as refuge points by locals fleeing the harsher islands. The companies are aware some of these caves exist but have never thoroughly mapped them.

## The Smaller Islands

Roughly fifteen smaller islands scatter through the playable area, varying from 80 m rock-stacks to 600 m wooded islets. They are mixed in among the large islands without obvious pattern — north of the chain, south of the chain, in the gaps between the big five. Most have at least one feature of interest. Names and exact positions are left for individual authoring; the categories below cover the functional types.

- **Resettlement villages.** Several smaller islands host fishing communities composed of locals pushed off the harshest large islands (especially Bryn Mor and Tor Galdoryn). These communities are small, tight, and quietly hostile to anything in company colors.
- **Lighthouse islands.** Three or four prominent rocks carry stone lighthouses maintained by the Sailor's Guild — the most important is the easternmost, marking the safe approach for ships running in from the east. Each has two keepers on six-month rotation.
- **The Quiet Harbors.** A handful of smaller islands carry Sailor's Guild safe-houses — what the Guild calls *quiet harbors*. Off the charts, used as contingency anchorages in heavy weather. Their existence is a Guild secret. The smallest one, called **Cradle**, is hidden inside a south-facing cove masked by overlapping rock spurs and is invisible from the standard sailing approach.
- **The Sailor's Guild Archive.** One of the larger small islands — north-northwest of Caer Aelynd, footprint roughly 600 m × 400 m, low and densely wooded — hosts the Sailor's Guild Archive. (See `lore/BACKSTORY_SAILORS_GUILD.md` for the Archive's role in the wider world; it sits here in this archipelago.) Four-story windowless stone building on the western cliff-top, built into an older lighthouse foundation.
- **Bird-rocks and pinnacles.** A handful of bare granite stacks rising sharply from the sea, uninhabited, used by sea-birds. Navigational landmarks.
- **The Boneyard.** A field of low rocky spires somewhere in the interior of the archipelago, breaking the surface at low tide and submerging at high. Has eaten three ships in living memory. Charted carefully.

## Bathymetry and Sea State

Bathymetry can be coarse — most of the playable space outside the islands is "open ocean, weather-hazardous." A few specific features:

- **Outer ocean** (most of the map): deep open water, 30+ m below sea level. Standard deep-blue color, heavy swells, frequent storm states from `WeatherManager`.
- **Inter-island channels:** deep enough for the chartered companies' iron-bound trade ships — generally 15–25 m. No special shallow shelves between the large islands.
- **Harbor approaches:** each large island has a sheltered harbor approach reaching 4–10 m at the wharves; outside the harbor mouth the water deepens fast.
- **The Boneyard shoal:** the only deliberately authored shallow hazard, charted on the locals' maps, marked by two iron pole-buoys.

Inner waters are not meaningfully calmer than outer waters. The archipelago's storm pattern (carried by `WeatherManager`'s `copper_isles_storm_belt.tres` profile, to be authored) defaults to overcast with frequent squalls; clear weather is rare and prized. Wind is variable and shifty due to the venturi effect of the gaps between the large islands.

## Climate, Wind, Weather

- **Latitude:** temperate. Cold-temperate by mainland standards. Winters wet and grey; summers brief, bright, and treasured. Snow rare at sea level, possible above the treeline.
- **Prevailing wind:** strong westerlies, with frequent shifts. Every island's west face is the windward face — taller cliffs, salt-stunted vegetation, copper-streaked rock.
- **Storm pattern:** hard. The Copper Isles sit at a latitude where Atlantic-style storm systems track regularly. Three-day blows are common in autumn and winter. The chartered companies' shipping schedules are built around predicted calm windows that often fail.
- **Weather profile:** new `assets/profiles/copper_isles_storm_belt.tres` (to be authored). Suggested authored opener: overcast / light-rain / heavy-rain / overcast / fog / light-rain. Random distribution beyond the authored window: 35 % overcast, 25 % light rain, 15 % heavy rain, 15 % fog, 8 % clear, 2 % storm. Lightning common during heavy-rain states (already supported by `WeatherManager`).
- **Tides:** ~2 m range, visually significant at the Boneyard and at the lower wharves of the larger ports.

## Cultural Identity (geographic notes only — see `BACKSTORY.md` for politics)

The archipelago has two populations whose footprints are spatially distinct:

- **Locals** (50+ generations on the islands): village clusters, traditional fishing wharves, forest paths, ancestral dugouts, the Cairn at Marrow Holt. Dispersed through every large island, dominant on Marrow Holt and Caer Vethryn, marginalized on the others.
- **Colonial settlement** (~60 years): the four chartered company fortresses (Furrow Keep, the Cinderhall counting-house, the three Triumvirate compounds at Aelynd Port, the Calderet on Tor Galdoryn, the dwindling Vethryn Concern compound), the company smelters, the company wharves, the formal Solgrade-administered town wrapping Caer Aelynd's harbor.

The two populations intersect daily at every point of contact and trust each other in almost none of them.

## Art Direction

**Palette:**
- **Marble peaks:** white with grey and pale-green veining (`#E8E4DC` to `#C8C2B4`). The defining art-direction beat for the archipelago. Bare, jagged, sharp against the sky.
- **Forest:** dark hard coastal greens (`#3E5240`, `#5A6E50`, `#728060`). Low and dense, never towering.
- **Cliff faces (windward, copper-streaked):** warm rust-grey base (`#8A7560`) with vertical streaks of oxidized copper green-blue (`#3E8C7A` to `#5FA89A`). Most visible on Bryn Mor's east face and on the lower cliffs of every island's windward side.
- **Cliff faces (leeward, plain):** warm pale limestone-into-marble (`#C8B89C`).
- **Mining scars (Bryn Mor east, Tor Galdoryn east):** raw rust-orange tailings (`#8E5430`), exposed white marble blast scars, dark soot from the smelter chimneys staining the surrounding rock and forest downwind.
- **Sea (outer):** standard deep blue (`#1E3A5F` to `#0E1F38`). Heavy swells.
- **Sea (harbor approaches):** slightly greener blue (`#2E5A6E` to `#1E4858`).
- **Architecture stone (colonial):** pale warm limestone for civilian buildings (`#D8CDB0`); white marble blocks for the Calderet fortress; weathered grey granite for the older lighthouses and the Archive.
- **Roof tiles:** muted reds and ochres on the colonial buildings (`#A85838`, `#C68A4E`); turf or slate on the older local cottages.

**Lighting:**
- Overcast is the default sky. Direct sunlight should feel earned — when it breaks through, the marble peaks blaze and the copper streaks on the cliffs flare a distinctive green.
- Storm states are the dominant mood — the player should remember this archipelago for its weather as much as for its silhouettes.
- Inside the forest, light is filtered, cool, and quiet.
- Inside the company fortresses, light is hard — torches, oil lamps, the orange of smelter fires. Every fortress should feel like a foreign body in the landscape.

**Visual focal beats** (what the player should remember):
1. The first sight of Tor Galdoryn's white peak from open water — the sailor's landmark
2. The Brothers spires of Bryn Mor with the smelter smoke trailing east from its scarred face
3. The Calderet fortress's white marble walls, alien in a landscape that has almost nothing else cut to those proportions
4. The Cairn at the summit of Marrow Holt's Pate, in early morning, with the whole archipelago laid out below in sea-mist
5. A storm rolling in from the west, breaking against Caer Aelynd's windward cliffs, while the leeward harbor stays just calm enough to keep the fishing boats afloat
