# Backstory: Seren

## What Seren Is

Seren is half-Aelorin — her mother Lirien was Aelthiren (an elf who chose mortality, severing themselves from the Aelthurion and accepting a human lifespan), her father a human healer from a village at the edge of the Aelorin forest. She is one hundred and eighty years old. She looks to human eyes like a woman in her mid-thirties, ageless in the way of the long-lived. She ages, but slowly — she will likely live another three hundred years if nothing kills her first.

She is nearly two thousand years younger than Aelthurion, a fact that matters more to her than to him. To him she is young and therefore urgent. To her, the gap is so vast it sometimes makes her feel like a child explaining something to a mountain.

## Her Mother, Lirien

Lirien Aelthiren had been an Aelorin for six hundred years before she chose mortality. She never gave Seren a full account of why. What Seren gathered across her childhood was that Lirien had held a position of significance in the Aelthurion — she had been involved in the aftermath of the Second Age unmaking, though in what capacity Lirien would not say — and that something in the long centuries following had made the immortal life feel like a burden she was no longer willing to carry.

Lirien settled at the forest edge, learned human customs with the patient precision of someone performing an anthropological exercise, married a man she came to genuinely love, and had Seren. She died when Seren was sixty-two — old age, by the standards of her shortened life, which did not make it easier.

Lirien left Seren a single locked box. Inside was a letter in High Aeluvain that took Seren eleven years to fully translate and another forty to fully understand.

## The Vethri-Glade Attack

When Seren was ninety-one, the village she was living in near the Vethri-Glade — one of the eight forest Glades — was attacked by a Naergrim warband. Not a raid. Something more purposeful: they were looking for someone, though Seren did not know that until later.

The village's healer, three farmers, and a child were killed. Seren survived by doing something she had never done deliberately before — she channeled her mother's Aelorin resonance, blunt and uncontrolled, in a way that stopped the warband long enough for survivors to flee into the Glade.

The intervention left her bedridden for two weeks. It also drew the attention of Aelthurion, who had sensed the resonance spike. He came to the Glade himself, which he rarely does. He sat with her for three days while she recovered. He did not teach her anything during those days. He asked her questions and listened to the answers, which was teaching of a kind.

Afterward, he told her that her mother had been a ward-keeper of the old binding. That the box's letter confirmed it. That the Naergrim's warband had been looking for Lirien's half-blood child because someone had told them such a child might carry a sensitivity to the Aeluvain they could use. He told her she had a choice: to disappear and protect herself, or to become someone the Dawnbringers could work with.

She chose the latter. She has not been completely sure it was the right choice for about eighty years and keeps choosing it anyway.

## The Dawnbringers Years

Seren has worked with the Dawnbringers for roughly eighty years — not as a field operative but as a scholar and sensitized observer. Her Aelorin heritage gives her a partial perception of Mordvar's reforming presence, different from Corvus's structural sense: she feels it more emotionally, as a grief or a weight, which is less useful for analysis but sometimes catches things he misses.

She has never told anyone in the Dawnbringers about her mother's specific role. She does not think it changes anything practical. She knows, privately, that she withholds it because it is the one thing about her origin that still belongs only to her.

## Character

Seren is warm but not soft. She has been alive long enough to have lost many people and to know exactly what that costs, and she no longer pretends it costs less than it does. She is direct, often uncomfortably so. She has a very long memory and a habit of noticing when people contradict something they said fifty years ago, which is disconcerting to those who forgot they ever said it.

Her wound is her mother's silence. Lirien left her a letter instead of a conversation, and Seren has spent a hundred years filling in the gaps alone. She is looking, without quite admitting it, for an elder she can actually talk to. Aelthurion is close, but Aelthurion's relationship to intimacy is complicated by two thousand years of watching people die.
