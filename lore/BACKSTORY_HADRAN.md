# Backstory: Hadran Volthen

**Role:** Master smith, last of the Long-Worked, Ashen Hand deep asset, keeper of Hadran's Forge in the Ash Tower
**Appears:** Game One (name only, in Edran's files); Game Two (named by Drossvik under interrogation); Game Three (present, confronted by Roland's party)

---

## Name

**Hadran** is an Aescryd-pattern given name — a rarity in the Third Age. Those who know Second Age naming conventions will recognize it for what it is before he says another word.

**Volthen** is the surname he chose when he abandoned his birth name. It means roughly "the one who finishes" in late Aescryd dialect. He chose it deliberately, decades into his work, when he became certain he would see it through. He answers to it without sentiment.

---

## Origins

Hadran was born approximately three hundred and forty years ago on the Sundered Isles — the shattered archipelago in the middle-north of the Shroud Sea that was once the island kingdom of Aescryd, the first human civilization.

He was born into one of the last Aescryd bloodline families that had stayed on the ruined home isles rather than fleeing to Mira after the drowning. These holdovers lived in the upper floors of half-submerged towers, fished the monster-haunted waters, and maintained what fragments of Aescryd knowledge they could salvage from the drowned archives below. Most of these families died out within four generations. Hadran's survived because they were smiths — the specific kind of smith the Aescryd kingdom had produced at its height: engineers of resonant metalwork, people who understood the relationship between material and intent.

A distant ancestor — sixty-some generations back on his father's line — had been on the team that forged the Sundered Crown for Bealoric. The knowledge passed down, imperfect and fragmentary, through the generations. Hadran inherited it.

---

## The Long-Worked: His Longevity

Hadran is human and mortal. He should not be alive at three hundred and forty years. The explanation is not magic. It is worse than magic.

After the drowning, the surviving Aescryd remnant attempted to reconstruct fragments of Bealoric's hollowing technique on themselves. They understood, imperfectly, that Bealoric's ritual had extended his existence by emptying his self out and replacing it with a self-sustaining engine of will. They believed they could apply a *partial* version of the same process — enough to extend life without losing selfhood.

There were eleven of these Long-Worked at the beginning. They called the practice the **Seconding**: a second life, purchased by giving up a measured portion of the first.

It works. It also kills, slowly, in the way any structural compromise kills: the building stands longer than it should, but the walls are thinner than they appear, and each decade takes a little more.

Of the original eleven Long-Worked, ten have died over the centuries. The partial hollowing, imperfectly applied to non-kings without Bealoric's specific relationship to the Crown, fails progressively. The selfhood thins. The will becomes erratic. Eight of the eleven degraded into something the survivors could not name and had to put down. Two simply stopped one morning and did not begin again.

Hadran is the last.

He is now approximately seventy percent of what he once was as a man. He knows this in the way a person knows their hearing has diminished — not as a crisis, just as a fact of the current world. What he has not fully acknowledged is what the thirty percent he has already given away *means*: he is closer to Mordvar than he admits. The belief that "Bealoric's diagnosis was correct" is not purely intellectual. It is partly the reasoning of someone who has already become Bealoric in slow miniature — someone whose self is already partly hollowed, who has already crossed a threshold he cannot uncross, and who needs the thesis to be true in order for what he has done to his own soul to have been worth it.

He does not lie about the Long-Worked when asked directly. He explains it carefully, in the same tone he uses for metallurgical problems. What he does not explain is that he understands, somewhere beneath the courtesy, that the process has compromised his judgment about whether the work should be finished.

---

## Belief

Hadran believes — sincerely, after two centuries of study, with the full weight of his remaining self behind it — that Bealoric was *right*.

Not that Bealoric's reign was good. He is not a fool. The army that crossed the Shroud Sea and killed a quarter of Mira's eastern population was a catastrophe. The hollowing of his own people was a horror. Hadran does not deny any of this.

What he believes is that Bealoric's *diagnosis* was correct: that mortality and grief, left unaddressed, breaks competent people into monsters. That the world as it is constituted asks too much of those who love, because everything they love will die. That Bealoric was not weak or evil for wanting this to stop — he was *accurate*, and the ritual he designed was a genuine solution, and the Sorrowmarsh strike was a panic-reaction by an Alliance that did not understand what it was destroying.

Hadran believes the Crown's working, completed properly this time — applied not to one frightened king but to the world's leadership broadly — can give the world what Bealoric tried and failed to give it: an end to the specific kind of loss that breaks competent kings into monsters.

He has spent two hundred years preparing to do the work right.

## Where He Works: Hadran's Forge and the Ash Tower

The **Ash Tower** is the largest surviving structure on the Sundered Isles — what remains of the Aescryd capital's central citadel. The lower three floors are flooded; the upper four are dry. The structure is built from Aescryd granite, which does not soften in saltwater the way lesser stone does. It has been standing for two thousand years and will stand for two thousand more.

Hadran's forge occupies the **fourth floor**, just above the permanent tide line. It is reached by small craft piloted at slack tide through a sea-cave below the Tower, which opens into a flooded inner courtyard; from there, iron rungs in the wall lead up to the fourth floor's stone doorway. The forge itself burns with a black-orange flame fed by Drûn-Khazad charcoal the Ashlord's supply chain brings east on black-sailed ships. The anvil dominates the center of the room. On its face, prepared and waiting, are seven shaped recesses — the sockets for the Sundered Crown's seven pieces, ready to receive them when they arrive.

Hadran has worked from this floor for ninety years. He does not leave the Tower.

**The Ash Tower as the Ashlord's command base:**

The Ashlord and his operation occupy the floors above:

- **5th floor — barracks.** Quarters for the human and Aelorin sympathizer corps that has gathered to serve the Ashlord directly. At peak perhaps eighty souls: former Conclave mages who lost their posts, second sons of minor noble houses who found the Ashen Hand's purpose compelling, two Aelorin who followed the Ashlord's ideology to its conclusion. They eat together, train on the beach below at low tide, and know exactly what they serve.
- **6th floor — war room.** The Ashlord's table. Maps of Mira and Thal cover the walls, marked with Hand cell locations, Crown piece last-known positions, Sailor's Guild shipping lanes, and Naergrim territory boundaries. Couriers arrive by the south cove and depart the same way. This is where the Ashlord plans his operations, receives reports, and issues orders.
- **7th floor — the Ashlord's quarters.** Spare. A single chair angled toward the window. The window faces west, toward Mira, across the Shroud Sea. He spends long hours there. What he thinks about is not recorded.
- **Roof — signal pyre and watch-post.** A goblin host musters on the longest beach below the Tower, organized by their own clan-captains under Ashlord instruction. Black-sailed Naergrim ships arrive in the south cove at intervals, bearing envoys and supplies. The signal pyre is lit when the Ashlord wants the fleet to move.

## The Sundered Crown — Specification

The Crown Hadran is preparing to receive and complete is described in the Aescryd forge-records he has spent two centuries reconstructing:

**Material.** Black gold — not blackened gold, but an alloy of true gold with a substance only Bealoric's original smiths knew how to produce. During the forging, portions of Bealoric's hollowed essence crystallized into the metal itself. The result is gold-weighted but light-eating: it swallows torchlight and returns nothing. Edges are visible only by the darkness behind them.

**Weight.** Heavy, but not crushing. A strong man lifts it one-handed. A king wears it.

**Form.** A circlet roughly a hand's depth, rising at the front into **seven irregular spiked peaks** — not symmetrical, not decorative; more like splinters of a once-greater whole. The tallest spike rises directly above the wearer's brow. The other six descend in height around the circumference, the shortest at the back.

**Script.** Two layers, etched into the outer band. The outer script is **ancient Aelorin** — Second Age sovereignty phrases, deliberately appropriated by Bealoric as a claim of authority over Aelorin domains. Beneath them, smaller and reading the wrong direction, are **demonic scripts**: the operative writings, the ones that performed the hollowing and gave Bealoric his sustained existence. The Aelorin script is camouflage. The demonic script is the work.

**Sockets.** The band contains seven recesses — one per spike — each shaped to receive a specific piece:

| Piece | Position | Function |
|---|---|---|
| The **Pommel** | Front spike (tallest) | Anchors the wearer's selfhood against the hollow |
| The **Coin** | Front-left spike | Counts the years of the wearer's reign |
| The **Ring** | Front-right spike | Keys the metal to the wearer's blood |
| The **Wire** | Left-rear spike | Threads Mordvar's corruption between sockets |
| The **Clasp** | Right-rear spike | Closes the working when complete |
| The **Disc** | Rear spike | Tunes the Crown to Drûn-Khazad |
| The **Obsidian Shard** | Center of the band | Keystone — without it, the others do nothing |

The seven sockets on Hadran's anvil are ready. They have been ready for thirty years.

## Mannerisms

Hadran is courteous. He does not raise his voice. When he speaks of Mordvar, he uses the name **Bealoric**, never the throne-name — the throne-name is a corruption, he says, of the man who existed before the ritual; he prefers to speak of the man.

He keeps a small sea-petrel in a wicker cage on his workbench and feeds it scraps from his own meals. He cooks his own food. He has a habit of running his thumb along the edge of any blade he discusses, even one he has never seen, as if he can feel its temper through the air.

When Roland's party reaches the Tower in Game Three, Hadran offers them tea. He answers their questions. He shows them the anvil and the waiting sockets. He explains, in plain language, what he intends to do. He does not lie.

He is the most frightening NPC in the trilogy because he is reasonable.

## The Convergence with Aldric Vane

Hadran possesses two centuries of reconstructed Aescryd metallurgical knowledge — the most complete understanding of the Crown's construction that exists outside the Crown itself. What he lacks is the bloodline: the Aeluvain, forged from Eighth Star material, was keyed to Aescryd blood by the Star-smiths who made it. Hadran is Long-Worked, hollowed at the margins, no longer fully human in the old sense. He cannot touch the Aeluvain without consequence.

Aldric Vane is the last of the Caelborn, the last living Aescryd bloodline. He has, through his line, an instinctive inheritance of the Star-smith tradition — not memory, but *recognition*. When placed before Aescryd metalwork, he knows what he is looking at. When given Hadran's notes and the damaged Aeluvain, he will know what to do.

Hadran has the knowledge. Aldric has the hands. Neither can finish the work without the other. This convergence — enemies in purpose, complementary in function — is one of Game Three's structural ironies: the party must take Hadran alive and extract his expertise for Aldric to use, which means both protecting and confronting the man who would have given Mordvar his body back.

## Arc Across Three Games

**Game One.** Off-screen entirely. He appears once: a name on a list Edran Vane has compiled of "the Hand's deepest assets." Edran has only the name and a notation — *smith, location unknown, Shroud Sea*. Roland may or may not see this list depending on Act III choices.

**Game Two.** Named concretely. During Drossvik's interrogation in Khorumzad, Drossvik — with nothing left to protect — reveals that the Crown pieces are not being collected for Vaeroth's use. They are being delivered east. To someone called the Smith. He does not know the name. He knows the destination is on the water. Vaeroth, in private dispatches the party intercepts, is dismissive of Hadran — he knows the plan requires the man and resents it.

**Game Three.** The party reaches the Ash Tower. Hadran is there. He does not flee or fight. He offers tea, answers questions, shows them the anvil. The choice: kill him and lose his knowledge, or take him alive and use it. In the **good ending** path, they take him alive, extract everything, and Aldric completes the reforging with Hadran's notes at hand. Hadran dies of old age within the year after the work is taken from him — the Long-Worked process unravels once the purpose sustaining it is denied. He dies on a Sailor's Guild ship the heroes give him, at sea, watching the Sundered Isles disappear behind him for the last time.
