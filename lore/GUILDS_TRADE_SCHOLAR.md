# Trade Guilds and Scholarly Orders

Cross-faction institutions that span the human kingdoms (and, for some, all peoples).

> **Index file.** Each entry below is a one-screen overview. Where a `**Deep history:**` link is present, the linked backstory file is the canonical source for founding details, internal politics, and trilogy arcs.

## Trade Guilds

### The Smiths' Confederation

**Oldest trade guild** in the human kingdoms. Only guild with formal affiliate chapters among **both** Aelorin and dwarves.

**Founder:** **Durak Ironwhisper**, a dwarven master-smith from Karaz-Dûn who retired four hundred years ago, lived among humans for sixty years teaching them to work iron properly because watching them do it badly was making him physically unhappy.

**Structure:** Six tiers of membership, from apprentice to **Grand Artificer**. Three living Grand Artificers — one human, one Aelorin, one dwarf. They meet every decade to argue about standards and refuse to agree on anything except that the previous generation's standards were insufficient.

**Politics:** Controls access to the highest-quality ore in the world. Every human king has at some point tried to bring the Confederation under crown control. None succeeded — the Confederation simply stops servicing that kingdom until the kingdom reconsiders. This has worked eleven times in three hundred years.

**The Ashsteel Secret:**
- **Ashsteel** is a metal that holds a better edge than anything currently produced and cannot be detected by Ash Throne magics
- The formula is in the Confederation's sealed archive
- Locked away sixty years ago after a member sold a batch to an Ashen Hand procurement agent disguised as a goblin buyer
- Reopening requires unanimous Grand Artificer vote
- Currently deadlocked: one Artificer (the nephew of the man who sold the original batch) votes no out of shame

**[OPTIONAL QUEST across all three games]:** Roland uncovers what happened, exposes the original betrayal publicly, and earns the trust of all three Grand Artificers separately. If completed by Game Three, Ashsteel weapons are distributed to key fighters before the final battle. They do not win the battle — but the people carrying them are alive at the end.

### The Sailor's Guild

Based in Caer Brannoch. Chapters in every port city on both coasts.

**Origin:** A mutual aid society — sailors pooling earnings into a fund that paid out to families of those who drowned. Evolved into the closest thing the world has to an international institution.

**Member benefits:**
- Reduced dock fees in member ports
- Guild safe-houses in foreign cities
- Access to the network of information that flows through every sailor who has ever crossed between coasts

**Absolute principle:** Information flows freely between members. Guild members do not report to crowns, do not carry messages that must be hidden from other sailors, and do not take contracts requiring them to sink another member's ship.

**Track record:** Thirty-one members expelled in Guild history. Twenty-nine for breaking the no-crown-service rule. The other two for reasons the Guild will not discuss.

**The Shroud Sea Charts:**
- The Guild has charted Shroud Sea storm patterns for two hundred years
- Believes a navigable window opens every forty years
- The next window opens **nine years** from Game One's start (revised from earlier estimates of eleven)
- Guild leadership is divided on what to do with this knowledge

**[SIDE QUEST] The Guild's Copper Isles Archive:** Access requires becoming a Guild-endorsed sailor, which requires completing one voyage with a Guild crew. Payoff: the Shroud window information and a Guild contact who can move people and intelligence across faction lines throughout the trilogy.

**Deep history:** see [BACKSTORY_SAILORS_GUILD.md](BACKSTORY_SAILORS_GUILD.md) — origin as a drowned-sailor mutual aid society, the three absolute principles, Council of Stewards structure, the safe-house network, the Ashen Hand's infiltration, and the Council's internal divide on the crossing question. For the Shroud Sea chart program specifically, see also [HISTORY_SHROUD_SEA.md](HISTORY_SHROUD_SEA.md).

### The Apothecaries and Surgeons' School (Solgrade)

**Not technically a guild** — closest thing the world has to a university.

**Founded** by a physician who survived the last great plague outbreak and spent thirty years writing down everything she learned, then started teaching others.

**Officially neutral.** Admits students from all races and factions. Has:
- A dwarven research wing (secretly funded by Thrarin) studying gold-hunger physiology
- An Aelorin loremaster occasional visiting program (the Aelorin find human medicine *crude but occasionally illuminating*)
- A six-year-running ethics committee on whether their battlefield wound research incentivizes the continuation of conflict (240 pages of inconclusive findings to date)

**Plot connection:** Game Two — Corvus Tane's environmental magic combined with the School's gold-hunger research provides the treatment that can [SIDE QUEST] save Bromrin's mind during the Khorumzad descent. Game Three — School-trained surgeons run the Alliance field hospitals during the final battle.

## Scholarly Orders

### The Conclave of the Unseen Hand (Eldermark)

**The only formal magic school** in the human kingdoms — though "school" implies more structure than exists.

**Description:** A loose collection of mages who have grudgingly agreed to share knowledge with each other. The agreement is maintained partly by the fact that mages who do not join tend to be found dead. The Conclave officially has nothing to do with this. Everyone understands the implication.

**Membership:** Sixty-three current members. Admission requires demonstrable **innate** magical ability. The Conclave does not teach magic — it teaches people who can already do magic to do it without destroying things they care about.

**Mortality rate:** Four members in their first year may not survive it — not from malice but from the statistical reality that learning advanced magic in concentrated doses from seven different teachers simultaneously is demanding on the human body.

**Internal threats:**
- Two current Conclave members are **Ashfallen** — compromised but not broken, working from the inside, or rationalizing. Unclear which. The Conclave does not yet know.
- Deeply afraid of the Ashen Hand and equally afraid of being confused with the Ashen Hand. This has caused three diplomatic incidents.

**Plot connection:** **Corvus Tane** (Roland's companion, joins between Game One and Two) is a third-year Conclave member. His arrival at the Conclave to report what he is sensing from the volcano triggers the events that drive him into the party. In Game Three, the Conclave commits its remaining loyal mages to the Battle of Drûn-Khazad as a counter-force to Vaeroth's Ashen Hand.

### The Loremaster's Archive (Eldermark)

**The largest written-knowledge repository** in the human kingdoms. Housed in a building in Aldenholt that has been continuously added to for three hundred years and now occupies an entire city block.

**Staff:**
- Forty full-time scholars
- Twelve archivists
- One woman named **Henrietta**, who has been there longer than anyone, knows where everything is, and refuses to create a written index because she *is* the index and she intends to remain indispensable

**Politics:** Maintains neutrality by charging equally high access fees to every kingdom and owing favors to none. Solgrade pays the most. Vosskara pays the least but consistently. The Aelorin Pilgrimage Circuit has a standing arrangement that allows Aelorin loremasters access without payment in exchange for occasional original-source contributions.

**Critical hidden asset:** The Archive maintains a complete genealogical record of Eldermark's noble houses going back to the Second Age. **Neither Henrietta nor any scholar has connected this record to Mordvar's bloodline.** The connection is there. Game One's research arc with Roland and Henrietta begins to make it.

**Plot connection (Game One opening):** Roland visits Henrietta to research the iron pommel he saw in the Iron Chalice chapel. She gives him notes she has been quietly compiling — a genealogical thread connecting an object matching the pommel to Second Age binding records. Within twenty-four hours she is dead, her quarters searched, and the original notes gone. Roland has only the duplicate she pressed into his coat at the door.

Her death is the first murder of the trilogy. It is what proves to Roland that the pommel matters and that hesitation will kill him.
