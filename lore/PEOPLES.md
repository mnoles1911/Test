# The Peoples of Mira-Thal

## The Kingdoms of Men

Humans are the youngest major people but the most numerous. Four distinct kingdoms cluster in Mira's central plains. They share the Ninefold Pantheon, common ancestry, and recurring wars with each other.

---

### Eldermark — The Heartland Kingdom

**Capital:** Aldenholt — the largest walled city in the world. Stone walls three men thick, population of roughly eighty thousand, a market district that never fully closes.

**Ruler:** King Othric IV, the Iron-Tongued. Age 63. Aging warrior-king, politically astute, physically diminished. Three rival sons: the capable-but-cold eldest, the decent-but-unremarkable middle son, and Prince Aedric (youngest), whose Ashen Hand connections are the trilogy's first major political crisis.

**Culture:** Anglo-Saxon flavor. Thanes, longhalls, the witenagemot (council of lords). Heavy infantry tradition. Worships Aldrath (war/endurance) and Aeadis (harvest) most prominently. Eldermark's people are proud, stubborn, and deeply suspicious of Solgrade's commercial influence.

**Wealth:** Greatest of the four kingdoms. Controls the Central Plains' best farmland and the major east-west trade road.

**Internal crisis:** Othric's succession is openly contested. The eldest son has the military loyalty; the middle son has the popular support; Aedric has Ashen Hand backing and a genuine (if catastrophically misguided) plan. This crisis is woven through all three games.

---

### Caer Brannoch — The Western Coastal Kingdom

**Capital:** Caer Brannoch — a cliff-fortress city carved into the western headland. The lower city sits at sea level; the upper city is accessible only by a single switchback road or the sea-lifts.

**Ruler:** Queen Eilwen the Grey. Widowed. The only female ruler among men. A former naval officer who came to the throne through a succession crisis of her own and has spent fifteen years proving she belongs there. Her court knows not to test this.

**Culture:** Welsh/Celtic flavor. Bards, druidic remnants at the rural level, bronze torcs as noble insignia, rain-soaked moors inland. Master sailors and shipwrights — Caer Brannoch builds the best ships in the world and controls the western trade lanes.

**The Shroud:** Eilwen's court-scholars have been studying the Shroud Sea's permanent storms for two generations. The Sailor's Guild's Shroud charts originate here. Eilwen believes a navigable window exists and opens every forty years. The next opens nine years from Game One's start.

**Tension:** Border disputes with Eldermark over fishing rights date back four generations. An old coastal massacre that neither kingdom officially acknowledges has never been resolved.

---

### The Vosskaran Despotate — The Frontier Kingdom

**Capital:** Vosskar-on-the-Iron — a fortress-city built into a river bend on the eastern frontier. Iron-banded walls, garrison culture, no wasted space.

**Ruler:** Despot Yaromir the Cold. Survived three assassination attempts. Governs through paranoia and competence in roughly equal measure. Slavic/eastern European flavor: fur cloaks, icon-worship of the Ninefold, brutal winters, a warrior culture that has been at war its entire history.

**Culture:** The shield of Mira. Vosskara's borders touch goblin lands. Its people are the hardest human soldiers alive because they have been fighting since they were old enough to hold weapons. They demand tribute from Eldermark for "holding the line," which is militarily justified and politically toxic.

**The Tribute War:** The ongoing dispute over what Vosskara is owed for frontier defense is the source of the most active human-on-human violence in Game One's world. Yaromir is not unreasonable — he is right that without Vosskara, Eldermark's eastern border would have collapsed decades ago. He is also inflexible about how he communicates this.

---

### The Free City of Solgrade — The Merchant Republic

**Capital:** Solgrade itself — a southern port city of terracotta roofs, banking houses, open markets, and hired swords. No king. Ruled by the **Council of Twelve Houses**, wealthy merchant families who have collectively decided that democracy is the most profitable form of government.

**Culture:** Italian/Mediterranean flavor. Banking, trade, contract law, mercenary armies. Controls the southern straits and the spice routes connecting Mira's south to the Copper Isles and beyond. Officially neutral in all conflicts. Secretly funds multiple sides of every conflict, because stability is expensive and instability is expensive differently.

**House Korvath:** The wealthiest of the Twelve Houses. They know the gold piece of the Sundered Crown is a piece of the Sundered Crown. They have been waiting for someone to come asking, at which point they intend to negotiate from the strongest possible position.

## The Aelorin — The Wood Elves

Single united people. Nearly immortal. Perhaps forty thousand total, declining. They inhabit the Greatwood and northern coastal forests. Ruled by **Lord Aelthurion the Sorrowful** and his consort **Lady Nimriel of the Singing Vale**.

### Three Cities

**Lirien-Thal** — "City of Silver Leaves." Capital. Built into the canopy of millennia-old silverwood trees. The trees are ancestral Aelorin who completed the Aelthiren — their hair became leaves, their bodies bark, over decades of transformation. Walking through Lirien-Thal is walking through a forest of ancestors.

**Aelorien** — "Vale of First Song." Religious center. Where the Aelorin first awoke under the stars. The Eight Glades nearest here are the most frequently visited. The Hall of the Vigil — where Aelorin who have begun the Aelthiren are tended before transformation completes — is here.

**Sirathiel-by-the-Sea** — "Where the Sea Sings Back." Coastal city of pale stone and mother-of-pearl inlay. The Aelorin here have the closest relationship with human traders. Sirathiel is the only Aelorin city where humans are regularly admitted without invitation.

### The Aelthiren

Aelorin who succumb to *aelnaeth* — the slow forgetting of why one ever cared, which comes to immortal beings as age comes to mortals — may choose the Aelthiren. They walk to one of the Eight Glades, lie at the roots of a silverwood tree, and slowly merge with the forest. Over decades: hair becomes leaves, body becomes bark, consciousness diffuses into the wood's vast slow dreaming.

The silverwoods are ancestors. The Aelorin tend them, speak to them, sing to them. Elves who approach a glade but cannot complete the Aelthiren surrender their name at the boundary (by custom) and live as **the Nameless** — reborn into new identities, operating in the world's grey spaces.

### Aelorin Culture

The Aelorin have a centuries-long outlook. They find human urgency alternately endearing and exhausting. Their fear is not death but *aelnaeth* — the slow loss of meaning across an immortal span. The word for this is one of the most dreaded in their language.

Pilgrimage between the Eight Glades is the central religious practice. Aelorin priests are loremasters who carry accumulated knowledge across centuries. Their political decisions can seem incomprehensibly slow to humans — an Aelorin lord considering a treaty may take years to decide, because years are a fraction of a percent of their expected lifespan.

Lord Aelthurion has been watching Mordvar's gathering strength for forty years through the Pilgrimage Circuit's intelligence network. He was not surprised by anything that happened in the trilogy. He knew it was coming. His grief is not for the world's danger but for the fact that he watched it approach for so long without being able to make humans and dwarves see it.

## The Three Dwarven Kingdoms

All three kings share descent from **King Brokkin Stonefather**, the dwarven founder-hero. Their realms are linked by the **Underway** tunnel network. The three kingdoms are close allies who come to each other's aid readily — more reliably than human kingdoms.

Dwarves live longer than humans (lifespans of three to four hundred years) but are shorter-lived than Aelorin. They are physically stocky, have excellent underground navigation, and build structures that outlast civilizations. Above ground they are adaptable but always faintly aware of the absence of stone overhead.

### Karaz-Dûn — The High Kingdom

**Location:** Northern Spine of the World. **Ruler:** King Thrarin Ironhand. The greatest hold — forges that have not gone cold in four thousand years. Master smiths whose work is recognized across both continents.

**Current crisis:** Thrarin has not left his treasury in two years. His gold-hunger has been artificially accelerated by **Drossvik**, the Ashen Hand mole in Bromrin's court who extended his operation to Karaz-Dûn through a compromised steward. A **Gold-Speaker auditor** documented the acceleration and had his report buried. He is now in protective custody in Kazaad-Brak, waiting for someone to ask him what he saw.

### Khorumzad — The Delving Kingdom

**Location:** Middle Spine. **Ruler:** King Bromrin Deepdelver. His realm tunnels deepest of all — nine layers from the Underdark. Pious, beloved, known for his personal humility despite commanding the most extensive underground infrastructure in the world.

**Current crisis:** Bromrin has been slowly poisoned into gold-hunger by Drossvik, his senior advisor and the Ashen Hand's primary inside agent in the dwarven kingdoms. His diggers, following the deepening gold-hunger's obsessive drive, broke through to the Ash Hearth chamber below Khorumzad and then to the **Vault of Aen-Vael** below that. Since the vault seal was disturbed, the gold-hunger has spread from Bromrin outward through the ambient magic contaminating the hold's atmosphere.

### Kazaad-Brak — The Warden Kingdom

**Location:** Southern Spine. **Ruler:** King Hagrim Bloodbeard. The southernmost hold, closest to goblin lands. Besieged eleven times in Hagrim's lifetime. Aging, no confirmed heir, throne contested by two cousin-clans who have been more or less openly hostile to each other for a decade.

**Hagrim's strength:** The most militarily experienced dwarven king. His soldiers are the best in the Underway because they have been in continuous conflict their entire careers. When the call comes for the final battle, Hagrim does not wait for a council vote.

## The Shadow — Mordvar and the Ash Throne

### Mordvar (the Enduring)

**Origin:** Born Bealoric, High King of the Aescryd (the First Human Kingdom) in the Second Age. A capable, beloved ruler who watched his kingdom slow and crumble and became consumed by a single fear: death. He sought immortality through a ritual that required emptying himself — surrendering the core of his selfhood, the thing that makes beings mortal, in exchange for a form that contains nothing and therefore cannot die.

He got what he wanted. He became hollow. His kingdom became a death-engine. The Grand Alliance — Aelorin, dwarves, and the remaining uncorrupted humans — wielded the **Aeluvain** — a sword forged by the Aelorin from the material of the fallen Eighth Star — and used it to unmake him at the **Sorrowmarsh**. He was unmade there two thousand years ago, his scattered essence slowly reforming.

Two thousand years later, Mordvar reforms. He cannot yet act directly. He acts through the Aescstól (Ash Throne), through the Ashlord, through the Ashen Hand and the Hosts.

**Physical presence:** Mordvar emerges from Drûn-Khazad's caldera on the night of the final battle's third day. He is tall and grey-pale, dressed in something that was once clothing and is now indistinguishable from his skin. He does not roar or speak or raise his arms. He simply begins walking down the slope. His presence is a cold of *will*, not temperature. Men who were fighting moments before find their arms heavy.

**His tragedy:** He was a man. He was afraid. What he did came from ordinary human fear of death — the same fear that wakes people at three in the morning. Understanding this does not make him less dangerous. It does make the question of how to end him more complicated.

### The Aescstól — The Ash Throne

An obsidian throne streaked with silver, forged from cooled lava and the bone-meal of Mordvar's first sacrifices, in the Ash Hearth within Drûn-Khazad. Mordvar's will flows through it into whoever sits upon it.

The current occupant is the **Ashlord** — faceless even to his own forces, Mordvar's will made partially present. He wears a featureless obsidian mask and has not been seen without it in two thousand years. His own inner circle believes he was always this way — a vessel built for the purpose, not a person converted to it. They are wrong.

**The Ashlord is Caerith**, formerly an ancient Aelorin loremaster who served as Sorrowmarsh Vigil-Keeper after the Grand Alliance's unmaking of Mordvar. Stationed at the Sorrowmarsh to watch for any resurgence of Mordvar's scattered essence, Caerith was instead slowly corrupted by that same essence seeping through the ruined ground over centuries. Mordvar's residual will — diffuse, patient, still reaching — took what it could while Caerith kept his long vigil. Not enough to destroy him — enough to turn him. Eventually Caerith abandoned the Sorrowmarsh vigil entirely and walked east to Drûn-Khazad, drawn by Mordvar's slowly reconstituting presence, and took his place upon the Ash Hearth.

Two thousand years of that. More hollow than the Ashfallen, less hollow than Mordvar himself. A being trapped between states, the instrument of the will he spent his life opposing, with just enough of himself left to know it.

Lord Aelthurion knows. He has known since the Second Age. Caerith's name is not spoken in Aelorin society. In the Archive at Lirien-Thal he is recorded as having completed the Aelthiren, because the alternative requires explaining what he became. The mask is worn because beneath it is an Aelorin face — and the Naergrim, who are themselves Aelorin-derived, would recognize what they were looking at.

**Symbol:** A black sun with seven rays. One for each Host. The seventh ray is vacant — reserved for Mordvar.

### The Hosts of the Ash Throne

**The Gulmrak Horde** — Sixty thousand orcs. Currently led by **Warlord Uglakh the Iron-Crowned**, who killed his predecessor (Drûn-Aghul) after the chaos following Game One's climax. Religious devotion to the volcano — they consider it their god's mouth and fight its defense with the fury of the righteous. They dislike light, prefer raw meat, and hate the smell of cooked food above all things. Their war-drums sound like the earth thinking.

**The Goblin Warrens of Vrothmor** — Eighty thousand goblins. Current king: **Kogul the Eight-Fingered**, eleven years in power — unprecedented stability for goblin politics, which normally sees kings killed within a year. Bound to the Ash Throne by terror. Individually weak, collectively a flood. They swarm, infiltrate, vanish underground and reappear unexpectedly. The Alliance commanders are more afraid of the goblins than the orcs.

**The Black Court / Naergrim** — Dark elves who chose blood-magic immortality when their Aelorin kin chose the stars. Five hundred elite fighters under **Serethi the Twice-Dead**, who survived his own partial Aelthiren attempt and returned wrong. Built into Mor-Vethrin, the obsidian city in the Weeping Wood escarpment of northeastern Mira. Bound to the Ash Throne by will — they chose this freely, unlike the orc and goblin forces. A minority faction within the Naergrim questions this choice but has not acted on the question.

**The Ashen Hand** — Turned mages. Hierarch: **Vaeroth the Pale**, former Conclave mage of Eldermark origin who sold his allegiance to the Ash Throne a decade before the trilogy begins. Bound by gift — the Ash Throne amplifies their power beyond what they could wield otherwise. They spread plague-blights, summon forbidden things, and serve as Mordvar's most intelligent agents. Two hundred mages at peak strength.

**The Ashfallen** — Humans, dwarves, and elves who were captured and returned wrong — hollowed just enough to serve. Three thousand distributed through the Ash Throne's forces. The hardest to fight because they wear familiar faces. A soldier freezes for a second recognizing someone; a second is enough.

## The Great Wounds

Sites where the world was broken and has not healed. These are not myths — they are current geography.

### The Sorrowmarsh

Eastern Mira, where the Grand Alliance broke Mordvar's first host two thousand years ago. The battle's dark magic was concentrated here when Mordvar's forces were finally routed. Nothing has grown since. The wetland exists but produces only grey sedge and fen-moss that smells wrong. Ghost-lights appear at night — small, cold, blue-white, moving with apparent purpose. The lights do not respond to approach. No one has successfully followed one.

### Caer Drowned — Formerly Caer Sulwyn

The jewel of Caer Brannoch's coast for three centuries. One night — the same night Mordvar is believed to have made his first significant move toward reforming — the coastal cliffs sheared into the sea. The upper city slid in sections. Parts of the towers still stand at wrong angles under grey water, visible at low tide. The harbor's shallows are navigable; the deep sections are too dangerous to dive.

The seas changed the same night. The Shroud Sea's permanent storms began within the week. Weather patterns along the entire western coast shifted and have not shifted back.

### The Weeping Wood

A section of ancient forest north of the goblin lands that was the site of a major Second Age battle involving dark magic of a kind the records do not clearly describe. The wood died. Trees stand but do not leaf, do not rot, do not fall — suspended in a moment of dying that never completes.

Voices speak in the trees. Not wind through branches — articulate voices, sometimes recognizable as specific people who entered and did not return. The voices repeat last words, fragments of conversations, names. Extended exposure causes dissociation and eventually permanent personality change. The **Nameless Council** maintains one operative who can enter and return; they have never explained how.

### Drûn-Khazad — The Burning Wound

The great volcano, Thal's heart. Active and worsening as Mordvar's essence slowly reconstitutes. Lava flows that were stable for centuries have shifted. The ash zone extends further west each decade. Orc theology holds that the volcano is their god's mouth and its increasing activity is their god's growing appetite — good news, to them.

### The Shroud Sea

Not a wound in the physical sense but a consequence of wounds. The eastern ocean locked in permanent storm since Caer Sulwyn's fall. Waves that come from nowhere, storm systems that move against the prevailing wind, weather that kills ships built for open ocean. The Sailor's Guild believes the storms are a seal — that something was put on the other side and the storms are the lock. The Guild's most senior members are divided between those who want to know what it is and those who believe not knowing is the correct response.
