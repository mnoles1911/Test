# Backstory: Mordvar (Bealoric of Aescryd)

## Before the Hollowing

His name was Bealoric. He was born on Aescryd — the island kingdom in the Shroud Sea, oldest of the human civilizations — and he came to its throne several generations after the kingdom's founding, inheriting an institution already capable of outlasting any one man's failures.

By most accounts his early reign was not cruel. He was a consolidating king. He brought the island's factions into alignment, built out trade infrastructure, and expanded the order of engineers that bore the island's name — the Aescryd — into a corps capable of work on the continental scale. He held audiences personally rather than delegating. He asked precise questions. He remembered names.

He was not loved the way celebrated kings are loved in songs. He was respected, which is rarer and harder to manufacture.

He was not a king of Thal. Thal was wilderness — unmapped beyond its fringes, dominated by no throne he recognized, and not part of any calculation he made until much later in his life. The eastern continent was simply the direction from which certain things came and into which, eventually, his armies would go.

His wife Sael died after twenty-six years of marriage. An illness that moved quickly — eleven days between the first fever and the end. He had adult children by then, both capable. He did not break visibly. He worked harder instead, which is a kind of breaking.

## The Hollowing — His Own Choice

There was no entity that found him. There was no voice from beneath a volcano. There was a king who had lost something irreplaceable, who had built his life around competence and administration and the management of problems, and who turned that same machinery toward a problem it was not suited to solve.

He spent several years working through fragments of old Aescryd lore — the kingdom's scholars had written extensively on the nature of the self, the persistence of identity, what it might mean for will to survive the dissolution of the body. He was empirical and methodical. He took notes. He was, as he had always been, precise.

He understood what the ritual would do. That is what makes it worse.

The hollowing was designed to empty him of his mortality — to strip the parts of himself that aged, that grieved, that could be worn down by loss. He saw this as a fortification. What he could not fully account for, because no one could, was that grief is not a contaminant in the self. It is structural. Remove it and you do not have a stronger version of the same person. You have a different person shaped like the original.

The ritual worked. It emptied him of his mortality and of his grief and of most of what had made him Bealoric. What remained was the will to endure, stripped of everything it had originally wanted to endure for.

He was not deceived. He was not coerced. He chose this, with full understanding of the mechanism and incomplete understanding of the cost. The distinction matters, though it does not excuse anything.

## The Sundered Crown

The ritual produced something material — an object the Aescryd chronicles call the Sundered Crown, though they do not fully understand what they are describing.

It was not made by smiths. It was not commissioned or designed as a separate project. It emerged from the ritual itself as the crystallized material form of a hollowed will — the shape of what Bealoric had been, made solid and permanent. It is him, in a meaningful sense. It is also what can destroy him.

The Crown is black gold: not blackened or tarnished gold, but an alloy of true gold with a substance only Aescryd smiths knew how to produce, the process for which did not survive the kingdom's drowning. Seven irregular spiked peaks rise from the band, asymmetrical in the way of something grown rather than crafted. The script work runs in two layers: an outer ring in ancient Aelorin, reading as a claim of sovereignty; beneath it, running the wrong direction, a demonic script that constitutes the operative working of the ritual itself.

He chose the name Mordvar himself. Old Aescryd dialect: "the one who endures." He was still making meaningful choices when he named himself. He was not yet whatever he would become. He was a man who had surgically removed the parts of himself that might have stopped him, and who was still, in the aftermath, capable of something like deliberateness.

## The Aescryd's Decline

Bealoric's engineers had built their order on empirical methods and careful record-keeping. These were professional habits he had encouraged — they were what made the Aescryd competent, and competence was what he valued.

After the hollowing, that same competence became a liability. Empirical record-keepers eventually notice things. The Aescryd noticed. Slowly, and in isolated ways, and without any single person seeing the whole picture — but they noticed.

The purge was gradual. Over twenty years, Mordvar replaced the Aescryd's leadership with people who asked fewer questions. The accountability mechanisms Bealoric had originally written into the order's charter were removed one by one, each removal individually defensible on administrative grounds. The engineers who remained were technically skilled and institutionally obedient, which is a different kind of institution than the one Bealoric had built.

By the time the Aescryd of Mordvar's later campaigns was a militarized engineering corps, almost nothing of the original institution remained except the name and the technical skill. This is one of the facts the Aelorin find most instructive about what Mordvar became: his first sustained act after the hollowing was to dismantle the thing most like himself.

This is also one of the details that will matter to Dagna. The Aescryd's later records — the ones Mordvar controlled — contain something important about the Spine of the World that Bealoric's original engineers documented before the purge removed them.

## The Grand Alliance and the Sorrowmarsh

The war Mordvar brought to the western continent Mira was not quick. His armies moved through the eastern territories and into the Spine's shadow over years, and the response assembled slowly — the way responses to something that has no precedent always assemble slowly, because people spend time arguing about whether it is as bad as it appears.

The Grand Alliance was Aelorin, dwarven, and uncorrupted human forces fighting together on ground they had never needed to share before. They held Mordvar's advance and pushed it back across eastern Mira over several years of campaign. The Aelorin contributed the Aeluvain — a sword forged from the material of the fallen Eighth Star, made for this purpose at a cost the Aelorin do not discuss in detail.

The final engagement was at the Sorrowmarsh: a wetland in eastern Mira, chosen for reasons of terrain and supply that made sense at the time. It has been dead and haunted for two thousand years since. The name came after.

A warrior named Aelric Caelborn — of Aescryd blood, ancestor of the man who would eventually be called Aldric Vane — carried the Aeluvain into the final engagement. He dealt the blow that unmade Mordvar. The Sundered Crown shattered. Seven pieces scattered across the field and beyond it.

Aelric did not survive the strike. The Aeluvain was taken by the surviving Aelorin commanders to the Vault of Aen-Vael, beneath Khorumzad in the Spine of Mira, where it has remained.

When the Crown shattered, Aescryd island began to drown. The connection between the Crown and the island's stability had not been clearly understood before this became apparent. The kingdom sank within a generation. What remains are the Sundered Isles — rock fragments and shallow water and the ruins of something that was once the first human civilization.

## After the Unmaking

Mordvar was not sealed. He was not imprisoned. He was unmade — scattered into his constituent essence, dispersed across the world in a form too diffuse to act.

He has been reforming for two thousand years. The process is not linear. The first eighteen centuries accomplished less than the last two combined. The reforming is exponential, and the curve has been steepening.

He is not yet present enough to act directly. He works through what has accreted around his dispersed will — the Ashen Hand, the theology of endurance, the slow recovery of the Crown's pieces. The Ashlord Caerith is the one who plans and executes; Mordvar is the goal, the shape of what they're working toward, the figure at the end of a long calculation.

The Sundered Crown's pieces are not the source of his power. They are the mechanism of his reassembly. The pieces are a lock, and assembling them is what completes his return. This is what the Ashen Hand is collecting. This is why the pieces matter.

No one living in the age of Game One has seen him. He is legend, then history, then legend again. The Dawnbringers know his name. The Aelorin remember. Most people in the human kingdoms know him as a story they half-believe.

## What Remains of Bealoric

He wanted Sael back. That is still in him, somewhere — the way the shape of a broken vessel is still present in the shards. He will never have her back. The ritual removed the part of him that could have accepted this.

He wants to have been right. A man who suspects he made a catastrophic error and cannot afford to examine the suspicion will spend a great deal of effort not examining it. Mordvar has had two thousand years of dispersed, reforming, partial consciousness in which the alternative to not-examining has been simply not-existing, which is its own kind of answer.

He is not a monster who forgot he was human. He is a human whose choices compounded into catastrophe over a long enough timescale that the original human is genuinely hard to find in the result. This is not an excuse. It is a shape, and understanding the shape is part of what must eventually be understood.

The Aescryd scholars who first documented the hollow of Mordvar concluded that the ritual's deepest flaw was not that it failed. It was that it worked.

## The Grain of the World

The Aelorin speak of something they call "the grain" — the deep structure of the world, the direction in which things are meant to move. Magic that goes with the grain costs the user but leaves the world intact. Magic that goes against the grain thins the world itself, the way forcing wood against the grain splinters it.

The hollowing was the most extreme going-against in recorded history. Not because of its scale — Mordvar's armies did greater immediate damage — but because of its nature. The ritual did not destroy things in the world. It altered what the world was willing to sustain.

Every year of Mordvar's reforming presence has made the thinning worse. This is not a metaphor. The Ashfields' deadness is not the aftermath of battle — the land has been scrubbed of something it cannot grow back. The Sorrowmarsh's haunting is not grief persisting in human memory. The Shroud Sea's permanent storm has not abated in two thousand years and will not abate.

The Aelorin do not know whether the world can recover. They know that Mordvar's full return — the Crown reassembled, his essence reconstituted — would be a second going-against that the world's grain might not survive intact. They do not say this plainly. They say it in the way they speak about the Eighth Star, and what it cost to bring it down, and why the material was used as it was used rather than kept.

The sword in the vault beneath Khorumzad is not a weapon. It is the answer to a question that should never have been asked. The question is being asked again.
