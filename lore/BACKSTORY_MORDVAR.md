# Backstory: Mordvar (Bealoric the Ash King)

## Before the Hollowing

His name was Bealoric. He ruled the eastern continent Thal for thirty years before the thing inside him finished taking root, and by most accounts those thirty years were not cruel ones. He was a consolidating king — he brought three warring coastal territories under a single administration, established trade routes into the interior that reduced the famine cycles that had plagued the Thal fringe settlements, and founded the order of engineers called the Aescryd, who built the infrastructure that made the eastern continent navigable for the first time in living memory.

He was not loved the way celebrated kings are loved in songs. He was respected, which is rarer. People who served in his court in those years describe a man who slept little, asked precise questions, and had no patience for ceremonial excess. He held audiences personally rather than delegating. He remembered names.

His wife Sael died in the twentieth year of his reign. An illness that moved quickly — she was sick for eleven days and then she was not there. They had been married for twenty-six years. He had two adult children by then, both capable. He did not break visibly. He worked harder instead, which is a kind of breaking.

## The Hollow Throne's First Contact

The entity beneath Drun-Khazad had been testing boundaries for decades — small influences, atmospheric, the kind of thing that makes a person's dreams heavier without their being able to say why. Bealoric was not specially vulnerable. He was simply the most powerful person within reach who had recently experienced a wound the entity could use.

The contact was not dramatic. It did not announce itself. It began as an intuition: that grief was a solvable problem, that the thing taken from him had a location, that a king with sufficient will could retrieve it. The intuition arrived dressed in his own voice, his own logic. He did not recognize it as external for nearly four years.

By the time he understood that the thoughts were not entirely his, the hollowing had already proceeded far enough that the understanding came with a second intuition: that stopping now would accomplish nothing. The thing was already in him. He could fight it and lose himself in the fighting, or he could use it and become something that could not be used in return.

This was not true. But it was persuasive, and he was tired.

## The Hollowing Ritual

The ritual was Bealoric's own design, built from fragments the entity fed him over three years. It was not submission — he understood it as a fortification, a way of armoring the self against further intrusion by filling the vulnerable spaces deliberately rather than leaving them open to be filled from outside.

What it actually did was complete the entity's access while leaving Bealoric's cognitive architecture intact. He retained his memories, his reasoning, his self-conception. The entity gained a vessel that could act in the world with full human plausibility because it was, in every measurable sense, still mostly human. Bealoric did not disappear. He was incorporated.

The name Mordvar came from an old Thal dialect word meaning roughly "the one who endures." He chose it himself, which is the detail that most disturbs the Dawnbringers when they piece together this history. He was still making meaningful choices. He still had preferences. He was not a puppet. He was a partnership in which one partner had systematically misrepresented the terms.

## The Aescryd's Decline

The engineers' order Bealoric founded did not survive his transformation intact. In the early years, Mordvar maintained it — the infrastructure work was useful, the loyalty of the engineers was a resource. But the Aescryd's culture was empirical and record-keeping, and empirical record-keepers eventually notice things.

The purge was gradual. Over twenty years, the Aescryd's leadership was replaced with people who asked fewer questions. The order's charter was revised to remove the accountability mechanisms Bealoric had originally written in. By the time the Aescryd of Game 1's era is a militarized engineering corps serving the Hollow Court's infrastructure needs, almost nothing of Bealoric's original institution remains except the name and the technical skill.

This is one of the details Dagna will eventually understand is connected to what happened in Khorumzad. The Aescryd's later records — the ones the Hollow Court controls — contain something important about the Spine of the World that Bealoric's engineers documented before the purge.

## What Mordvar Wants

The entity's goal is the dissolution of the Aeluvain binding. Mordvar's goal — to whatever extent Bealoric's original desires survive — is harder to name. He wants Sael back, which is impossible, and the entity has kept that want alive as a motivating pressure without ever delivering on it. He wants to have been right, which is the specific need of a man who suspects he made a catastrophic error and cannot afford to examine the suspicion.

He is not a monster who forgot he was human. He is a human who made a series of defensible choices — each one reasonable given his position and grief — that compounded into catastrophe. This is not an excuse. It is a shape, and understanding the shape is part of what the party must do.

## The Aescstol Crown

The Aescstol Crown was built in Bealoric's later years as an energy receiver — designed to channel Drun-Khazad's volcanic output into the binding network that will free the Hollow Throne when the Aeluvain finally breaks. Mordvar built it without knowing it had a second function: the Grand Alliance, when they designed the Aeluvain binding, included a kill-switch drawing on the same volcanic source. The Crown, placed on a specific anchor-point, can complete or collapse the Hollow Throne's connection.

This is what the party will find in Game 3. The weapon and the threat are the same object.
