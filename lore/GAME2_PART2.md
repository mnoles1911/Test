# Game Two: The Singing Vault — Acts III & IV

---

## Act III: The Vault and the Star

### [MAIN QUEST] Inside the Vault of Aen-Vael

The vault is a chamber that should not be possible — larger inside than the approaches suggest, with walls that curve in directions that do not resolve. The stone is pale blue-white, faintly luminous.

The Aeluvain is in the center: a sword, pale blue-white, cold to the touch. It does not glow — it drinks the light in the room and returns nothing. The blade's edges are defined only by the darkness behind them. It is propped upright, hilt toward the door, as if placed by someone who expected it to be retrieved.

**Seren's response:** She hears it. Not words — a frequency the world is missing, which her senses have been noting as absence since the beginning of Game Two. She walks to it before Roland does. She does not touch it. She stands close to it for a long time, then says: *The Eighth Star did not fall. It chose to stay.*

**What the Aeluvain is:** The sword is not magic in the conventional sense. It is a piece of the world's original song given edge and weight — forged from the material of the fallen Eighth Star by Aelorin smiths two thousand years ago. It cannot simply be used as a weapon; it can only be returned to where it belongs. Wielded by someone of Mordvar's bloodline — the last of the Caelborn — against Mordvar himself, it completes the note that was silenced when the Eighth Star fell and the Crown was made.

It is also, Seren observes quietly, deeply patient. It has been here for two thousand years. It is not in a hurry.

**The cost of carrying it:** The Aeluvain is not dangerous to carry, but it is uncomfortable. Corvus, who is the most magically sensitive human in the party, says it feels like standing next to something grieving. Seren says: *Yes. That is accurate.*

Roland takes it. He is the least sensitive to its presence — another small mercy for a party already carrying enough.

---

### The Hard Choice — Bromrin

Deep in the descent, before they found the Vault, Bromrin had a clear moment and asked Roland to promise: if the treatment fails and Bromrin becomes something unrecognizable, Roland will not let the others watch his dignity slip.

On the ascent, Bromrin has a difficult episode — the worst yet. He does not recognize Darna. He demands gold he has already been given. He becomes frightened of the descent party and draws his belt knife.

Roland keeps his promise. He gets between Bromrin and the others, speaks to him quietly, and waits. Bromrin's clarity returns after six minutes. Bromrin sheathes the knife without acknowledging what just happened.

Corvus, who overheard the original promise and watched this play out, says nothing. Later, alone, he tells Roland: *I did not think you would actually do it.* Roland says: *I made a promise.* Corvus says: *Yes. That is not what I meant.*

## Act IV: The Siege and the Exit

### [MAIN QUEST] The Ashen Hand Siege

While the party was descending, the Ashlord's forces completed the cordon and began the siege. Khorumzad's outer gates are holding — the hold is built for this — but the eastern approach is cut off.

Darna's military assessment: the hold can sustain a siege for eighteen months. They cannot sustain the contamination long-term, even with the second agent neutralized. They need to clear the siege.

**The relief fleet option:** Caer Brannoch's naval commitment from Game One is relevant here — Eilwen's ships can land forces on the eastern coast near the Spine's eastern foot and march inland. This is the plan Roland sends word for via the Sailor's Guild's communication network.

**The timing problem:** The relief fleet takes six weeks to mobilize and sail. The Ashlord knows the fleet is coming. He will attempt to take what he wants from Khorumzad before the fleet arrives.

---

### [MAIN QUEST] The Ashlord's Gambit

The Ashlord does not attack the gates directly. He sends a message: give him the Aeluvain and the Crown pieces, and the siege lifts. He is precise in his terms. He offers to let Bromrin's people leave.

He knows they will refuse. He is buying time for a different operation — a tunnel approach through Level 7 that his people have been digging for three weeks.

The tunnel approach is discovered by Orion (whose background in finding hidden passages and exits proved relevant again) and Seren (who can sense the displacement of old stone). The defense of the tunnel breach is the climax of Act IV — a close, confined fight in the lower levels with poor light and limited space, exactly the environment Tidewarden-style combat was designed for.

The tunnel fails. The Ashlord does not withdraw.

---

### [SIDE QUEST] The Nameless Council's Price

The Nameless Council operative who helped Roland reach Khorumzad through the blockade delivers their price: Roland must carry a message to someone in Lirien-Thal. The message is sealed. The operative says only: *She will know what it means.*

In Game Three, this message is one of the threads that reveals more of the Aelorin's longer history with the Sundered Crown — and explains why Aelthurion was watching for someone to come asking for forty years.

### Game Two Climax — The Unmasking

Eilwen's relief fleet arrives ahead of schedule — Orion provided the Guild with a route through the storm-window that cut two weeks from the standard approach. The fleet lands forces at the Spine's eastern foot and marches for Khorumzad.

The Ashlord is now trapped. His army is between the hold's defenders and Eilwen's advancing force. He does not have the numbers to fight both simultaneously. He has perhaps twelve hours before the position collapses.

He enters Khorumzad himself — through the tunnel his people dug, alone, having sent his remaining forces east. This is not surrender. It is a last attempt. He is going for the Aeluvain and the Crown pieces directly.

The party meets him in the lower levels — Level 8, the sealed approach, where the air is old and does not move. He is still masked. He is still formidable, despite the wounds from the Ashfields. The fight is real and costly.

He is cornered, finally, in the antechamber before the Vault door. Eilwen's forces are entering the upper gates above. The tunnel is blocked. There is nowhere left.

He removes the mask.

Beneath it is an Aelorin face — angular, old in the way only the very long-lived are old, with eyes that have seen too many things outlive their context. He looks at Seren first. She goes still.

He says his name. *Caerith.* Not the Ashlord's name — his name, the one from before the mask, the one Aelthurion recorded as departed two thousand years ago.

He gives the party three things in the time remaining:
1. The location of a secondary document cache in the Ash Tower — Hadran's forge notes, which Aldric will need
2. The confirmation that the Ashlord's civilian network is intact and will need to be dismantled cell by cell; there is no decapitation strike that ends it
3. A message for Aelthurion, spoken, not written: *He was right about the patience. He was wrong about what I would need it for.*

He dies of his wounds before Eilwen's soldiers reach the lower level. He does not ask for anything. He sits with his back against the Vault door and does not begin again.

**Game Two ends** with the Aeluvain secured, Caerith/the Ashlord dead, Bromrin stable and beginning slow recovery, and the party carrying the sword, the seven Crown pieces, and Caerith's final intelligence — en route to find Aldric Vane in Coldstoke and then, eventually, the Ash Tower.

## Game Two: Summary of Consequences

**Acquired:**
- The Aeluvain (sword forged from Eighth Star material; wielder must be of Mordvar's bloodline)
- Caerith's final intelligence: Ash Tower cache location, civilian network warning, Aelthurion's message
- Knowledge of the Gulmrak mobilization (Uglakh is done waiting)

**Bromrin's state:** Recovering. Will not command at the final battle — Darna leads Khorumzad's forces. Bromrin writes a letter to the assembled Alliance before the battle that Darna reads aloud. It is short.

**Corvus:** Has been changed by the descent. The frequency he felt in the Vault — standing next to something grieving — has recalibrated how he understands his own power. He does not discuss this until Game Three.

**Seren:** Heard Caerith's name at his unmasking and has not spoken of it since. Something in her relationship to the Vigil-Keepers' history has been disrupted. She will not explain what until Game Three.

**Orion:** Passed something in the descent. He does not say what it was. He is quieter afterward. Not damaged — older.

**Permanent losses:**
- Caerith / the Ashlord (dead, lower levels of Khorumzad; unmasked)
- One of Bromrin's personal guard (Ashfallen hesitation, Level 7)
- Three Khorumzad soldiers killed in the tunnel breach defense
- Drossvik (imprisoned; his eventual fate is left implicit)

**State entering Game Three:**
- Roland holds all seven Crown pieces and the Aeluvain
- Aldric Vane must be located and brought to the Ash Tower, where Hadran's craft is the only way to reforge the Aeluvain for its wielder
- The Hand's civilian intelligence network is intact; dismantling it is a Game Three background objective
- Mordvar is close to full reformation; the Shroud Sea window is narrowing
