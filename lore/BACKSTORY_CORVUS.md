# Backstory: Corvus Tane\n\n## Origins: Solgrade Merchant Family\n\nCorvus was born in Solgrade, the largest trading city-state in the south, to a family that had been in the import business for three generations. The Tanes were not aristocratic but were comfortable — a townhouse near the dockside market, a small warehouse, two clerks, a name that meant something to the right people. Corvus's father Daven managed accounts. His mother Ysel handled supplier relationships and was the sharper of the two. They expected Corvus to join the business.\n\nHe was good at the work. He had a memory for numbers, a facility for reading people, and patience with the slow arithmetic of negotiation. He was also, from his early teens, hiding something.\n\n## The Manifestation\n\nAt fourteen, Corvus's resonance emerged without warning during a warehouse inventory. He was alone, checking a crate manifest by candlelight, when the candle went out. He reached for a taper to relight it — and the light came back, sourceless, sitting in his palm like a coin.\n\nHe held it for a long moment. Then he closed his fist and it was gone.\n\nHe told no one. He practiced in secret for two years, learning the shape of what he could do through trial and error and two near-disasters he managed to contain. What he discovered was an environmental sensitivity: he could feel ambient conditions with unusual precision — temperature gradients, pressure shifts, the charge building in storm air before lightning. He could nudge these conditions, slightly and at cost. He could sense when weather was about to change before any natural sign confirmed it.\n\nThis was the surface of his ability. It was not all of it.\n\n## Discovery and the Conclave\n\nAt sixteen, a junior Conclave assessor passing through Solgrade on routine census work tested Corvus as part of a standard sweep of merchant-district households. The test was supposed to be a formality. Corvus failed to conceal what he was.\n\nThe next six months were the most frightening of his life. The Conclave was not cruel — they were procedural, which was its own kind of pressure. He was assessed, documented, and offered a scholarship to the Conclave's training facility in the capital. His parents were informed. His father was proud in a complicated way. His mother understood immediately that the business had lost him and did not pretend otherwise, which Corvus respected.\n\nHe trained at the Conclave for eight years. He was a competent student — careful, analytical, not given to the showy demonstrations that got other students attention. He specialized in ward-theory and binding mechanics, which suited the environmental sensitivity the Conclave had documented: sensing interference in existing workings is related to sensing pressure in existing weather systems. He graduated without distinction and without incident, which was his intention.\n\nWhat the Conclave documented was his environmental ability. They did not document the other thing, because Corvus did not show them the other thing.\n\n## The Hidden Layer\n\nBeneath the environmental sensing — deeper, stranger, and impossible to explain in the Conclave's existing frameworks — Corvus can perceive the underlying architecture of magical workings themselves. Not their surface effects but their structure: where a spell is anchored, how a binding is organized, what a ward is actually doing underneath what it appears to be doing.\n\nMost resonance is generative or transformative. His environmental ability fits those categories loosely. The structural perception does not fit any category the Conclave has. He has never found a name for it in their literature.\n\nAt its deepest reach, this structural perception extends to Mordvar's reforming presence itself. Not clearly — not as information he can read or report — but as a pressure, a directionality, a wrongness in the texture of things when he is near a site connected to the old working. He has felt it intensify over the years in ways that disturb him.\n\nHe has told no one at the Conclave. Partly because he doesn't have the vocabulary to explain it. Partly because he is not sure what they would do with the information. And partly because the wrongness he senses has a specific quality he cannot shake: it does not feel like something breaking. It feels like something waking up — as though what was unmade is slowly, patiently becoming again.\n\n## Joining Roland\n\nCorvus left the Conclave's formal service two years before Game One begins, taking a research post that gave him freedom to travel. He had been quietly following reports of anomalous magical signatures in the Ashfields region — the kind of interference pattern consistent with something vast and ancient slowly reconstituting — the unmade will of Mordvar, reforming. His environmental sensitivity flagged the anomalies first. His structural perception confirmed what they meant.\n\nHe meets Roland without either of them knowing yet how much the other already understands.\n\n## Character\n\nCorvus is precise, observant, and slow to extend trust — not from coldness but from a lifetime of keeping a significant secret. He has a dry humor that surfaces unexpectedly. He is genuinely good at his work and aware of it without being arrogant about it.\n\nHis central tension is between his instinct for caution and careful process and the growing certainty that the thing he has been cautiously processing is already far past the point where caution helps. The environmental sensitivity tells him the world is changing. The structural perception tells him what it is changing into. Neither ability gives him a way to stop it alone, which is why — eventually, in the scene that defines his Game Two arc — he tells Roland about the structural perception. Not because he has decided to trust. Because he has run out of reasons not to.\n
