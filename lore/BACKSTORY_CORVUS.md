# Backstory: Corvus Aldenmere

## Origins: Solgrade Merchant Family

Corvus was born in Solgrade, the largest trading city in the Eldermark, to a family that had been in the import business for three generations. The Aldenmeres were not aristocratic but were comfortable — a townhouse near the dockside market, a small warehouse, two clerks, a name that meant something to the right people. Corvus's father Daven managed accounts. His mother Ysel handled supplier relationships and was the sharper of the two. They expected Corvus to join the business.

He was good at the work. He had a memory for numbers, a facility for reading people, and patience with the slow arithmetic of negotiation. He was also, from his early teens, hiding something.

## The Manifestation

At fourteen, Corvus's resonance emerged without warning during a warehouse inventory. He was alone, checking a crate manifest by candlelight, when the candle went out. He reached for a taper to relight it — and the light came back, sourceless, sitting in his palm like a coin.

He held it for a long moment. Then he closed his fist and it was gone.

He told no one. He practiced in secret for two years, learning the shape of what he could do through trial and error and two near-disasters he managed to contain. His resonance had an unusual quality the Conclave would later classify as "structural" — he could perceive the underlying architecture of magical workings, feel where spells were anchored, sense interference in existing bindings. This is rare. Most resonance is generative or transformative. Corvus's was diagnostic.

## Discovery and the Conclave

At sixteen, a junior Conclave assessor passing through Solgrade on routine census work tested Corvus as part of a standard sweep of merchant-district households. The test was supposed to be a formality. Corvus failed to conceal what he was.

The next six months were the most frightening of his life. The Conclave was not cruel — they were procedural, which was its own kind of pressure. He was assessed, documented, and offered a scholarship to the Conclave's training facility in the capital. His parents were informed. His father was proud in a complicated way. His mother understood immediately that the business had lost him and did not pretend otherwise, which Corvus respected.

He trained at the Conclave for eight years. He was a competent student — careful, analytical, not given to the showy demonstrations that got other students attention. He specialized in ward-theory and binding mechanics, which suited his diagnostic resonance. He graduated without distinction and without incident, which was his intention.

## The Hidden Resonance

What the Conclave documented was only part of what Corvus could do.

His structural perception extended, at its deepest reach, to something the Conclave has no good framework for: he can sense the Aeluvain binding itself. Not clearly — not as information he can read or report — but as a pressure, a directionality, a wrongness in the texture of things when he is near a site connected to the old working. He has felt it intensify over the years in ways that disturb him.

He has told no one at the Conclave. Partly because he doesn't have the vocabulary to explain it. Partly because he is not sure what they would do with the information. And partly because the wrongness he senses has a specific quality he cannot shake: it does not feel like something breaking. It feels like something waking up and testing the walls.

## Joining Roland

Corvus left the Conclave's formal service two years before Game 1 begins, taking a research post that gave him freedom to travel. He had been quietly following reports of anomalous magical signatures in the Ashfields region — the kind of interference pattern consistent with a very large and very old binding under stress.

He meets Roland in the circumstances of Game 1 without either of them knowing yet how much the other already understands.

## Character

Corvus is precise, observant, and slow to extend trust — not from coldness but from a lifetime of keeping a significant secret. He has a dry humor that surfaces unexpectedly. He is genuinely good at his work and aware of it without being arrogant about it. His central tension is between his instinct for caution and careful process and the growing certainty that the thing he has been cautiously processing is already far past the point where caution helps.
