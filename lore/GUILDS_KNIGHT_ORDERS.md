# Knight Orders of the Human Kingdoms

One major chivalric order per kingdom. Each has its own founding myth, internal politics, and connection to the trilogy's plot.

> **Index file.** Each entry below is a one-screen overview. Where a `**Deep history:**` link is present, the linked backstory file is the canonical source for founding details, doctrine, internal politics, and trilogy arcs.

---

## The Iron Chalice — Eldermark

**Founded** three centuries ago after the **Red Siege of Aldenholt**, when a last company of soldiers held the city gates for nine days on nothing but well water and the belief that someone was coming. The Order was named for the cup of well water the last surviving defender was still holding when relief arrived.

**Composition:** Twelve hundred knights at full strength. Heavy cavalry trained to fight as dismounted infantry once their horses are gone — their founding doctrine assumes the cavalry has already failed.

**Religion:** Devoted to **Aldrath**, but specifically the aspect of *endurance* rather than *victory*. The Order does not celebrate wins. It commemorates the times it held.

**Current Grandmaster:** **Dame Calla Vane** (no relation to Aldric Vane). Knows the Order is financially insolvent — a debt to Solgrade's banking houses comes due in two years. She has told no one. She is quietly looking for a way out that does not involve selling the Order's independence.

**Plot connection:** The Order's chapel displays an iron pommel on its founding-relic altar. Dame Calla, like every grandmaster before her, does not know it is the **iron piece of the Sundered Crown**. **Roland Ashford** discovered this three years before Game One and was expelled for refusing to stay silent. Game One opens with him hunted for that knowledge.

**Endgame:** If Roland resolves the Order's debt during the Solgrade arc, the Iron Chalice arrives at the Battle of Drûn-Khazad as committed allies rather than reluctant conscripts. Of twelve hundred knights, one hundred and twelve survive the final battle — they spend themselves escorting Aldric to the summit.

**Deep history:** see [BACKSTORY_IRON_CHALICE.md](BACKSTORY_IRON_CHALICE.md) — the Red Siege founding, the doctrine of endurance, Brightwatch fortress, the Solgrade debt crisis, and Roland's expulsion in detail.

## The Tidewarden Order — Caer Brannoch

**Founded** before Caer Brannoch was a kingdom — when the western coast was still a collection of cliff-villages whose sailors had grown sick of taking pirate bribes. The founding charter contains the line *"We will not be bought. We have tried it and it makes us sick."* This is not metaphorical.

**Composition:** Roughly nine hundred knights. Every Tidewarden must navigate, swim a mile in modified armor, and sail a single-mast vessel alone before being accepted. They fight on water as readily as land.

**Style:** Their land combat is adapted from shipboard fighting — close, economical, built for confined spaces and sudden footing changes.

**Religion:** Devoted to **Sorael**, who in Caer Brannoch theology is god of sea, journeys, and honest reckoning.

**Internal faction — the Deepwater Voices:** A minority within the Order who believe the **Shroud Sea's** permanent storms are not natural but a deliberate seal placed over something that needed to stay contained. The Order's leadership officially discourages this question because they find it *too interesting*.

**Plot connection:** Provides naval support for the Khorumzad relief fleet (Game Two). Plays a critical role in Game Three: Eilwen's fleet at the final muster on the Copper Isles is built around Tidewarden command.

## The Frost Brotherhood — Vosskara

**Founded** as a ranger compact, not a chivalric order. Began as men and women who tracked goblin raiding parties into the winter steppe and dealt with them before they reached settlements. Has retained the practical, unornamented culture of its origin.

**Composition:** Roughly four hundred members. **Initiation:** survive three days alone in the northern steppe in midwinter with one knife and no fire. **Failure rate:** approximately forty percent. The Brotherhood considers this acceptable — the ones who come back are reliably the ones who should be in the Brotherhood.

**Style:** No ceremonial armor. No tournaments. Every member is a tracker, interrogator, and wilderness surgeon as well as a fighter.

**Function:** Vosskara's intelligence apparatus as much as its military arm. They hold the eastern frontier with fewer numbers than any comparable force because they know the ground, know the enemy, and do not waste effort on anything except the work.

**Classified history:** The Brotherhood ran a covert information exchange with a Gulmrak orc warlord (predecessor to Drûn-Aghul) for eleven years. The exchange ended with that warlord's death in Game One. Three current captains know what was exchanged. They have agreed never to tell Despot Yaromir.

**Plot connection:** Provides intelligence on Ashen Hand frontier movements throughout Games One and Two. In Game Three, the Brotherhood is critical to **Aldric Vane's** protection in Coldstoke — they slow the Hand's pursuit and cover the escape route.

## The Golden Lance — Solgrade

**Officially:** Solgrade's chivalric order, founded to protect merchant caravans and the Council of Twelve Houses' interests.

**In practice:** Everyone in the other three kingdoms knows the Golden Lance is a political instrument. Members are drawn from second sons of wealthy families, trained to fight, and deployed primarily as diplomatic escorts, trade negotiators, and quiet problem-solvers.

**Combat style:** Precise and economical — drawn-out violence is bad for commerce. Sword and a small fast-draw crossbow that can be drawn and fired in under four seconds. Considered dishonorably practical by every other Order until the fourth time a Golden Lance member killed someone who had already disarmed them.

**Track record:** Has never fought a major pitched battle. Has been present at twelve significant assassinations, six contested inheritance settlements, and three changes of government in small city-states along the trade routes. Officially denies all of this.

**Current Grand Commander:** **Ser Aldric Vossant** (no relation to Aldric Vane — *Aldric* is a common Eldermark name). Has developed inconvenient genuine convictions about justice that conflict with the Council. Currently refusing contracts he believes cross a line. The Council is deciding whether to remove him or simply route problematic work elsewhere.

**Plot connection:** In Game One, Vossant offers Roland his help in exchange for evidence of a House Korvath weapons-smuggling operation he has been trying to prove for two years.
