# Backstory: The Naergrim and the Wound

## What the Naergrim Are

The Naergrim are not a human ethnic group, though casual outsiders sometimes mistake them for one. They are the descendants of a mixed lineage — human, with a thread of something older woven in through centuries of living near the eastern forest's edges — who are defined less by blood than by the oath their ancestors swore roughly eight hundred years ago.

Before the oath, the Naergrim were called the Fringe Kin: scattered frontier communities along the eastern Borderlands who had been displaced by the expansion of the four kingdoms and pushed into marginal territory near the ashland approaches. They were not organized. They were surviving.

## The Wound

The entity beneath Drun-Khazad — the Hollow Throne, already working its influence westward even then — found the Fringe Kin in the third century of the Third Age. It did not approach them as a conqueror. It approached them as a patron.

The bargain it offered was simple: protection, territory, and a clear identity in exchange for a blood-magic oath that bound the community's leadership to the entity's interests and their descendants to the leadership's authority. The entity called the oath-binding "the Wound" — a name that emphasized sacrifice rather than servitude, which is a meaningful rhetorical distinction when you are offering the bargain to people who have been surviving rather than living.

Most of the Fringe Kin's leaders accepted. The ones who refused were not punished. They left. Their descendants are the human-integrated communities Sorin works with today.

The Wound does not give the entity direct control over individual Naergrim. It creates a hierarchy of obligation: the clan leaders are bound most tightly, their obligations enforceable through physical consequence. Ordinary Naergrim feel the Wound as a cultural identity rather than a direct constraint. They are taught that the entity is the source of their cohesion, that the world outside the oath-community is hostile, and that the Wound is what makes them a people rather than scattered survivors.

This is not entirely false. The Wound did provide what was promised. The Naergrim have territory and cohesion that the Fringe Kin never had. The price is that every generation, the clan leaders must re-ratify the oath, and the entity gets eight hundred years of loyal positioning in the Borderlands.

## Serethi-Twice-Dead

Serethi is the oldest living Naergrim and the only one who has survived the Aelthiren — or what she thought was the Aelthiren.

Roughly two hundred years ago, when she was in her sixth decade, Serethi attempted the ritual at the boundary of the Vethri-Glade. The Aelthiren requires the Aelorin community's acceptance — the willing extension of the forest's life-force to bridge the mortal span. The Aelorin at Vethri-Glade at the time were willing. The ritual proceeded.

Serethi died at the glade boundary. Her companions carried her body back to Naergrim territory to be prepared for burial rites.

Three days later, she woke up.

She was unchanged. Not as an elf — not extended, not transformed. Unchanged as herself: the same woman, the same age, apparently unaffected by three days of clinical death. The Aelorin, when she returned to the glade to understand what had happened, could not explain it. The ritual had worked correctly. She had died correctly. Something had refused the transition.

She has been investigating this for two hundred years. Her current best theory is that the Wound prevents the Aelthiren: the oath-binding that runs through Naergrim leadership creates an obligation that the entity enforces even across death, because a leader who becomes Aelorin is no longer available to ratify the generational oath. The entity let the ritual proceed to the point of death and then simply declined to release its claim.

She calls herself Twice-Dead because she died once at the glade and once, more slowly, in the decades afterward, as she understood what the name meant: that she belongs to the Wound more completely than she had known.

She has been Serethi-Twice-Dead for two hundred years. The Wound has been silent on the matter since — no punishment, no communication, no acknowledgment. This silence is, to her, the most frightening thing about it. The entity did not need to explain itself. It simply held on.

## The Naergrim Dissidents

Serethi is not alone in wanting the oath ended, but she is the most significant figure in the dissident faction because she holds clan-leader authority and has held it long enough that challenging her directly would fracture the community. The entity's practical problem with Serethi is that removing her creates worse instability than tolerating her.

The dissident faction has three positions that distinguish it from the mainstream Naergrim:

1. The Wound should be ended even if the protection it provides disappears.
2. The outside world is not uniformly hostile, and the Naergrim's isolation is a choice the entity benefits from, not a necessity the Naergrim require.
3. Whatever is bound beneath Drun-Khazad should not be freed.

These are not popular positions. The third especially: most Naergrim do not have enough information about the entity's nature to understand why its freedom would be catastrophic for them specifically.

Serethi has the information. She has had two hundred years to gather it. She is waiting for someone from outside who can do something with it.

## The Party's Path to Serethi

Reaching Serethi requires Sorin's contacts and a specific approach that avoids triggering the Wound's surveillance — the entity monitors approaches to Naergrim territory through the oath-network, which means the party cannot simply arrive. They need an introduction that comes from inside the dissident faction, which means Sorin's years of relationship-building are the only viable entry point.

What Serethi can give them: a complete account of the Wound's mechanics, the specific vulnerability in the oath-binding that would allow it to be severed at the source, and two hundred years of observations about the entity's patterns and weaknesses that no one outside the Naergrim has ever had access to.

What she wants in return: a promise that if the entity is defeated, the Naergrim's right to continue as a people — without the Wound, on their own terms — will be recognized. She does not ask for much. She asks for dignity. This is harder to deliver than military victory, and the party will need to reckon with that.
