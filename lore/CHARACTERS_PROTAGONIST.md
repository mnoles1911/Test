# Roland Ashford — Protagonist

---

## Overview

**Full name:** Roland Ashford
**Age:** 34 at the start of Game One
**Origin:** Eldermark, raised in a border town in the Ashfields
**Former role:** Knight of the Iron Chalice, expelled

---

## Appearance

Lean and weathered beyond his years — the Ashfields age people. Brown hair cut short, always slightly too long by the time he gets around to cutting it again. Grey eyes. A scar on his jaw from a training accident at fourteen that he has long since stopped noticing. Carries himself like someone who was trained to stand straight and has slowly stopped bothering.

---

## History

Born to a minor landholder family in the eastern Ashfields, the youngest of three. His two older sisters inherited the land; Roland joined the Iron Chalice at sixteen because it was the most honorable exit available to a third child with a good sword arm.

He was a good knight. Not remarkable — no exceptional battles or heroic moments — but reliable, thoughtful, and well-liked by the enlisted soldiers he worked alongside. He rose to senior knight by competence rather than connections, which in the Iron Chalice means something.

**The expulsion:** Three years before Game One, Roland was assigned to inventory and document the Order's chapel relics — a routine administrative task. He recognized the iron pommel on the founding-relic altar from a description in a Second Age text he had read while recovering from a broken leg. He spent four months quietly researching before bringing his findings to the Grandmaster: the pommel is a piece of the Sundered Crown.

Dame Calla Vane's response was to ask him to write nothing down and speak of it to no one. Her reasons were not corrupt — the Order was financially insolvent, and the pommel was the founding relic's centerpiece; losing it would end the Order. Roland refused silence. He was expelled without charges, because charging him would require acknowledging what he had found.

He has been hunted since. The Iron Chalice did not send killers — but word spread, and those who want the Crown's location found him.

---

## Character

**Defining quality:** Roland does the thing that needs doing even when he cannot see how it ends well. This is not courage exactly — he is afraid often — but an inability to choose comfortable inaction once he knows what the right action is.

**Flaw:** He assumes people will eventually come to the same conclusion he has if he presents the evidence clearly enough. He is wrong about this more often than he expects, and it costs him relationships.

**Relationship to institutions:** The expulsion broke something in his faith in formal structures. He still believes in what the Iron Chalice *was* — the endurance doctrine, the culture of holding rather than winning — but not in its current execution. This creates a useful ambivalence: he can work within institutions without deferring to them.

**Sense of humor:** Dry, rare, usually at his own expense.

---

## Arc Across the Trilogy

**Game One:** Pursues the Sundered Crown's pieces while building the alliances that will eventually become the Grand Alliance. The central question is whether he can hold his own integrity under pressure to compromise.

**Game Two:** The Khorumzad descent is the hardest thing Roland has done. He watches Bromrin's mind deteriorate. He finds the Aeluvain in a place where the world's original song can be faintly heard by those close to the dying Eighth Star — a sound that should be beautiful and is instead profoundly sad. The question of the trilogy's second game: what do you do when doing the right thing requires watching people suffer?

**Game Three:** Roland's role shifts from seeker to protector. He has found what needed to be found. Now he has to keep Aldric Vane alive long enough to end this. The question of Game Three: can he let someone else be the instrument of the ending, or will the compulsion to do the right thing himself get in the way?

**Ending:** Roland does not wield the Aeluvain. Aldric does. Roland's role at the summit of Drûn-Khazad is to stand between Aldric and everything that would stop him. In both endings, Roland survives.

---

## Relationships with Companions

**Corvus Tane:** Corvus finds Roland exhausting and respects him deeply. Roland finds Corvus difficult and trusts him completely. They argue constantly. They would not survive the trilogy without each other.

**Seren of the Third Glade:** The most equitable relationship in the group — two people who have each seen institutions fail them and drawn similar conclusions. Seren challenges Roland's human-centric assumptions without contempt for them.

**Orion Farr:** The youngest companion. Roland's instinct is to protect him; Orion's instinct is to resist being protected. The resolution of this tension is one of Game Two's emotional throughlines.

**Bromrin:** Roland and Bromrin meet properly in Game Two, when Bromrin's mind is already beginning to slip. Roland is the only person in the descent party who consistently treats Bromrin as the king he still is, not as a patient. This matters more than any tactical decision Roland makes.
