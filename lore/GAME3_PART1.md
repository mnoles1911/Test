# Game Three: The Last Star — Acts I & II

---

## Premise

Aldric Vane is the last of the Caelborn line — the Aescryd bloodline the Aeluvain is keyed to — and the only person who can wield it against Mordvar. He is a blacksmith in Coldstoke, a frontier village in Vosskara. He does not know any of this. He has already survived one assassination attempt and does not know why.

The Gulmrak Horde under Uglakh is marching. The Ashen Hand's remnant forces — leaderless after the Ashlord's death at Khorumzad, but structurally intact — are hunting Aldric specifically. They know what the bloodline means. The race to reach Aldric before the Hand's agents do is Game Three's opening act.

In parallel: the Grand Alliance is assembling — a military operation of a scale not seen since the Second Age, with the three-to-one disadvantage that the Alliance's bond of shared purpose must overcome.

Before the march east, Roland must also reach the Ash Tower in the Sundered Isles, where the smith Hadran Volthen has spent two centuries preparing to receive the Crown pieces and reforge the Aeluvain for its wielder. Without Hadran's craft and knowledge, Aldric cannot safely wield the Aeluvain. Both tasks must be completed before Mordvar finishes reforming.

## Act I: The Race to Coldstoke

### Parallel Opening

Game Three opens in two simultaneous threads, cutting between them:

**Thread A — Aldric Vane (Coldstoke):** Aldric at his forge. A second assassination attempt — more professional than the first. He survives using a hammer and a furnace in ways that show he is not a trained fighter but is competent and calm under pressure. He does not know what to do next. He goes to the village elder. The village elder says: *There was a letter.* The letter is Roland's original message, arrived late. Aldric reads it. He says nothing for a long time. Then: *I need to leave.*

**Thread B — Roland (en route):** Roland's party is riding hard from the Khorumzad region west and north toward Vosskara. Frost Brotherhood intelligence has confirmed the Hand's agents' direction. The race is explicit — whoever reaches Coldstoke first determines the trajectory of the rest of the game.

The player controls Roland. The thread-cutting creates dread.

---

### [MAIN QUEST] Reaching Coldstoke

Roland arrives in Coldstoke six hours after the second assassination attempt. Aldric is still there — he waited, which Roland respects and finds alarming.

**The conversation:** Roland explains, as plainly as he can, everything. Mordvar. The bloodline. The Aeluvain. What wielding it requires — that Aldric will need to consciously carry Mordvar's hollow for the duration, and know what it feels like to be the king who emptied himself out of fear.

Aldric asks: *What happens after?*

Roland says: *You can return it — complete what the Star was singing and let Mordvar dissolve. Or you can hold it — keep the hollow anchored in your bloodline so it can never escape. The difference is what you're willing to carry for the rest of your life. Aelthurion described both paths. You choose at the summit.*

Aldric says: *I haven't agreed to go to any summit.*

Roland says: *No. You haven't.*

**This is the scene that establishes the party's dynamic for Game Three.** Aldric is not a chosen hero. He is a man with a craft and a life, who is being asked to sacrifice both for people he does not know. He agrees because the alternative is watching Coldstoke burn — not because of destiny, not because he has always been special, but because he is the only person who can stop it and he is aware of this.

---

### [MAIN QUEST] The Frost Brotherhood Rearguard

The Hand's agents arrive in the Coldstoke area three days after Roland. The Frost Brotherhood, acting on their standing intelligence operation, has already positioned three captains and their units along the approach routes.

The rearguard action that buys Aldric time to leave Coldstoke is brutal and one-sided. The Brotherhood is outmatched by the Hand's mages — but they know the terrain in a way the Ashen Hand does not, and they buy the time needed.

**Captain Mira Halsten** commands the western approach. The Salt Road mercenaries are with her. If the Ashsteel optional quest was completed, her people are carrying Ashsteel weapons. The Ashen Hand cannot detect them magically — which is the advantage that saves Mira's life specifically.

## Act II: The Alliance and the Forge

### [MAIN QUEST] The Ash Tower — Hadran Volthen

Before the Alliance can march, Aldric must be prepared. The Aeluvain is a sword of extraordinary provenance — it will respond to Aldric's bloodline, but the reforging requires the seven Crown pieces as raw material and the smith's knowledge only Hadran Volthen possesses. Without Hadran, Aldric could attempt the wielding and survive. With Hadran's work, he will survive it intact.

The Ash Tower is in the Sundered Isles — accessible by Sailor's Guild chart through the Shroud Sea's outer storm-edge. Roland's party makes the crossing. The approach to the Tower requires a small craft through the sea-cave below at slack tide, then iron rungs up to the fourth floor.

Hadran is there. He does not flee. He does not fight. He is at his anvil when they arrive, running his thumb along an edge he cannot feel. He offers them tea.

**The conversation:** Hadran answers their questions. He shows them the anvil and the seven sockets, prepared and waiting for thirty years. He explains, in plain language, what he intended to do — guide the Crown's reassembly to complete Mordvar's return, because he believes Mordvar's diagnosis about mortal grief was correct, and that completing the return was the more honest resolution.

He is the most frightening figure in the trilogy because he is reasonable. He makes no speeches. He does not ask for mercy. He is simply present, and articulate, and wrong in a way that took two centuries of intelligent effort to build.

**The choice:** Kill Hadran and lose his knowledge, or take him alive and use it. In the path leading to the best outcome, the party takes him alive. Hadran, offered the chance to let his work be used toward a different end than he intended, agrees. Not from conversion — he still believes what he believes — but because he is Long-Worked, three hundred and forty years old, and he has been running on the purpose of completing the Crown's work. If that purpose is redirected, what remains is a very old man who is tired, and curious about whether the new use of his knowledge is as durable as the one he planned.

He reforges the Aeluvain with Aldric's hands on the hilt throughout: the seven Crown pieces are fed into the forge as raw material, their metal worked into the blade, binding the sword to Aldric's specific bloodline so the wielding does not consume him. The process takes two days. It is not painful — it is the opposite of pain. Aldric says afterward that he could hear something, very faint, that he could not describe. Seren says: *Yes. That is what it sounds like.*

Hadran dies of old age within the year — the Long-Worked process unravels once the purpose sustaining it is denied. He knows this when he agrees. The party gives him passage on a Sailor's Guild ship. He dies at sea, watching the Sundered Isles disappear behind him for the last time.

### [MAIN QUEST] The Muster at the Copper Isles

The agreed rally point for the Grand Alliance is the Copper Isles — neutral ground, Guild-connected, accessible by sea to all parties.

The forces that arrive depend entirely on what Roland accomplished in Games One and Two:
- **Iron Chalice:** 1,200 knights (if debt resolved in Game One)
- **Tidewarden:** Naval command of the eastern fleet under Eilwen
- **Frost Brotherhood:** What remains after the Coldstoke rearguard
- **Karaz-Dûn soldiers:** King Hagrim's force (does not wait for a vote)
- **Khorumzad:** Darna Deepdelver's command (Bromrin stayed home; he wrote a letter)
- **Vosskaran Despotate:** Yaromir's full frontier force (if Tribute War de-escalated)
- **Aelorin Dawnbringers:** Seven operatives, each worth a platoon
- **Conclave mages:** Forty-one mages total (two Ashfallen identified and isolated; thirty-nine loyal)
- **Golden Lance:** If Vossant's contract honored in Game One
- **Brightwatch garrison:** Iron Chalice forward post; already at the frontier

**Total Alliance forces at maximum commitment:** 55,000. The Ash Throne fields 160,000.

The ratio is known at the muster. Aelthurion, who is present, says: *We have been here before. The Second Age Grand Alliance was smaller. They held.*

### [MAIN QUEST] Aldric's Training

Aldric is not a warrior. He needs to survive the approach to the summit of Drûn-Khazad — which means learning enough to not be killed before he reaches it. He does not need to fight Mordvar in the conventional sense. He needs to reach him.

**Who trains him:**
- Roland: endurance and practical defense. What the Iron Chalice teaches — not how to win but how to keep moving when you're losing
- Seren: the nature of what he will carry. She sits with him in the evenings and explains the Aeluvain not as an object but as a presence. What he will hear. What it will ask of him
- Orion: how to move in confined terrain, how to use obstruction, how to fall correctly

**The most important session:** Aldric asks Roland: *Have you ever been afraid of what you were doing?*

Roland says: *At the beginning. And then at the end. The middle is the part where you're too busy to be afraid.*

Aldric says: *I don't think that will work for me.*

Roland says: *It doesn't have to. You only have to get to the summit. After that it's done.*

### [SIDE QUEST] The Nameless Council's Message

Roland carries the sealed message from Game Two — the Nameless Council operative's price for the Khorumzad approach. At the muster, he delivers it to the Aelorin representative from Lirien-Thal.

She reads it. She folds it. She says to Roland: *Lord Aelthurion has been carrying this question for eight hundred years. You have answered it.* She does not explain further.

What the message contains: evidence that the Eighth Star's fall was not random loss but a choice — the Star chose to come down and stay, to be present at the moment it would be needed rather than watching from a distance it could not close. The Aelorin theology around the Aeluvain shifts: from mourning the fallen star to understanding that it went where it could help.
