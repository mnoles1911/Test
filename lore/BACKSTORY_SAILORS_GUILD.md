# Backstory: The Sailor's Guild

> **Companion file:** `HISTORY_SHROUD_SEA.md` covers the Sailor's Guild's chart program, the decade window theory, the buried Harwick crossing report, Orion's history, and the crossing protocol. This file covers the Sailor's Guild as an institution: its origin, principles, structure, the Copper Isles Archive, and the Hand's Infiltration.

---

## Origin

The Sailor's Guild began two and a half centuries before Game One as a mutual aid society. There was no founder. There was a meeting in a Caer Brannoch tavern attended by approximately thirty sailors who had collectively buried fourteen colleagues in the preceding year and had concluded that the families of drowned sailors were not being adequately supported by the kingdom or the dock guilds.

The meeting agreed on a fund. Each member contributed a percentage of every voyage's earnings. The fund paid out to the families of members who drowned, were lost overseas, or were killed in dock disputes. The administrative work was done by an elected steward — initially the tavern-keeper, who agreed to keep the records in a strongbox behind the bar — and the fund's existence was kept informal precisely because formalizing it would have made it taxable.

It worked. Within a decade the fund had spread to every major port on Mira's western coast. Within thirty years it had crossed to the Copper Isles, to the eastern coast's Vosskaran ports, and to the Aelorin coastal settlement at Sirathiel-by-the-Sea, where two Aelorin sailors had quietly joined the membership and been treated by the rest of the network as Guildmates without comment. Within a century it was the closest thing the world had to an international institution.

What it became was not what it began as. The mutual aid function still operates — it is the largest of the Guild's continuous programs, and the steward's fund still pays out to drowned-sailor families on every coast. But the institution's significance now is larger than the fund. The Sailor's Guild is the only organization that operates across every kingdom on both coasts, that has its own internal communication network, and that has spent two centuries patiently observing the sea that no one else can sail.

## Absolute Principles

The Sailor's Guild has three rules. They are not in any document. They are in every member's induction conversation. Violating any of them is grounds for permanent expulsion, and the network treats expelled members as if they had never existed.

1. **No crown service.** Guild members do not work for kings. They will accept commercial contracts from royal households, will ferry royal correspondence at standard rates, will trade information for money. They will not take loyalty oaths to crowns, will not carry royal commissions on Guild ships, and will not allow Guild resources to be used in service of any kingdom's policy. The rule was added in the Guild's seventieth year after the steward at the time accepted a sealed Eldermark commission and was dead within six months under circumstances that the surviving stewards agreed to call instructive.

2. **No hidden messages between members.** Information flows freely between Guild members. A member who carries a message that another member is forbidden to read has broken the principle. There are no Guild secrets between Guild members. There are Guild secrets *from outsiders*, but the line is clear: if you are a member, you may ask any other member anything, and the other member's only honest options are to answer or to refuse on the grounds that they do not know.

3. **No member sinks another member's ship.** The phrasing is literal. A Guild member may not be a party to the destruction of another Guildmate's vessel, regardless of the contract or the circumstances. Members hired by hostile parties have, on multiple occasions, walked off jobs at sea when the target was identified as a Guildmate's ship.

Thirty-one members have been expelled in the Guild's history. Twenty-nine for breaking the no-crown-service rule. The other two for reasons the Guild will not discuss — the steward's archive in Caer Brannoch contains the records, but the relevant dossiers are sealed and the seals have not been broken in over a century.

## Structure and Endorsement

The Sailor's Guild does not have a single leader. It has a Council of Stewards: one steward per significant port, elected by the local membership for terms of variable length determined by local custom. The Council meets formally every three years at Caer Brannoch and informally as needed through the network.

Decisions that affect the whole Guild — adding a port, amending the principles, expelling a member — require Council consensus. Decisions that affect a single port are the local steward's call.

**Endorsement** is the Guild's gating mechanism. A non-member sailor who wishes to access Guild resources — safe-houses, the Copper Isles Archive, the network's information channels, the chart libraries — requires endorsement from an active member who has personally observed their work on at least one voyage. The endorsement is not membership. It is conditional access. An endorsed outsider may be present at Guild facilities, may be addressed by Guild members, may receive Guild assistance in emergencies — but cannot vote, cannot inherit member benefits if they die, and cannot endorse other outsiders.

Roland is endorsed in Game One through Orion's vouching. The endorsement is what gets him into the Copper Isles Archive. It is also what gets him onto the Guild safe-house tunnel network during the Act IV Ashfields retreat — a network that is otherwise invisible to non-Guild members.

## The Safe-House Network

The Sailor's Guild maintains safe-houses in every member port and at intervals along the major coastal trade routes. The network was built over the Guild's first century — initially as emergency lodging for sailors stranded between voyages, then as discreet meeting places where Guild members from different kingdoms could conduct cross-border conversations without crown surveillance.

There are also safe-houses that were not built for sailors at all. These are tunnel networks, hidden anchorages, and overland refuges in places where the Guild has, over generations, helped move people who needed to disappear from one kingdom into another. Most of these were built during the period seventy to a hundred years ago when several human kingdoms experienced rapid succession crises and the Guild found itself acting, through individual member decisions, as a quiet resistance infrastructure. The Council never formally authorized this work. The Council also never asked anyone to stop.

The Ashfields tunnel that Orion uses in the Act IV escape is one of these. It was built approximately ninety years ago by a Guild-affiliated smuggling operation that no longer exists. The Guild has maintained the tunnel quietly because losing the route felt wasteful. Most members do not know the tunnel exists. Orion knows because his uncle, a former Caer Brannoch steward, told him as part of the standard briefing for sailors who were going to spend significant time in eastern Mira.

The small-island safe-house where Roland's storm-detoured voyage discovers the Ashen Hand agent is a different kind of installation. It is what the Guild calls a *quiet harbor* — a safe-house on an unmapped island used as a contingency anchor for ships caught in weather they cannot ride out. The Guild maintains a dozen of these along the Copper Isles approach. Their locations are not in any chart available to non-members. The Hand agent who infiltrated Guild communications knew enough to suspect they existed but did not know which island the storm would force the ship to.

## The Copper Isles Archive

The Archive was established roughly a hundred and seventy years ago in the Guild's largest member port. Its original purpose was practical: to consolidate the chart libraries that individual members had been keeping in their own homes, where they were vulnerable to fire, theft, and the ordinary entropy of personal storage.

What the Archive became, over generations, is the largest collection of maritime knowledge in the world — including charts, voyage logs, weather observations, port surveys, ship-design specifications, and a great many objects sailors brought back from voyages that they did not want to keep in their homes but did not want destroyed.

The catalogue includes a number of items the Guild does not understand. The copper wire — Piece Four of the Sundered Crown — is one of these. It was deposited approximately eighty years ago by a Guild member who described it as a Second Age artifact she had purchased from a Solgrade dealer who could not explain its provenance. She catalogued it as "function unknown" and asked that it be kept in the deep collection. She died at sea two years later. Her ship's loss is one of the historical drowning incidents the Guild's mutual-aid fund paid out on. No one at the Archive has thought about the copper wire in fifty years.

The archivist who releases it to Roland in Game One Act III is named Maesha Halloran. She has held the position for nineteen years. She knows the deep collection contains items of unknown significance and is not surprised when an endorsed outsider asks for one of them. Her offhand mention of the revised window estimate is not idle — she is making a point Roland is meant to hear, though she will not say what the point is unless he asks her directly.

## The Hand's Infiltration

> **See `BACKSTORY_ASHEN_HAND.md → The Civilian Intelligence Arm — Notable Operations` for the agent's full operational history.**

The Guild's leadership has known for approximately three years that something is wrong with the Copper Isles communications — small inconsistencies in chart-revision timing, a pattern of certain information reaching certain external parties faster than the network's normal latency would allow. They have not been able to identify the agent. They have not stopped looking.

The Council's working assumption has been a leak rather than a plant. They have considered, and rejected, the possibility that an endorsed outsider was the source. They have not considered the possibility that the source is a fully credentialed, long-tenured, well-liked member.

Game One Act III: Orion's voyage detours to the quiet harbor. The Hand agent is forced to reveal himself when he attempts to prevent Roland from inspecting a chest in the safe-house's lower cellar. The cellar contains, among other things, a duplicate set of the Guild's most recent Shroud Sea chart revisions — sealed in waxed leather, packed for transport, addressed (by code) to a destination outside the network.

What the party does with the agent is one of the voyage quest's branching choices. The Guild will accept whatever outcome Roland delivers and will quietly handle the network audit that follows. They will not ask Roland for information he does not volunteer. They will, however, remember.

## The Guild's Internal Divide

The Council of Stewards is currently divided on a question they have been unable to resolve for generations and that the recent window revision has made urgent: what should the Guild do with the Shroud Sea chart program now that the next crossing is plausibly within the decade?

**The conservative faction** holds that the Guild's role is to chart, not to act. The charts should be preserved and shared with members; the decision to use them should belong to the kingdoms that contract for crossings. This faction is currently the majority by a thin margin. Its strongest voice is the Vosskaran steward, who has held the seat for twenty-three years and whose patience for ambiguity is legendary.

**The active faction** holds that the Guild's two centuries of work cannot be handed to crown navies for purposes the Guild would not endorse. They argue that the network should organize the crossing itself, on its own terms, with its own ships and crews. The strongest voice is the Caer Brannoch steward, who is also Eilwen's primary Guild contact and who has been in regular communication with the Dawnbringers through Maeva for fifteen years.

The divide is not yet a schism. It is, however, the largest internal political question the Guild has faced in over a century. Game Three's resolution depends in part on which faction holds the steward's chair when the decade window opens.

## The Sailor's Guild in the Trilogy

**Game One:** Orion joins the party in Act II. The Copper Isles voyage in Act III is the central Guild quest — endorsement is earned, the copper wire is acquired, the Ashen Hand agent is exposed. The Guild's internal politics are introduced but not resolved.

**Game Two:** The Guild provides ferry and supply support for the Khorumzad relief and the eastern coastal logistics. Eilwen, who is Queen of Caer Brannoch and a former naval commander, is encountered through this route. The Caer Brannoch steward becomes a recurring contact. The active faction begins to gain ground in Council debates.

**Game Three:** The Guild organizes the crossing. The active faction wins the Council vote — by what margin and at what cost depends on Game Two outcomes. Three Guild ships carry the Alliance vanguard across the Shroud Sea. The crossing protocol developed by Maeva and Eilwen (see `HISTORY_SHROUD_SEA.md`) is the technical foundation. Orion serves as the navigator on the lead ship.

The Guild loses approximately fifty members in the crossing. The Council's mutual-aid fund, after three centuries of patient operation, is briefly insolvent for the first time in its history. Other ports' stewards quietly send what their treasuries can spare. The fund is restored within a year.
