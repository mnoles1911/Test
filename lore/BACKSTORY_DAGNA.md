# Backstory: Dagna Ironkeep

## Origins: The Stonecroft Mines

Dagna grew up in Khorumzad's lower quarters — not among the Dragon-Watchers or the high-hall clans but in the working district near the Stonecroft mines, where families spent generations pulling iron and silver from the mountain's deeper seams. Her father Brenn was a charge-setter, responsible for controlled detonations in new tunnel sections. Her mother Heva kept the family's account books and supplemented the household income by tutoring clan-children in mathematics.

Dagna was the second of four children. She learned her numbers from her mother and her patience from watching her father work. Charge-setting is deliberate, unhurried craft — Brenn moved through a new tunnel the way a careful person reads a letter they don't want to misunderstand. Dagna inherited this quality.

She was eight years old when the explosion happened.

## The Explosion

The cause was never definitively established, which is its own kind of damage. The official inquiry concluded that a gas pocket had been improperly assessed before the charge was set. Brenn's name was on the charge-setting record. He was not killed in the explosion — the blast went inward rather than outward, and he was in the safe corridor — but three other miners died, and one of them was a man named Durrak whose family had been working that seam for forty years.

Brenn spent the following years under the weight of official blame he did not entirely accept and could not entirely refuse. He had followed procedure. He believed the gas assessment had been wrong before he arrived and that the record had been adjusted after the fact. He could not prove it. The clan elders closed the inquiry.

Dagna watched her father carry this. She was eight, then ten, then twelve, watching a precise and careful man absorb the institutional conclusion that he had been imprecise and careless. She began, without quite naming it, to understand how official records can be wrong in ways that are very hard to argue with.

## Falsification Discovered

At thirty-four (young by dwarven reckoning, roughly equivalent to a human in their late teens), Dagna was working as a junior records clerk in Khorumzad's administrative archive. She had taken the position specifically because the archive held the original inquiry documents from the explosion.

She found the discrepancy through a clerical error — not her own. A misfiled equipment log from the day of the explosion had been tucked into the wrong folder and never moved. It was a maintenance record for the gas-testing instrument used on that tunnel. The calibration check, dated two days before the explosion, showed the instrument had been flagged as unreliable. The flag had not been noted in the inquiry record.

She sat with this for three days. Then she brought it to her section supervisor, who brought it to an elder, who told her the matter was closed and to refile the document correctly.

She refiled it. In the correct folder. Which is not where it had been. Which means anyone who checks the original inquiry record against supporting documents will now find a discrepancy that wasn't there before.

This is as far as she could go inside the system. It was not enough, and she knew it.

## Barak Stonecroft and the Road Out

Barak Stonecroft is the master engineer whose name is on the original gas-safety protocols for the Khorumzad lower mines — the protocols Dagna believes were violated and then the violation buried. He retired from Khorumzad forty years ago and now operates out of a small engineering hall in the middle ranges of the Spine, taking private contracts and, according to the people who know him, keeping meticulous records of everything he has ever worked on.

Dagna has been denied access to Stonecroft's private archive twice through official channels. When Game 1 begins, she is on the road with the unofficial intention of finding him herself. She has her father's original work logs, the misfiled maintenance record, and enough mathematical patience to present the case in terms no one can dismiss as imprecise.

She does not know yet that the Stonecroft mines sit near the approach to Khorumzad's deeper levels — and that what she will find in Barak Stonecroft's records is connected to more than one family's grief.

## Character

Dagna is methodical, stubborn, and funny in the way of people who grew up in tight quarters with a lot of siblings — fast, dry, and specific. She makes friends slowly and keeps them permanently. She has a temper that she controls well and that surfaces unexpectedly when she encounters institutional dishonesty, which she identifies faster than almost anyone.

Her wound is her father's uncompleted rehabilitation. She is doing this for him, and she knows it, and she suspects he would tell her to stop and live her own life. She is not ready to hear that yet.
