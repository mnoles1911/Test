# Backstory: Dagna Irontrack\n\n## Origins: The Stonecroft Mines\n\nDagna grew up in Khorumzad's lower quarters — not among the Dragon-Watchers or the high-hall clans but in the working district near the Stonecroft mines, where families spent generations pulling iron and silver from the mountain's deeper seams. Her father Brenn was a charge-setter, responsible for controlled detonations in new tunnel sections. Her mother Heva kept the family's account books and supplemented the household income by tutoring clan-children in mathematics.\n\nDagna was the second of four children. She learned her numbers from her mother and her patience from watching her father work. Charge-setting is deliberate, unhurried craft — Brenn moved through a new tunnel the way a careful person reads a letter they don't want to misunderstand. Dagna inherited this quality.\n\nShe was eight years old when the explosion happened.\n\n## The Explosion\n\nThe cause was never definitively established, which is its own kind of damage. The official inquiry concluded that a gas pocket had been improperly assessed before the charge was set. Brenn's name was on the charge-setting record. He was not killed in the explosion — the blast went inward rather than outward, and he was in the safe corridor — but three other miners died, and one of them was a man named Durrak whose family had been working that seam for forty years.\n\nBrenn spent the following years under the weight of official blame he did not entirely accept and could not entirely refuse. He had followed procedure. He believed the gas assessment had been wrong before he arrived and that the record had been adjusted after the fact. He could not prove it. The clan elders closed the inquiry.\n\nDagna watched her father carry this. She was eight, then ten, then twelve, watching a precise and careful man absorb the institutional conclusion that he had been imprecise and careless. She began, without quite naming it, to understand how official records can be wrong in ways that are very hard to argue with — and how the people who falsify them are rarely the ones who pay.\n\n## Becoming a Dragon-Watcher\n\nDagna joined the Dragon-Watcher order at forty-one, which is young by dwarven reckoning. She had spent the preceding years working administrative and records roles in Khorumzad — including a stint as a junior clerk in the hold's central archive, where she located a misfiled equipment log from the day of her father's explosion. The log showed the gas-testing instrument used in that tunnel had been flagged as unreliable two days before the blast. The flag had never been noted in the inquiry record.\n\nShe brought it to her supervisor. The matter was closed. She refiled the document in the correct folder — not where it had been — so the discrepancy would be findable by anyone who checked carefully. It was the only move available to her inside the system.\n\nThe Dragon-Watchers took her for the same qualities that had made her good at administrative work: precision, patience, and a specific intolerance for readings that didn't match reality. Her posting was the Spine's eastern slope — tracking seismic and thermal changes that in dwarven theology signal something waking that shouldn't be. She was good at it. For fourteen years she filed accurate quarterly reports.\n\n## The Falsification\n\nFor the last three years before Game One begins, Dagna's reports have not reached their intended recipients. An Ashen Hand-compromised steward in Karaz-Dûn's administrative apparatus has been intercepting them, rewriting them to show stable readings, and filing the falsified versions under her name.\n\nShe discovered this when a fellow Dragon-Watcher in a different district received her original report by mistake and sent it back asking why she'd filed two contradictory readings. She pulled the filed version and found a document she had not written, bearing her credentials, describing conditions she had not observed.\n\nThe recognition hit in a way she had not expected. This was her father's situation — a record falsified, a name attached to work that wasn't done, a system that would close the inquiry if she raised it through proper channels. She was not eight years old watching it happen to someone else. She was forty-seven and it was happening to her.\n\nShe went to report the falsification to Karaz-Dûn's Gold-Speaker office. She was told the auditor she needed to speak with — Barak Stonecroft, the retired master engineer whose name is on the original gas-safety protocols for the Khorumzad lower mines — was in protective custody in Kazaad-Brak. She is three days into the Underway when Roland's party enters it from the other direction.\n\n## What She Carries\n\nDagna is pursuing two things simultaneously, and she knows it. One is her own professional rehabilitation: proving the falsification, restoring the accurate seismic record, and making sure someone understands how bad the situation in the Spine's eastern slope actually is. The other is her father's rehabilitation: Barak Stonecroft's archive almost certainly contains the original gas-safety assessment records that would confirm what Brenn always believed — that the instrument was flagged and the flag was buried.\n\nShe has not told anyone about her father. It is the older wound and the more personal one, and she is not sure yet whether she has the right to use a world-threatening crisis as the occasion to settle a forty-year family grievance. She suspects her father would tell her to focus. She suspects he would also understand why she can't entirely.\n\n## Character\n\nDagna is methodical, stubborn, and funny in the way of people who grew up in tight quarters with a lot of siblings — fast, dry, and specific. She makes friends slowly and keeps them permanently. She has a temper that she controls well and that surfaces unexpectedly when she encounters institutional dishonesty, which she identifies faster than almost anyone. She has been training herself to identify it since she was eight years old.\n\nHer wound is double: her father's uncompleted rehabilitation and her own. They are the same wound at different depths. The trilogy gives her the chance to close both — not by revenge, and not dramatically, but by the simple act of correct records being filed and read by people with the authority to act on them. For Dagna, this is everything.\n
