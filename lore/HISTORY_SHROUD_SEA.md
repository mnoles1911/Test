# History: The Shroud Sea

## What the Shroud Sea Is

The Shroud Sea is the body of water separating the continents of Mira and Thal. It is approximately nine hundred miles at its narrowest crossing and considerably wider along most of its length. It is not navigable under normal conditions.

The specific problem is not distance or weather in the conventional sense. The Shroud Sea generates atmospheric phenomena that have no recorded natural explanation: persistent fog banks that do not move with the wind, compass interference of a consistent and systematic kind, wave patterns inconsistent with any observable wind or current, and what sailors who have entered the outer edges describe as a pressure — not physical, but experienced as something between anxiety and grief that intensifies with proximity to the center.

The Sailor's Guild has been studying this for two hundred years. Their working hypothesis, developed over generations and systematized by Maeva and Oran Saltfish forty years ago, is that the phenomena are a side effect of Mordvar's slow reconstitution — specifically, that his growing reforming presence creates a resonance field that the Shroud Sea's geography amplifies across its surface. This would explain why the phenomena have been intensifying over the past century as Mordvar's reconstitution accelerates.

## The Forty-Year Windows

The Shroud Sea has five observable navigable windows, each lasting approximately three to four months, cycling on a forty-year period. During a window, the atmospheric phenomena reduce to a level that makes crossing dangerous but possible with prepared crews and reliable charts.

The window pattern was first documented by a Sailor's Guild navigator named Eska Two-Stars in the late first century of the Third Age. She crossed during what she described as a "thin period" and returned with a fragmentary account of Thal's western coastline. Her records were treated as anomalous for a century before later pattern analysis confirmed she had been in a window without knowing it.

Subsequent windows were documented as they occurred. The current window — the one relevant to the party's crossing in Game 3 — began eighteen months before the game opens and has approximately fourteen months remaining.

## The Buried Crossing Report

Forty years ago, during the previous window, the Sailor's Guild authorized one crossing expedition under Captain Venn Harwick. The ship carried twenty-two crew and four observers. Eight people returned.

The fourteen who did not return died on Thal's western coast, killed in conditions the survivors described variously as hostile wildlife, environmental hazard, and something else they were not willing to specify in official testimony.

What the eight survivors found before their colleagues died: the ruins of an Aescryd installation on Thal's coast, decades-abandoned but structurally intact, showing evidence of manufacturing activity that had no civilian application. And something in the ruins that none of the eight survivors would describe in detail, which caused three of them to leave the Sailor's Guild within a year and all eight to suffer lifelong recurrent nightmares.

Captain Harwick's official report was filed with Guild leadership and buried. The stated reason was that the report contained unverifiable claims that would destabilize the Sailor's Guild's relationship with the four kingdoms, which had contributed funding to the expedition on the basis of commercial surveying objectives. The actual reason, as the three captains in Game 3's side quest will tell Roland, was that Guild leadership read the report and understood that what the expedition had found was not something they were equipped to act on, and that publicizing it would create pressure to act.

The report's physical copy is in Yaromir's predecessor's archive. Yaromir has never read it. Three people in the Sailor's Guild know it exists. The three captains are not among them until Game 3.

## Orion's History with the Sea

Orion was nineteen years old when he was selected as one of three window-approach testers — a Sailor's Guild operation designed to measure how close a ship could get to the navigable corridor's edge before the phenomena became disabling. The operation was not a crossing; it was a measurement.

The first tester, Desca, died when her ship's compass interference became severe enough to cause a navigation error at speed. She was twenty-two. The second tester, a man named Farne, returned with severe resonance sensitivity symptoms and retired from sailing. He lives in a Sailor's Guild coastal town and does not go near the water.

Orion completed his approach and returned with clean measurements. He did not develop Farne's symptoms. He does not entirely know why. He has thought about this a great deal.

He joined the Sailor's Guild's formal cartography division afterward and has spent twenty years building the most accurate approach charts the Guild has ever had. He is the person most responsible for the current crossing having a viable route, and he has been waiting for the window to open again for the entirety of that twenty years.

## Eilwen's Twenty-Year Investigation

Eilwen is Queen of Caer Brannoch and a former naval commander who has been conducting private research into the Shroud Sea's phenomena for twenty years alongside her duties as queen and commander of the Tidewarden fleet. Her investigation began when she found Eska Two-Stars' original records in the Guild archive and noticed that they contained a detail no subsequent documentation had included: Eska described the phenomena reducing not just in intensity but in character during the window — as if whatever was generating them had changed its attention briefly.

Eilwen's interpretation of this detail is the foundation of her theory: the Shroud Sea's phenomena are not static. They are responsive. Something is generating them with intent, and that something occasionally looks away.

She has not published this theory. The Sailor's Guild's research culture is empirical and her theory requires an inference that goes beyond what the data technically supports. She has shared it with Maeva, who finds it consistent with the window theory's mechanics without fully endorsing it, and with the Dawnbringers through Maeva's introduction.

By the time the party meets Eilwen, she has the most complete picture of the Shroud Sea's behavior of any living person. She also has a theory about the crossing's true risk that the Sailor's Guild's official charts do not account for: the phenomena reduce during windows, but they do not disappear. A crossing during the window is survivable. It is not safe. The thing generating the phenomena can notice a crossing even during a window. It simply cannot stop it.

## The Crossing Protocol

Maeva and Eilwen together developed the crossing protocol that the party uses in Game 3. Key elements:

- Crossing in the window's second month, after the phenomena have reduced to their minimum but before the Sailor's Guild's historical records suggest they begin to rebuild.
- Three-ship formation with specific spacing designed to minimize resonance interference between vessels.
- No metal compasses. Navigation by star charts and a modified Sailor's Guild instrument that uses a non-metallic medium.
- The specific approach corridor Orion's twenty years of charting identified as having the lowest documented interference signature.
- Crew briefed on the pressure phenomenon and given specific instructions for managing it: name it when it begins, tell the person next to you, do not try to reason with it or suppress it. Naming it reduces its effect. This was discovered by Eska Two-Stars, who wrote in her original record that she made her crew speak aloud what they felt and they made it through.
