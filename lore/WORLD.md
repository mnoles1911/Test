# The World — Mira-Thal

## Overview

Mira-Thal is a world of two continents in the third age of its existence. The western continent **Mira** is the heartland of civilization: four human kingdoms clustered at its center, the ancient Aelorin forests to the north, three dwarven mountain-kingdoms threading the Spine of the World, and the wild eastern frontier where the ash-lands begin. The eastern continent **Thal** is wilderness — unmapped beyond its fringes, dominated by the Ash Throne's influence, and anchored at its heart by the volcano **Drûn-Khazad**.

The age is paradox: men are more numerous than ever, cities grow, trade flows — but the world's deeper fabric frays. The Aelorin dwindle. Dwarven kings grow old or greedy. Something unmade two thousand years ago is learning, slowly, to reform.

## Geography

### Mira — The Heartland Continent

**The Central Plains**
Flat, fertile, heavily farmed. The four human kingdoms occupy this region, borders contested by rivers and old battle-lines. The most densely populated area in the world.

**The Spine of the World**
A vast mountain range running north-to-south through Mira's eastern third. Home to the three dwarven kingdoms. Impassable at its heights except through the **Underway** — the dwarven tunnel network threading the mountain's heart. The deepest point of the Spine, below Khorumzad, contains the **Vault of Aen-Vael** — the resting place of the **Aeluvain** — the sword forged from Eighth Star material by the Aelorin, placed here by the Grand Alliance as far from Drûn-Khazad as the world allows.

**The Greatwood**
Northern Mira. A vast ancient forest where the Aelorin have lived since the First Age. The wood grows darker and older the further north one travels. The Eight Sacred Glades are scattered through it. The silverwood trees in the heart of Lirien-Thal are not trees — they are ancestors.

**The Western Coast**
Dramatic sea-cliffs, rainy moors, fishing villages. Caer Brannoch territory. Site of **Caer Drowned** — the sunken cliff-city formerly known as Caer Sulwyn.

**The Ashfields**
Eastern Mira, beyond the Spine's eastern slopes. Once productive rolling hills and light forest — farmland and wilderness that supported life. Dead now: grey-brown soil, ghost stumps of ancient trees, crumbled stone walls of long-abandoned farmsteads, dry creek beds. The land died over centuries as ash from Drûn-Khazad drifted west on prevailing winds across the Shroud Sea, growing denser year by year as Mordvar stirs. The dead zone creeps westward. Frontier territory: Vosskaran garrison towns at the western edge, goblin colonies in the deep Ashfields, and outlaws who fled justice in Vosskara and Eldermark and find the lawless wastes useful.

**The Sorrowmarsh**
A dead wetland in eastern Mira where the Grand Alliance unmade Mordvar two thousand years ago. The final blow was struck here. The mud remembers. Nothing grows. Ghost-lights at night. The mud smells of old blood in warm weather.

**The Weeping Wood**
A dead forest in northeastern Mira, north of the Ashfields, east of the Greatwood's southeastern fringe. Leafless year-round, permanent dark cloud cover overhead that does not move with regional weather. Naergrim-controlled territory: the Naergrim dark elves settled here two thousand years ago when they chose the Ash Throne, and their corruption magic killed the forest. The permanent cloud cover is a side effect of that corruption. Voices in the trees are Naergrim scouts watching the borders. Extremely dangerous — almost no one enters and returns unchanged. Home to Mor-Vethrin, the obsidian city of the Naergrim.

**Mor-Vethrin**
The obsidian city of the Naergrim dark elves, built into the dark cliff-faces of the Weeping Wood's eastern escarpment in northeastern Mira. Black stone, no windows facing west, entrance through a single gate guarded by a carved arch of bone. The city blends into the dark sedimentary and metamorphic cliff rock. The Naergrim have held this position for two thousand years, since they chose the Ash Throne and settled the dead forest around them.

### Thal — The Wild Continent

**The Ash-Steppe**
Western Thal — grassland transitioning to grey hardpan. Where the Gulmrak Horde ranges. Sparse water, brutal winters, occasional ruins of pre-First Age settlements that no scholar has been able to identify.

**The Vrothmor Peaks**
Central Thal — jagged volcanic mountains riddled with goblin warrens. The Vrothmor Peaks are older than any recorded history and contain tunnel systems that predate even the dwarven Underway.

**Drûn-Khazad — The Burning Wound**
The great volcano at Thal's heart. Called the "World's Mouth" by orcs, who consider it sacred ground. Active — permanent lava glow at night, ash drifting west on bad years. The caldera contains the **Ash Hearth**, where the Aescstól (Ash Throne) was hidden after Mordvar's defeat. The Ash Hearth is below the caldera.

**Keth-Vethrin**
A Naergrim outpost city on the western volcanic coast of Thal — smaller and more militarized than Mor-Vethrin. Built into dark cliff-faces above the rocky shoreline. Serves as the sea-watching watchtower of the Naergrim's Thal presence, and the point of contact between the Naergrim of Mira and Mordvar's Ash Throne. Not visited in Game One. Relevant to Game Two.

**The Shroud Sea**
The eastern ocean. Locked in permanent storm since the night Caer Sulwyn slid into the sea. No ship that sails east returns. The Sailor's Guild has charted the storm patterns for two hundred years and believes a navigable window opens within the decade.

### Key Islands and Archipelagos

**The Copper Isles**
Off Mira's southern coast. Nominally Solgrade territory. Major trading hub, politically unstable, home to the Sailor's Guild's main archive and the primary records of the Shroud Sea charts.

**The Pilgrim's Chain**
Three small islands off Mira's western coast, each containing one of the Eight Sacred Glades. The most protected pilgrimage route in the Aelorin Circuit. No hostile force has successfully landed on the Pilgrim's Chain in recorded history.

**The Sundered Isles**
A jagged archipelago in the middle-north of the Shroud Sea — what remains of the island civilization of Aescryd, the first human kingdom, after it drowned when the Sundered Crown shattered at the Sorrowmarsh. Half-submerged towers rise from monster-haunted channels. Pirates use the outer anchorages. The deep interior is held by the Ashen Hand. The **Ash Tower**, largest surviving structure on the isles, is the Ashlord's command base and the location of Hadran's Forge.

## The Three Ages

### The First Age — The Age of Dragons

Titan-beasts ruled sky and stone. Most records lost. The dwarves remember it through the **Dragon-Watchers**, who maintain a continuous oral and written record from this period. The Aelorin have Loresingers who can recite the First Age's end. Humans have only fragments and myths.

Key event: **The Dragonfall** — the death of the last titan-dragon, cause unknown across all three peoples' records, that ended the age and allowed the Elder Folk to emerge.

### The Second Age — The Age of the Elder Folk

Aelorin and dwarves built great civilizations whose ruins scar every horizon. The first human kingdom — the **Aescryd** — rose in the middle Second Age on a large island in what is now the Shroud Sea. They were engineers and record-keepers, builders of roads and trade routes, the civilization that made organised human life possible.

The most consequential of their kings was **Bealoric**, who ruled late in the age. He was not the founder. He was the king who broke everything.

Bealoric, consumed by grief and the fear of losing what he loved, performed a ritual of his own design that emptied him of his mortality — and of his self. He called the object that ritual produced the **Sundered Crown**: black-gold, seven-spiked, the crystallised material form of a hollowed human will. With it he lived past death and bent the world toward his grief. He took the name **Mordvar** — old Aescryd dialect for "the one who endures." He chose it himself. He was still making meaningful choices. He was not a puppet. He was a man who had surgically removed the parts of himself that might have stopped him.

The **Grand Alliance** of Aelorin, dwarves, and uncorrupted humans fought him across eastern Mira for years. The final battle was at the **Sorrowmarsh**. A Caelborn warrior of Aescryd blood — **Aelric Caelborn**, ancestor of Aldric Vane — wielded the **Aeluvain**, a sword the Aelorin had forged from the material of the fallen Eighth Star, and dealt the blow that unmade him. The Crown shattered. Its seven pieces scattered. Aelric died in the strike.

When the Crown shattered, Aescryd island drowned. The kingdom sank within a generation. The Sundered Isles are what remain.

Mordvar was not sealed. He was unmade — scattered. He has been reforming ever since.

### The Third Age — The Age of Men / The Long Twilight

Current era. Men are the fastest-breeding and most numerous people in the world. New cities rise faster than old ones fall. Trade routes span both continents. But the Aelorin dwindle through the Aelthiren, the dwarves grow inward and suspicious, and the Long Twilight is both the age of humanity's greatest expansion and its most existential threat.

The word *twilight* is used by Aelorin loremasters — not human scholars, who would name this a golden age. The Aelorin have seen ages end before. The world does not know that two thousand years is long enough.

## Religion

### Human Religion — The Ninefold Pantheon

Nine gods of the old world. Each kingdom emphasizes different aspects:

| God | Domain | Primary Kingdom |
|---|---|---|
| **Aldrath** | War and endurance | Eldermark |
| **Aeadis** | Harvest and sustenance | Eldermark |
| **Sorael** | Sea, journeys, honest reckoning | Caer Brannoch |
| **Lorthis** | Sky and sight | Solgrade |
| **Dunrath** | Beneath and secrets | Vosskara |
| **Aevelis** | Healing | All |
| **Lernath** | Learning | Solgrade |
| **Morith** | Death and transition | Vosskara |
| **The Hearth-Nameless** | Household, unnamed, always present | All |

The Ninefold are worshipped through shrines in homes, civic temples in cities, and the **Orders of the Shrine** — wandering priests who maintain the rural shrine circuit. No unified church exists. Religious authority is local.

### Aelorin Religion — The Eight Stars

Eight star-spirits sang the world into being and then withdrew. The Eighth Star fell when Bealoric hollowed himself — drawn down by the disappearance of the note of completion it sang. The Aelorin forged its fallen material into the **Aeluvain**: a sword, pale blue-white, cold to the touch, that hums at a frequency only Aelorin can hear. It is not magic in the conventional sense — it is a piece of the world's original song given edge and weight.

The **Eight Glades** are sacred sites — three on the Pilgrim's Chain islands, five in the Greatwood — where each star-spirit is said to have last walked. Pilgrimage between them is the central religious practice of the Aelorin. Aelorin priests are **loremasters**: they remember rather than petition.

The eighth glade exists and is tended in anticipation. It has no star. The Aelorin believe the sword's return will change this.

### Dwarven Religion — The Forge and the Lost

The **Forge-Father** made the world's bones. The **Mountain-Mother** fills the bones with ore and life. **Kradir the Unmoving** guards the dwarven dead and demands that all things be correctly remembered — he is the god of memory, patience, and ancestral record; the theological foundation of the Dragon-Watchers' devotion to accurate record-keeping, and the reason dwarven gold-hunger is considered a sin when it corrupts accounting rather than accumulates it. The **Lost Brother** — a fourth god, equal to the other three — was slain by dragons in the First Age, which is the religious foundation of the dwarven vendetta against all dragonkind.

The **Night of the Listening** is a yearly ceremony: all dwarves in every hold observe one hour of total silence to remember what has been heard in the deep and to acknowledge that some things should not be heard.

The **Dragon-Watchers** order is simultaneously a military intelligence organization and a religious institution — tracking dragons is both tactical and devotional. Their meticulous record-keeping is understood as an act of worship to Kradir, who demands the dead be remembered accurately and the world's changes witnessed truthfully.

## Magic

### Nature

Magic exists and everyone knows it. It is rare — perhaps one person in a thousand has any innate ability, and of those, fewer than one in ten has enough to be practically useful. Those born with it often have unstable early lives: things happen around them before they understand why.

Magic is old. In the First and Second Ages, more beings could wield it. Most of those beings and their knowledge have faded. What survives is fragmentary, jealously guarded, and expensive to use.

### Cost

Every significant magical act has a personal cost borne by the user:
- Migraines lasting days
- Temporary sensory loss (blindness, deafness, numbness)
- Weeks of physical exhaustion
- Permanent alteration at high levels — hair whitens, eyes change color, the aging process accelerates

The Ashen Hand accepts costs the Conclave of the Unseen Hand would refuse. This is part of why they are more immediately powerful and ultimately more fragile.

### Types

**Innate magic** — Born with it. The Conclave trains this. Cannot be taught from scratch; can only be developed in those who already have it.

**Item magic** — Objects enchanted in the Second Age that retain properties. Increasingly rare. The Smiths' Confederation maintains the most complete catalog of known items. The Forgemasters' Society can sometimes create new ones.

**Ritual magic** — Slow, requires preparation, draws on place-memory or collective will. The Ashen Hand's plague-blight spreading is ritual magic. The Sundered Crown's original forging was ritual magic at a scale not seen since.

**The Aeluvain** — Not magic in the conventional sense. It is a piece of the world's original song given edge and weight. It cannot be used as a tool; it can only be returned to where it belongs — wielded by the hand it recognises.

### The Underwriting

Beneath all magical working — beneath the cost, the skill, the item or the ritual — there is something older: a quality that runs through the world the way grain runs through wood. The Aelorin loremaster tradition has no single word for it. Some call it the grain itself. Others call it the Song's remainder.

What they agree on is this: some workings go with it; some workings go against it. The ones that go against it are not merely dangerous — they are corrosive. They do not only cost the user. They thin the world.

Bealoric's hollowing was the most extreme act of going-against in recorded history. The Ashen Hand's plague-blight follows the same direction. The Ashfields' deadness, the Sorrowmarsh's haunting, the Shroud Sea's permanent storm — these are not metaphors. They are the world's response to something in it that should not be.

The Aeluvain goes with it. That is what makes it the only thing that can unmake what he made.

### Institutions

**The Conclave of the Unseen Hand (Eldermark)** — The only formal human magic school. Sixty-three members. Trains innate users. Does not teach magic to those who lack it. Two current members are Ashfallen (compromised but functional). See GUILDS_AND_FACTIONS.md.

**Aelorin Loremaster Tradition** — Millennia of accumulated magical knowledge, conservatively applied. Not a school — an inheritance.

**Dwarven Runework** — No innate magical tradition, but deep knowledge of Second Age enchanted items and stonework. The Forgemasters' Society preserves most of this.
